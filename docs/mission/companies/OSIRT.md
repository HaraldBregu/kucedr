# OSIRT

Research note for the [product direction](../PRODUCT.md). Read 2026-11-25 from the sources at the end.
"Not stated" means the pages read did not say.

## At a glance

- **Company:** OSIRT Ltd, company 14488378 in England and Wales, registered office in Hatfield. A
  university spin-out founded by Dr Joseph Williams.
- **Origin:** OSIRT (Open Source Internet Research Tool) began as Williams's doctoral research,
  built with the UK College of Policing and used on its RITES training course, which trains over
  100 officers a year. Papers from 2016 to 2018 describe a free, open-source browser for police,
  also used in the USA, Canada, Australia and Israel.
- **Now:** a commercial product with a 30-day trial. Prices are not published.
- **Users:** investigators, analysts, journalists, safeguarding teams and OSINT professionals in
  law enforcement, fraud, government and legal work. The site shows Hertfordshire, Devon and
  Cornwall, and Dorset police and the NPCC under "working with", without saying how.

## Mission

> "At OSIRT Ltd., we believe in a safer digital world. Through our software and services, we
> support organisations in capturing, understanding, and presenting digital evidence with clarity
> and confidence."

## What they sell

- **OSIRT iii Core and Pro:** an investigation browser installed locally on Windows, macOS and
  Linux.
- **Features:** a Digital Casebook that records investigative steps, screen capture and recording,
  video download, notes, customisable reports and "secure hashing of digital items". Pro adds the
  Chronicle timeline and "Dark Web Access".
- **PULSE:** "an advanced text analysis platform", custom pricing.
- **Training:** optional, "at an agreed rate".
- The terms also mention a browser extension; the browser is not named.

## Prices

- Not published. Fees are in pounds sterling, usually annual with a one-year initial term, and
  "non-cancellable and non-refundable".
- 30-day free trial.
- Licence: a non-exclusive subscription for the client's own business; copying and reverse
  engineering are restricted. The free, open-source releases in the early papers are not what the
  site sells today.

## Evidence and AI

- The early version logged every page visited and put each item in a case container with its
  date, time and a cryptographic hash.
- AI features: not stated.
- Anonymity routing: not stated beyond Pro's "Dark Web Access".

## Compared with Kucedr's direction

- OSIRT is the closest in shape: a locally installed investigation browser with case files,
  capture, hashing and reports, rooted in police training. Its site shows no AI agent.
- It shows a path from a free research tool to a paid subscription, and that UK police forces
  will buy from a small vendor.
- Room for Kucedr: the same local model plus an agent and the user's choice of AI model. OSIRT's
  police roots make it the likelier pick for law enforcement, which the product direction leaves
  aside anyway.
