# What Kucedr could be: second search

A second, wider search for a commercial direction, run on 26 November 2026. The first search
produced the [investigation desk](PRODUCT.md) and narrowed it to
[briefs for due-diligence firms](RESEARCH.md). This one looks at twelve other directions and ranks
all of them together. Nothing here is decided or built.

Figures come from pages read on that date. "Secondary" marks a figure taken from press, an
aggregator or a search snippet rather than the company's or authority's own page. Article 13 of the
Italian AI law and the Simpliciter and Libra prices were read twice. What could not be read is
listed [at the end](#not-verified).

## Summary

- **No direction is empty.** Every one has a funded competitor, a free one, or both. The search
  removes nine directions and leaves three.
- **Privacy alone does not sell.** In no market did buyers pay more for local processing, and
  professionals name skills and reliability, not data location, as what holds them back. Kucedr's
  local, own-model design is a condition of entry in some markets, not the reason to buy.
- **New first candidate: a private AI desk for Italian professional studios** (lawyers,
  commercialisti, consulenti del lavoro). About 379,000 professionals, most in practices of one to
  five people, a legal duty in force since October 2025 to tell clients which AI systems are used,
  proven spending of €29 a month to €1,500 a year, and no Italian product found that works on
  client files on the studio's own machine.
- **Second: [briefs a reviewer can check](RESEARCH.md)** for due-diligence firms. Still the only
  direction where nobody sells the specific feature at a small-firm price, but it needs the most new
  building and has the least direct evidence of spending.
- **Third, as a means and not a mission: paid extras on the open general client.** It is the one
  category where solo developers show revenue, but Kucedr has 12 GitHub stars and 258 downloads, so
  the audience it would sell to does not exist yet.
- **Choose between the first two by talking to buyers, not by more desk research.** Ten studios and
  five firms, three weeks, before building anything specific to either
  ([next step](#next-step)).

## What every track found

1. **The model companies bundle the horizontal jobs.** Claude Cowork is included in the $20 plan
   and runs scheduled tasks with the app closed; Claude in Chrome became generally available on
   26 August 2026; ChatGPT Work replaced Atlas in July 2026; Chrome has its own auto browse.
   [Claude in Chrome](https://claude.com/blog/claude-in-chrome-generally-available) ·
   [Cowork scheduled tasks](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13854387) ·
   [ChatGPT Work (secondary)](https://ppc.land/openai-kills-atlas-browser-folds-it-into-new-chatgpt-work-agent)
2. **Local processing earns no premium.** Privacy-first products charge the same as cloud ones or
   less. Among Italian lawyers the barriers to AI are skills (47.1%), errors (42.8%) and distrust
   (23.4%); about 40% take no privacy measures at all when using AI, 52% in small firms. Both
   figures are secondary: Censis 2026 as reported by Guida al Diritto on 6 June 2026, and a Milan
   bar survey as reported by Fisco e Tasse on 22 May 2025.
3. **Small buyers pay for three things:** licensed data (Lexroom's legal sources), accountability
   for an outcome (grant consultants' success fees of 3% to 10%), and someone to set it up (the top
   blocker for Italian firms not using AI is skills, 58.6%).
   [ISTAT, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/imprese-e-ict-anno-2025) ·
   [Incentivimpresa fees](https://www.incentivimpresa.it/pricing-success-fee-consulenza-bandi/)
4. **Consumer AI subscriptions churn fast.** Across 3,519 AI apps, 6.1% of monthly subscribers
   remain after twelve months, against 9.5% for other apps.
   [RevenueCat, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-app-retention-study)
5. **Italian niches are already served cheaply.** Tender tools start at €23 a month and tax-portal
   downloaders at €149 a year. A local language is not a gap by itself.

## 1. Private AI desk for Italian professional studios

**Who.** Solo practitioners and studios of one to five people: 228,641 lawyers enrolled in Cassa
Forense in 2025, 66.2% of them in one-person firms (about 140,000 of the 211,464 active); 119,050
commercialisti; 26,102 consulenti del lavoro; 5,138 notaries. Together about 379,000.
[Cassa Forense figures (secondary)](https://ntplusdiritto.ilsole24ore.com/art/avvocati-numeri-ripresa-ma-futuro-incerto-pesa-ricambio-generazionale-AI1siInC) ·
[Notaries](https://www.giustizia.it/cmsresources/cms/documents/archivi_notarili_guida2025.pdf)

**The job.** Work on client files (contracts, filings, statements, correspondence) with AI while the
files stay in the studio, and be able to show the client and the professional body which AI was
used on what. Not legal research: that needs a licensed database.

**Why now.** Law 132/2025, article 13, in force since 10 October 2025, limits AI in the intellectual
professions to "attività strumentali e di supporto" and requires that information on the AI systems
used be given to the client "con linguaggio chiaro, semplice ed esaustivo". The article states no
sanction. The lawyers' council published a model notice; the accountants' council added a client
clause and six-monthly AI audits (secondary). The European bar council lists running AI locally
among the acceptable safeguards for client data, next to a zero-retention contract, and calls
on-premise "the most secure option" while noting smaller models are "significantly below" the
largest ones.
[Law 132/2025, art. 13](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:2025-09-23;132~art13) ·
[CNF model notice (secondary)](https://edotto.com/articolo/avvocati-il-modello-di-informativa-sulluso-ai-del-cnf)

**They already pay.** Lawyers using AI rose from 27.5% to 55.3% in a year. Lexroom reported more
than 7,000 firms in February 2026, about 6,500 of them with one to ten professionals, paying about
€1,500 a year on average with 93% retention; by May it had more than 8,000 firms and over
$73 million raised. Simpliciter charges €29 plus VAT a
month for one user and 200 interactions, €59 for three users and 600, and claims 45,000
professionals. Libra costs €900 to €2,400 a user a year.
[Lexroom (secondary)](https://thenextweb.com/news/lexroom-50-million-series-b-civil-law-legal-ai-europe) ·
[Simpliciter](https://www.simpliciter.ai/) · [Libra pricing](https://libratech.ai/pricing)

**The gap.** No Italian product was found that runs on the professional's machine with a local or
chosen model. The cloud products sell research over licensed sources. Kucedr would sit beside
them: the place where the client's own documents are read, compared and drafted from.

**What exists and what is missing.**

| Need | Today | Missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work on client files locally | Local index over folders, with consent before text leaves the machine; Word, Excel and PDF skills | A workspace per client ([planned](../WORKSPACES.md)) |
| Show which AI was used | A run record per chat; the index asks consent for each recipient of document text | A client notice and an AI-use register generated from those records |
| Keep files safe at rest | Sealed keys; vault encryption and app lock in progress | Finished and verified on a release |
| Start without API keys | Setup centred on providers and keys | A ready hosted model in the EU under a no-retention contract, and a local model path |
| Trust the installer | Unsigned builds | Signed and notarised builds |

**Price and margin.** €29 a month per seat, matching Simpliciter. Two hundred interactions of
30,000 input and 2,000 output tokens cost $16 (€14.63) at Sonnet 5.5 list prices and $0.80 (€0.73)
at Haiku 5.5 prices, so the allowance has to be capped or run on the smaller model. With €1.03 of
payment fees, a €5 model allowance and five to ten minutes of support a month, a seat leaves about
€13 to €18, and each €1,000 a month of fixed cost needs 56 to 78 studios. These are assumptions on
the same footing as [the unit economics](ECONOMICS.md).

**Why it could fail.**

- Confidentiality is not shown to be what makes a professional buy, and the rules allow cloud AI
  under a data-processing agreement. The notice and the register are the stronger hook, and that is
  untested.
- The fear of errors (42.8%) is answered by licensed sources Kucedr does not have.
- Reaching 140,000 solo lawyers takes trust and channels: professional bodies, training credits, the
  IT resellers who already serve studios.
- The lawyers' council has asked for a free AI system for all lawyers (secondary).
- Local models are weaker and need hardware; the hosted path must exist from the first day.

## 2. Briefs a reviewer can check

Unchanged from [the commercial research](RESEARCH.md) and [the competition review](COMPETITION.md):
boutique due-diligence firms, a seat near €49 a month, and a reviewer who can reopen the preserved
source behind each sentence. It stays second because the evidence that firms would buy software for
this is one field study and one audit report, and because DeepState is a few features away.
The capture record and case separation it needs also serve direction 1.

## 3. Paid extras on the open general client

TypingMind, built by one developer, shows about $137,000 a month from one-time licences and a team
plan; BoltAI $15,000 to $30,000 a month (both self-reported, secondary). Obsidian charges $4 to $5
a month for sync and $50 a user a year for an optional commercial licence; Msty $149 a year or $349
once.
[Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing) · [Msty pricing](https://msty.ai/pricing) ·
[TypingMind team pricing](https://custom.typingmind.com/pricing)

This is not a mission, for the reason the [product direction](PRODUCT.md#1-where-kucedr-stands)
gives: a general assistant serves no one in particular, and the free clients have hundreds of times
Kucedr's audience. But signed builds, checkout and encrypted backup are needed by every direction,
so build them once and offer them to everyone: a supporter or commercial licence and paid backup,
alongside whichever desk is chosen.

## Dropped, and why

- **Browser back-office agent.** Italian studios already buy scheduled tax-portal downloads for
  €149 to €349 a year (Febot, Cassetto Fiscale Click), the tax agency opened its own bulk services
  in July 2026, banks block agents that log in with customer credentials, and independent
  benchmarks put browser agents at 42% to 57% task success against vendor claims of 97%.
  [Febot](https://febotapp.it) · [Cassetto Fiscale Click](https://cassettofiscaleclick.it/prezzi) ·
  [Agenzia delle Entrate services](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/i-servizi-per-gli-intermediari-fiscali)
- **Tender desk.** At least five Italian tools monitor tenders and draft the technical offer, from
  €23 a month. About 95% of service and supply acquisitions are direct awards with nothing to
  draft, and Kucedr's schedules stop when the app closes.
- **Grants desk.** Applicants pay success fees for accountability; software sells at €35 to €130 a
  month against 2,374 measures to track.
- **Compliance monitoring.** The thinnest evidence of any direction; the value is editorial
  judgement.
- **Personal chief of staff.** Bundled into $20 plans that run with the app closed; needs mobile
  and deep trust.
- **Dictation and meeting notes.** Wispr Flow raised $361 million, Granola charges $14 a month, and
  Linux dictation is free. A gap exists for local meeting notes on Windows and Linux, but audio
  capture on three systems is heavy support for one person.
- **Private research desk.** NotebookLM comes with Google plans from $4.99; Zotero plugins are
  free.
- **Private workspace for small companies.** What companies pay for is single sign-on, roles,
  audit and installation, none of which exist in Kucedr; Open WebUI and Onyx are free. Worth
  revisiting through IT resellers once direction 1 has workspaces and encryption: on-premise AI
  software is reported to qualify for Italian tax incentives where cloud does not (secondary, not
  checked against the ministry's pages).
- **Agent platform.** No documented revenue from plugins or skills anywhere, 341 of 2,857 skills in
  one marketplace found malicious, and the MIT licence already allows free rebranding.

## Next step

Three weeks, before building anything specific to one direction.

1. **Ten studios.** Five lawyers and five commercialisti in practices of one to five. Ask what they
   paste into a chatbot today, whether clients have received the article 13 notice, what the
   six-monthly audit will ask of them, and what they pay for AI now. Show the client-file workflow
   on a sample folder. Offer €29 a month, first three months prepaid.
2. **Five due-diligence firms**, as [already planned](RESEARCH.md#paid-pilot-and-decision-criteria),
   with the pilot at €500.
3. **Decide.** Direction 1 if at least four of ten studios name the notice, the register or client
   files staying in the studio as a reason to buy, and two prepay. Direction 2 if two of five firms
   pay for the pilot. If both pass, direction 1 first: larger market, the founder's language, less
   to build. If neither passes, stop and reconsider before building further.
4. **Meanwhile build only what both need:** a workspace per client or case, encryption at rest and
   app lock, signed builds, checkout.
