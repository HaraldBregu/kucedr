# Frameworks: Leila Hormozi

Built from the 48 videos in sources.md (her channel, long interviews, and Acquisition.com vlogs and internal talks, 2021 to 2026). 25 frameworks, roughly most-used first. The Sources line counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). She says she gives her frameworks no fancy names, so most short names here are descriptive and marked *(unnamed)*; names in italics without that mark are her own words. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from her stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Values as the decision filter (her board of directors)
- **What it is:** The founder's values are the company's values, and every hire, fire, deal, and priority runs through them. Values are constraints on how you reach the vision, so they also say no to good opportunities that are not yours.
- **Parts:** Acquisition.com's three values: sincere candor, unimpeachable character, competitive greatness (the last from John Wooden). Keep it to three. Define each value as behaviors so people can be held to it. Repeat mission, vision, and values at annual, quarterly, and monthly meetings and call out people who lived a value. Tie shout-outs in daily huddles to a named value. Lead with values, not rules: rules make rule followers, values make people who can think.
- **When to use:** Any opportunity, deal, partner, or hire that is attractive on numbers. Any time a founder is tempted to keep a high performer who breaks the culture.
- **How to score:** 5 if the values are written as behaviors and the company has already turned down money or let a performer go over them; 3 if values are posted but never used to decide; 1 if the founder would keep someone who breaks them because of results.
- **Quote:** "it's not what do I want to do it's not what do I feel like doing but it's what decision what action is approved by your board of directors" (S014) "Values are basically constraints of how you get to the vision." (S008)
- **Sources:** 11 (S001, S002, S005, S008, S014, S016, S019, S020, S022, S034, S038)

## 2. Reinforcement over punishment (latency matters more than intensity)
- **What it is:** Both punishment and positive reinforcement change behavior. Punishment is faster but breeds secrecy about mistakes, resentment, and minimum effort; reinforcement is a J curve that starts slow and lasts. How fast you respond to a behavior matters more than how big the response is.
- **Parts:** Spot the behavior and recognize it right away (Slack DM, public kudos, a nod on a call). The further from the event, the more specific the praise must be. Recognition scrambler: pick one *when* (outcome, effort, milestone, value), one *who* (manager, company, peers), one *what* (formal or spontaneous), one *how* (written, verbal, reward). Watch for accidental punishment (a face, silence, a side comment about time off). A negative reaction is reserved for immoral, unethical, or illegal acts.
- **When to use:** A team that hides mistakes, a manager who yells, a culture that is falling apart, or a founder planning to fix performance with threats.
- **How to score:** 5 if managers recognize specific behavior within a day and punishment is kept for ethics; 3 if recognition is formal and rare, or lives only in the founder's head; 1 if fear is the main management tool. Her own blind spot: "i will recognize someone in my head and it's like i won't verbally do it right" (S042)
- **Quote:** "latency matters more than intensity" (S035) "the only time that I use negative reinforcement is when something is somebody does something immoral unethical or illegal" (S012)
- **Sources:** 11 (S001, S002, S005, S010, S011, S012, S028, S034, S035, S038, S042)

## 3. Accountability = expectation, measurement, feedback
- **What it is:** Accountability does not exist until a person knows the expectation, has a number to judge themselves against, and hears where they stand often enough to change. Telling people they are wrong is not accountability.
- **Parts:** Four levels of expectations: core values, brand promise, department KPIs, role KPIs, each flowing from the one above (speed is king became a 5 minute promise, a 2 minute internal target, and 80% of replies under 120 seconds). Tie each expectation to a measurement (CRM, leaderboard, 1 to 10 score). Build self-assessment: weekly end-of-week reports that she reads and answers line by line. Hold up the mirror and ask how they think they are doing. Mix about 20% tactical accountability (small, frequent) with 80% developmental (career goals, habits). Hold executives and older hires to the same standard as the front line.
- **When to use:** Someone is underperforming, a founder says people should just know what to do, or the team has grown past what the founder can see.
- **How to score:** 5 if every role has written results with a number and weekly feedback; 3 if expectations exist but feedback is quarterly ("once a quarter which is absolutely not enough" (S032)); 1 if people are judged on expectations nobody stated.
- **Quote:** "Accountability is really you set an expectation, you tie a measurement to that expectation or a metric and then you give someone feedback about their ability to hit or not hit that expectation." (S025)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S025, S030, S032, S034, S038, S045, S047)

## 4. Demonstrate, document, duplicate
- **What it is:** Her delegation method. People copy what they see before they do what they are told, so the founder shows the work first, the learner writes it down, then does it from their own document while being watched.
- **Parts:** Demonstrate (do it in front of them); document (the learner writes the SOP or script, because whoever documents learns the most); duplicate (they do it from their document until they hit the standard); then follow up and check quality. Skip steps for experienced hires. If a process takes more than 30 minutes to explain, it is not a process yet. Keep SOPs living in the task tool so people must act to confirm.
- **When to use:** Any time the founder wants to hand off something they do now, or hire a replacement for their own work.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has shown it, the new owner wrote the playbook, and someone checks quality after handoff; 3 if a document was handed over with no check; 1 if delegated and forgotten (her recruiting playbook was gone ten months later, S032).
- **Quote:** "you want to demonstrate, you want to document, you want to duplicate." (S025) "the person who documents the process learns the most so why would you keep that to yourself" (S002)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S011, S019, S021, S025, S028, S032, S043)

## 5. Before you fire: wrong seat, wrong season, wrong boss *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most problems with a supposedly bad employee are misalignment, and more coaching effort does not fix misalignment. Define the right person first (honest, hard working, fits the values, smart, kind, good teammate), then check whether a right person is just in the wrong place.
- **Parts:** Wrong seat: list strengths and weaknesses from skills, not experience, compare to the job description, look for another open role, reshape the job, add admin support, or split an overloaded role. Wrong season: of the person's life (offer a lighter role or help them find work) or of the business (tiny, 20 to 30 people, 50 to 100 people who need functions and defense; peacetime versus wartime). Wrong boss: add a dotted line to another leader. Her firing checklist (S045): four reasons (missed performance, tearing down culture, apathy, drama), each with a "did I do my part" check; manage up or out; know whether you fire too fast or too slow; interview the person's colleagues. Then judge the rate of improvement against what the business can bear.
- **When to use:** Before any firing decision, and whenever a plan assumes an existing person will grow into a bigger role.
- **How to score:** Re-seat if values are strong and the problem is seat, season, or boss; coach if improvement is fast enough for the business; exit if values fail, or if improvement stays slower than the business can carry after clear expectations. Do not change a job every time someone is below a 10 out of 10 (S015).
- **Quote:** "you're cutting grass with scissors by just trying to put more effort into this person when it's actually just a misalignment problem" (S015)
- **Sources:** 7 (S011, S015, S022, S031, S043, S044, S045)

## 6. Goal-anchored feedback (critique the do, not the who) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ask every hire for their ultimate goal on day one, then frame all feedback as the gap between how they acted and how someone in their goal role would act, plus what to do next time and the exact words to use.
- **Parts:** Anchor to their stated goal; name the gap, never the person (a critique names the gap, an insult attaches the person to something negative); future-facing, not past-dwelling; give a script, not a skill; be as clear as a weight scale, since vague critique is heard as praise; find the one piece of feedback that matters, not 18; normalize it with Feedback Fridays (one piece to every person each week).
- **When to use:** Any performance gap, any manager whose feedback produces no change, any leader who avoids it.
- **How to score:** 5 if the manager can name each report's goal and gives weekly, specific feedback tied to it; 3 if feedback is quarterly or general; 1 if it is about who they are rather than what they did, or absent.
- **Quote:** "you're not giving them feedback about themselves. You're giving them feedback of how to achieve their goal." (S005) "you need to focus on the do rather than the who" (S011)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S005, S011, S020, S028, S032)

## 7. A vision big enough to hold everyone's vision *(unnamed; DSS is her term)*
- **What it is:** People stay as long as their own vision fits inside the company's. A small vision loses big talent, and founders usually stall because they run out of vision, not because the market does.
- **Parts:** A desired superior state (DSS): the measurable and qualitative state of the company in 3 to 5 years, built to survive change. Values say how you get there, performance says how fast, vision says where; leaders who want a different destination have to go even if they perform. For each top person, show them where they fit (a 10 year projection and 3 year org chart for executives).
- **When to use:** Losing or recruiting senior people, setting the next stage, or a leadership team that disagrees on direction.
- **How to score:** 5 if the top three to five people can each name their next role inside the plan; 1 if the best person has nowhere to go (she lost a superstar this way, S024).
- **Changed over time:** In 2022 she wanted a goal "so big that I am forced to change" (S008). By 2026 she sets goals her mind can believe and calls them "something that I think is doable, though very hard" (S005). Newer view: big but evidence-based.
- **Quote:** "you have to have a vision big enough that other people's visions for their own lives can fit inside of it." (S003)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S005, S006, S008, S024, S026, S038)

## 8. Not-dumb decision framework, then pick your problems
- **What it is:** Long-term results come mostly from avoiding dumb decisions (her Buffett line), and one very bad decision (wrong partner, investor, buyer, niche) can cancel many good ones. Her 2021 process for avoiding them, plus her later framing that most choices are trade-offs of problems.
- **Parts:** Ask the right question (format: how might I [blank] so that I can [blank]; a right question has several answers). Separate problem from symptom (what is not happening that would make this go away, what is happening that should stop). Test assumptions (where are you using opinions as facts). Map each option: upside, downside, can I live with the worst case, how likely is it. Pick the most probable compounding upside with the lowest risk. Then ask which set of daily problems you would rather live with, and under what conditions the opportunity makes sense. Create more options before choosing (coach, move, new boss, mentor, not just keep or fire).
- **When to use:** Hiring or firing a leader, a new partner, selling, a new division.
- **How to score:** 5 if options were mapped with a worst case the founder can live with; 1 if the choice is made in a high-emotion state with one option. Her worked example kept, coached, or fired an underperforming director of marketing and chose to fire and run the department herself in the interim (S044).
- **Changed over time:** 2021 (S044) centers on avoiding wrong decisions. 2024 (S029, newer): "there are so many right answers with only one wrong answer" and the only wrong answer is doing nothing.
- **Quote:** "the most probable compounding positive outcome plus lowest risk" (S044) "what problems do I want to live with every day" (S029)
- **Sources:** 7 (S011, S014, S016, S018, S026, S029, S044)

## 9. Talent acquisition funnel (mirror the client funnel)
- **What it is:** Hiring is marketing and sales pointed at candidates. Many companies stall on talent acquisition, not client acquisition.
- **Parts:** Applicant generation: the job title is the headline (search the top three tasks, use the title the target already uses, no abbreviations or rockstar language); the job description opens with mission and values so the wrong people opt out, then the problem the role solves; use every channel for rare roles (network, outbound, paid ads, organic content, recruiters, referrals). Applicant nurture: speed (same or next day), volume (two or three finalists), personal messages for senior roles, every channel. Indirect nurture is what a candidate finds when they search you.
- **When to use:** A role that will not fill, a founder hiring from friends and network, or growth capped by sales or delivery capacity.
- **How to score:** 5 if the role has a researched title, three or more channels, same-day follow-up, and two or three finalists; 3 if filled from one board or the founder's network; 1 if the rule is "if you've got a pulse and you apply for the job you're in" (S024).
- **Quote:** "if you can market and find customers you can market and recruit Talent it is literally the same skill set" (S037)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S024, S033, S037, S038)

## 10. Multi-step interview with the CEO as gatekeeper *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Script the interview like a sales process, with an objective for each step, and keep the founder at the gate on culture until a proven C-suite can do it.
- **Parts:** 2021 version (S048): admin phone screen, then interview 1 on qualifications and values, interview 2 with peers on skill (real scenarios, portfolio, an assignment), interview 3 as the gatekeeper on culture and whether the role is truly in the candidate's interest. 2023 version (S037): screening, expectations and culture, skill test (give them last quarter's problem), alignment, CEO interview once the company passes 30 people. Score against a weighted must-have scorecard and add a second interview when you like someone. Do not oversell the job; overemphasize the hard parts.
- **When to use:** Any key hire, especially one the plan depends on.
- **How to score:** 5 if three or more steps, a real skill test, a weighted scorecard, and the founder or a proven executive in the final interview; 3 if one or two interviews and a good feeling; 1 if hired after one call.
- **Changed over time:** 2021 to 2023 she took first and last interviews herself (S039, S048, S003). In 2025 she asked Alex to help with select roles while recruiting heavily (S023), and in 2026 she leans on the scorecard after a likable mishire (S013).
- **Quote:** "if you can script and choreograph the sales process to acquire clients then you can script and choreograph the interview process to acquire Talent" (S037)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S013, S023, S037, S039, S048)

## 11. Operationalize values in interviews *(her phrase: operationalizing values)*
- **What it is:** Never ask a candidate whether they are honest. Get the candidate to show the value, and trust what they do in the process over what they say.
- **Parts:** Turn each value into a past-situation question (the last hard conversation and how it went, for sincere candor; why they come to work, for competitive greatness). Share a flaw of yours and see if they volunteer one. Read them the red flags your team raised and watch for defensiveness. Watch the pay negotiation for competitive greatness. Multiplier test: would I want ten more of this person, and what bad would happen if I had them. For results, ask for a time they produced the result you need (cut churn by how much, how).
- **When to use:** Values-heavy hires, leaders, and anyone who will represent the brand.
- **How to score:** 5 if every value has a behavioral test and interviewers compare reactions; 1 if values are checked by self-report.
- **Quote:** "it's not asking a question do you have this value it's getting someone to show you if they behave in a way that exemplifies that value" (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S012, S048)

## 12. Producer, manager, leader, owner *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Four levels of contribution, each with traits and blockers. A manager has two hands; a leader has ten.
- **Parts:** Producer: autonomous, driven, industrious; blocked by no awareness of deficits, inability to delay gratification, and weak people skills. Manager: reliable, confident not arrogant, organized, service not control; blocked by getting stuck in details, not solving their own problems, and treating the team as employees, not followers. Leader: humility, empathy, poise; blocked by not seeing the team as the product, short time horizon, and seeking approval. Owner: allocates money and human capital. Later versions (S019): manager runs tasks, director chooses direction, executive owns a P&L. Keep a technical track so strong individual contributors can earn more without managing; under-title so you can hire above someone if the business outgrows them.
- **When to use:** Deciding who carries a new initiative, a promotion, or a top rep moving into management.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person asked to carry it already works at the level it needs; 1 if the plan depends on a top producer suddenly managing (the reward becomes delayed and shared, so many hate it).
- **Quote:** "so when i think of a manager i think of someone with two hands when i think of a leader i think of someone with 10 hands" (S043)
- **Sources:** 6 (S019, S026, S032, S035, S036, S043)

## 13. Delegation filter: importance, reversibility, dollar per hour *(unnamed; uses the Eisenhower Matrix)*
- **What it is:** The founder keeps the big, urgent, hard-to-reverse calls and hands off the rest by what they are bad at, dislike, and what is cheap to hire.
- **Parts:** Eisenhower Matrix as a CEO filter (do, schedule, delegate, delete). Her own zone: investments, contracts, selling a company, new divisions, executives; she does not manage anything under 10,000, hire non-managers, or set priorities below her direct reports (S012). Delegation quadrant: rank tasks by bad at, dislike, good at, like, then lay a dollar-per-hour value over it. Value of an hour: annual revenue divided by hours, used to test every request. Keep important, irreversible, high-dollar work even if you dislike it. Keep tasks that carry your personal voice.
- **When to use:** An overloaded founder, a new project that would land on the founder, or a founder who cannot let go.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder's week is mostly irreversible, high-dollar calls and the rest has owners; 1 if the founder spends hours on work below their hourly value.
- **Quote:** "if there's something very important and is very hard to reverse and has a very high dollar amount tied to it even if I don't like doing it probably going to do it" (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S005, S009, S010, S012, S013)

## 14. Jockey over horse (and the founder self-rating gap)
- **What it is:** When judging a company or initiative, judge the person running it first. A business can change strategy, product, and team, but not the person running it.
- **Parts:** Each quarter have the leader rate themselves and rate them yourself; the founders who failed rated themselves higher than she did, the ones who succeeded rated themselves lower (S007). Look for strong beliefs loosely held, someone who has been humbled, self-control, and character. Wooden-based CEO self-rating (industriousness, enthusiasm, poise, competitive greatness) with the top gaps on a sticky note (S041). If no good jockey is available, do not run the race.
- **When to use:** Investing in or acquiring a business, picking the leader for a new division, or any plan that rests on one person.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person who will run it has done it before, rates themselves at or below the evidence, and changes course on data; 1 if the opportunity is great but the leader lacks self-control or has never been wrong.
- **Quote:** "bet on the jockey not the horse" (S012) "if I can't find a jockey that I like no jockey is better than the wrong jockey" (S026)
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S006, S007, S012, S026, S041)

## 15. Exit and layoff protocol *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How you let people go teaches the rest of the team who you are. Move fast, say less, be generous, and own it.
- **Parts:** Meet individually, never as a group, manager first. Severance larger than comfortable, lined-up interviews, pre-written recommendation letters, calls to contacts who could hire them. Remove access before the conversation ends. Say less and remove humiliation. Never retaliate or fight a former employee. Take the blame in front of the team if you caused it, and tell everyone the same story. Ask what your actions are telling the rest of the team.
- **When to use:** Any firing, layoff, or department closure.
- **How to score:** 5 if the exit is fast, private, generous, and explained honestly to the team the same day; 1 if it drags for weeks, is announced to a group, or turns into a fight.
- **Quote:** "be as honest as possible and just do overextend yourself for the people that you're letting go" (S030)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S011, S016, S030, S041, S043)

## 16. Subtract before you add (earn the right to do more)
- **What it is:** The best operators solve through subtraction. Do not add a product, format, department, or tool until what exists works, and when you add, trade something out.
- **Parts:** Start-over test: would we start this, hire this person, or go this way if starting from scratch. Ask whether the problem is even a problem, and what would need to be true for it to not exist. Cut side quests. Maximize the current business before the next one. Add a division only if it makes the others better (flywheel). Never kill a revenue stream without replacing it.
- **When to use:** A new product line, division, show, hire, or software project, especially when the existing core is not yet working.
- **How to score:** 5 if the core is working and something is traded out to make room; 1 if a new thing is stacked on a struggling core (her team spent 30 developer hours to save 3 hours a week, S017).
- **Quote:** "Solving through addition is easy. It feels good temporarily. It is never the right answer." (S017)
- **Sources:** 6 (S010, S016, S017, S026, S027, S036)

## 17. Constraint first, including your own *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Put money, effort, and founder time on the single biggest constraint, and fix the personal constraint that eats the founder's attention before the business one.
- **Parts:** Theory of constraints for spend: where does the next dollar get the highest return, and skip the last 5%. Return on effort. Solve the biggest problem before chasing the biggest opportunity. Each week she asks one question: what is the most important and urgent problem in the company. Attention is a jar of marbles; anything holding marbles gets handled. Level-one constraint list: lead generation, nurture, sales, delivery, retention, employees, team building.
- **When to use:** A founder with a new opportunity while a known problem is unsolved, or a team spread across many fixes.
- **How to score:** 5 if the decision attacks the current constraint; 1 if it is an opportunity taken while the biggest problem sits untouched.
- **Quote:** "you won't have the power or attention to solve the real constraint unless you solve your personal constraint" (S026)
- **Sources:** 6 (S012, S014, S018, S026, S030, S031)

## 18. Certainty and neutrality (the leader as the rock) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Teams mirror the emotional state of the person in charge, so regulating yourself is a leadership duty, not self-care.
- **Parts:** Emotional regulation: awareness, a pause between feeling and action, a neutral tone. Regulation is acknowledging and moving on; suppression is avoiding. Never vent down, only up, but tell the team what is on your mind so they can predict you. Certainty chain: certainty creates clarity, clarity speed, speed progress, progress momentum. Lend your certainty to the team in a crisis. Predictable meeting cadence (pulse, purpose, preparation, S040).
- **When to use:** A crisis, a big change, a reorg, or a founder whose mood swings set the company's tone.
- **How to score:** 5 if the leader stays calm and predictable and the team knows where it stands; 1 if the founder is the source of the chaos.
- **Quote:** "humans mirror the emotional state of authority figures and this alone can make or break a business." (S020) "leaders who lack certainty create followers who lack commitment" (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S014, S020, S025, S029, S040, S044)

## 19. Coach off the court (transfer judgment)
- **What it is:** The founder's job shifts from playing to coaching. When a leader brings her a hard people problem, she does not take it over; she helps them craft the conversation, lets them do it badly, and has them try again.
- **Parts:** Transfer judgment, not tasks. The coach preps, reflects, and coaches but never gets on the court when the game is on the line. Build the machine, do not be the machine. Prove something first, then set the team up to take it over. The founder moves from one-person army to manager, operator, architect, and finally manager of the systems that manage people.
- **When to use:** The founder is the bottleneck on judgment calls, or messy people problems keep landing on the founder.
- **How to score:** 5 if leaders own their people problems with the founder as coach; 1 if every hard call routes to the founder. Her trigger: messy problems grew from an hour a day to four (S013).
- **Quote:** "You need to transfer your judgment to them." (S013) "build the machine don't be the machine" (S036)
- **Sources:** 5 (S013, S019, S021, S036, S041)

## 20. Stage ladder (the ten levels of the scaling roadmap)
- **What it is:** Each stage has one constraint and one function to build to graduate. The question is never how to reach the next level but what function the next level needs.
- **Parts:** Level 0 improvise (learn, not earn), 1 monetize (close people yourself), 2 advertise (rule of 100), 3 stabilize (stop doing everything, document everything), 4 prioritize (5 to 9 people; one avatar, one offer, track numbers), 5 productize (10 to 19; fix LTV to CAC), 6 optimize (20 to 49; training, one source of data, a real P&L, an operator), 7 categorize (50 to 99; directors above managers, HR, budgets), 8 specialize (100 to 250; executives own P&Ls), 9 institution (250 to 500; brand as the moat). Systems built on memory break around 30 people; expect to replace systems about every 18 months. Her revenue version: to $1M sell and be consistent; around $3M you must duplicate yourself.
- **When to use:** Any growth plan, hire, or system change, to check it fits the current stage.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan builds the function the current level needs; 1 if it skips a level (a sales team hired before the founder has sold, S019).
- **Quote:** "the real question is not how do I get to the next level. It's what function do I need to build to get to the next level?" (S019)
- **Sources:** 5 (S005, S011, S015, S019, S042)

## 21. Growth plan: red, yellow, green, then a challenge
- **What it is:** Grow people through real challenges, not courses. Paint the future role, sort its skills by color, and throw the person into situations that force the red skills.
- **Parts:** Demonstrate before you duplicate; anchor to their goal; describe the future role (the five main things, what comes off their plate); mark each skill green (strong), yellow (improving), red (deficit); work two or three reds for three or four months through a real challenge. Stretch goal: a task above their job with about an 80% chance of success. Do half of the job above yours once you do 100% of your own. Frequency beats duration. Make your own challenges someone else's opportunity, framed as asking for their help.
- **When to use:** Preparing someone to carry a new initiative, a succession plan, or a leader who wants to step back.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person who must carry the plan has a named stretch assignment and frequent reps; 1 if their growth plan is a course.
- **Quote:** "start with the Reds and expose them as quickly as possible to situations where they will have to acquire that skill in order to succeed" (S028)
- **Sources:** 5 (S010, S024, S028, S038, S043)

## 22. Employee value proposition (lead with growth)
- **What it is:** A job is a product. Like a product leads with good, fast, or cheap, a job leads with money, growth, or status. Hers leads with growth, then status, with pay at the top end but not astronomical.
- **Parts:** Five parts (her *Company Talent Match*): compensation, benefits, career development, work environment (the vibe), culture. The more senior the role, the more base pay is traded for uncapped performance pay. Promote and raise on output, not tenure. Retention pillars: growth, measurable impact, fair pay, recognition, community. You compete against the employee's own alternative, not other employers.
- **When to use:** Recruiting against bigger payers, retaining top performers, or designing comp for a new leader.
- **How to score:** 5 if a candidate can say why to join over a bigger payer and sees a path; 1 if cash is the only lever.
- **Quote:** "there's tons of money there's tons of growth there's tons of status which one do you lead with" (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S003, S012, S024, S038)

## 23. Hard conversation formula (six Es, then four As)
- **What it is:** A script for the conversation most leaders avoid. She calls hard conversations her most valuable skill, learned, not natural.
- **Parts:** Before they speak: establish tone, eliminate assumptions (say whether their job is on the line), explain the goal, explain where the problem came from, emphasize the impact, estimate severity on 1 to 10. After they speak: ask for their view, advise what to do, agree on a cadence and plan (for example check-ins twice a week for six weeks), assure them where they stand. Watch for defense mode versus coachability mode. Prep: put time between the feeling and the talk, write it down, record it and review like game tape. Unspoken issues pile up as conversation debt.
- **When to use:** Any performance, behavior, or culture problem a leader has been sitting on.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person leaves knowing where they stand with a follow-up cadence; 1 if the founder has weeks of conversation debt.
- **Quote:** "the gap between where you are and where you want to be in your business is the amount of hard conversations you're willing to have" (S046)
- **Sources:** 4 (S020, S028, S045, S046)

## 24. Power of a decision (reach, repetition, duration, reversibility)
- **What it is:** A decision's weight comes from multipliers: how many people execute it without you, how many repeat the framework, how long it stays in force, and how hard it is to unwind. Big companies turn like the Titanic, small ones like a rowboat.
- **Parts:** Reach, repetition, duration, and a reversibility tax. Easy-to-reverse calls get a test, not a debate. Hard-to-reverse calls get the multipliers checked, because being wrong runs all of them backward. Batch decisions into a running list and decide in decision mode, not while executing.
- **When to use:** Reorgs, firing a leader, pricing or product changes that touch the whole team, or anything that sets precedent.
- **How to score:** 5 if a hard-to-reverse call has been checked for reach and precedent and the team will hear the reasoning; 1 if a precedent-setting call is made casually. Her example: firing two leaders who treated people badly and posting why, which other leaders then repeated (S016).
- **Quote:** "reach is how many people can execute this decision without you." (S016) "things that are easy to reverse rarely ever need much thought put into them." (S016)
- **Sources:** 3 (S012, S016, S022)

## 25. Disappear-for-a-month test (seven components of a sellable business)
- **What it is:** A business is valuable when it runs without the founder. Buyers buy a business, not a person; her team running Gym Launch raised its price.
- **Parts:** Seven components: growth potential (meat left on the bone), unique value proposition (about 20% different on price, promotion, or product), revenue diversification (one client at 35 to 40% is not diverse), cash flow, recurring revenue, financial performance (revenue trend and margins), strength of team. Team test: would the business decline, maintain, or grow if you vanished for a month. Install a CEO and COO before a sale.
- **When to use:** Exit planning, a founder stepping back, or judging whether the team can carry a new initiative without the founder.
- **How to score:** 5 if the business would grow while the founder is gone; 3 if it would hold; 1 if it would decline.
- **Quote:** "if I were to disappear for a month would my business decline maintain or grow" (S027)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S020, S027)
