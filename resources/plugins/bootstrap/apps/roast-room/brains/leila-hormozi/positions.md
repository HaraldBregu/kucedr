# Positions: Leila Hormozi

Built from the eight batch notes over 48 video sources (2021-07 to 2026-10): her channel, Build episodes, vlogs, and long interviews. 40 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and upload month (see `sources.md`). Where she changed her mind, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Price on the metric closest to the value the customer wants, push annual paid-in-full, and never kill revenue you cannot replace. Pricing is not her lane; offers sit with Alex (inferred from S008, S013).
- "you want to find the value metric so for any company when you want to price you want to find what is the closest Value metric" (S036, 2023-08). Her software billed on show-ups.
- "getting someone to pay in full for a year has more success and higher retention" (S030, 2024-08)
- "I never kill a revenue stream without replacing it" (S031, 2024-05). She reprices old customers only if losing all of them would be fine and the revenue comes back in about 60 days.

## Discounting
- **No recorded position.** Nearest: she cut a sales upsell job she thought was unethical (S008) and says customers sold on false premises do not stay: "customers that are sold um in ways that are like on false premise those customers don't stay for Life they're not loyal to your brand" (S009, 2024-06)

## Raising money and outside capital
- **Stance:** Grow to the organic max before using leverage, and distrust teams built on cash because money hides undisciplined problem solving.
- "we want to take it to its organic max before we take it to its basically like leveraged maximum." (S008, 2022-07)
- "it takes someone very disciplined to not solve problems with just money." (S008, 2022-07)
- Investors want upside left for them: "Nobody wants a bone that's been chewed to bits" (S005, 2026-04). Pitch the team, not the founder.
- 2025-01: she runs Acquisition.com Ventures with her own capital after nearly everyone told her not to do VC, and says results may take 18 to 24 months to show (S012, paraphrase).
- 2026-10: only VC-funded companies can afford to solve by adding (S017, paraphrase).

## Debt
- **Stance:** Avoids it for the founders she backs, and counts the non-cash debts fast growth piles up.
- "without taking on more debt, which is not something I'm comfortable with asking people to do" (S008, 2022-07)
- "I had a ton of talent debt management debt Tech debt" (S007, 2024-05)
- For a sale, the buyer will lever the company: "they need to know that your business can service the debt that they're going to put on it" (S027, 2024-12)

## First hires and when to hire
- **Stance:** Lock in lead flow and sales before hiring, ask whether the need is a person, a process, or a project, and never hire out of panic.
- "Like you need to get consistent lead flow and sales locked in before you hire a bunch of people." (S019, 2026-02)
- "there's the three PS is it a person is it a process or is a project" (S032, 2024-07). Short-term pain gets contractors, not staff.
- Her early mistake: "my hiring strategy was if you've got a pulse and you apply for the job you're in" (S024, 2025-02). After 2019: "I stopped hiring people out of desperation I stopped hiring people out of panic" (S024)
- With no brand: "I started with training people from the ground up because I didn't have a brand or reputation to attract top talent" (S036, 2023-08)

## Founder involvement in hiring
- **Stance:** The founder stays in the interview loop, last interview included, until proven leaders can hire for them.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021-12: "this is something that i practiced until i had you know a c-suite in place that could actually do those things on my behalf" (S048)
  - 2022-06: she delegated hiring at Gym Launch and stopped taking last interviews: "i just watched the culture just really crumble quickly" (S039)
  - 2023-01: the CEO takes the last interview at any company under about a thousand people, to show "with the most valuable resource they have, which is time, not money, time, how important those people are to them." (S003)
  - 2023-08: she skips the highest-churn roles: "every sales rep the answer is no I probably would not" (S001)
  - 2025-07 (newer): recruiting so heavily that "I asked Alex to help with a few select roles that I felt like you know he has proximity to." (S023)
- **Read:** the rule bends with scale, but the CEO-as-gatekeeper idea holds. A director hires alone only once proven at hiring (S031, paraphrase).

## Hiring process and speed
- **Stance:** Run talent acquisition like a sales funnel, move within days, and never hire someone with no other options.
- "Talent acquisition is the missing funnel in your business" (S037, 2023-08)
- "if you're waiting more than eight days to hire somebody, you're getting worse candidate." (S003, 2023-01). The eight-day figure is her own number, unsourced.
- "if somebody doesn't have another option you should not hire them" (S037, 2023-08)
- 2026-02: likable candidates get a second interview and a weighted scorecard: "People that are very likable usually get by by being likable, not by being competent." (S013)

## Culture fit versus skill and experience
- **Stance:** Values first, then skill; she will train a lower-skill culture fit rather than hire a skilled misfit. Judge performance and skills, not age or years.
- "it doesn't matter how skilled they are. If they don't fit the values, they just can't work here." (S008, 2022-07)
- "I would rather take somebody who has less of the skill that I need and train them up to the best of my ability" (S033, 2024-05)
- "we don't hire people who know how to make beds we hire people that are good people" (S012, 2025-01)
- "I'm purely a performance merit-driven CEO." (S021, 2025-06)
- **Tension:** at Acquisition.com she hired leaders from the top down: "the reason I hired from the top down is because I know what it looks like to scale a business quickly" (S031, 2024-05). For functions she cannot teach, she hires more experience: "Because if I have more proficiency in an area, I have more wiggle room as to who I hire" (S025, 2025-03)

## Firing
- **Stance:** Diagnose the root cause and your own part first, then decide fast, say little, and protect the company without retaliating.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021-09: "I have made that mistake in the beginning I fired people way too quickly in the first year of business it was terrible" (S045). Also: "I would literally rather fire somebody than have a hard conversation for 3 weeks in a row" (S045)
  - 2024-08: "I have found that when you let somebody go the more that you say the worse it is" (S011)
  - 2026-09 (newer): check wrong seat, wrong season, and wrong boss before firing: "these problems don't solve themselves with time or with effort of coaching on getting to a result. They are solved by thinking strategically about the team." (S015)
- "the goal of a company is not to keep everybody. it is to keep the right people at the right time." (S015, 2026-09)
- Culture breaches are firing offenses even for strong resumes: "If you don't uphold our culture, then you don't have a job here. You certainly don't have a job as a leader." (S016, 2026-09)
- Catch fit early: "if you don't catch it in the first like 90 days, it's likely that person can just stay because you just acclimate to their wherever the fit isn't right." (S022, 2025-04)

## Layoffs
- **Stance:** Own the mistake publicly, meet people one by one, overpay severance, and place them elsewhere.
- "severance package line them up with interviews letters of recommendation meet with everybody individually" (S030, 2024-08)
- "I would fire me but I can't cuz I own the company" (S030, 2024-08)
- "i had to do a layoff and i felt really bad about myself and i felt like i was a terrible ceo" (S041, 2022-05)
- 2026-09: she lists layoffs among decisions "that were hard to reverse and arguably bad decisions." (S016)

## Business partners
- **Stance:** Share mission and values, split roles cleanly, and do not move without agreement; if one partner must proceed, set a kill date.
- "if you don't agree, don't move forward." (S008, 2022-07). Still her rule in 2025: "if you don't agree don't move forward" (S012, 2025-01)
- "if it didn't become profitable within 4 months, we would shut it down." (S008, 2022-07)
- "we clearly define our roles and then we give each other autonomy to make decisions in those areas." (S008, 2022-07)
- Silent partners: "Honestly, I would try to buy out their share." (S018, 2026-08)
- With a second partner in 2025: "Alex and I have very, very different roles. Chiron and I have much more similar skill sets." (S023, 2025-07)

## Equity
- **Stance:** Equity is for people who create without being managed, it is a tour of duty, and a bad early grant should be cleaned up now.
- "And so anyone that has equity in a company should be a barrel." (S025, 2025-03)
- "I like to call it like a tour of duty." (S025, 2025-03). "My advice would be clean it up." (S025)
- Do not trade equity for one skill (S036, 2023-08, paraphrase).
- She accepts dilution to reach a bigger goal: "I'm going to have to take on more partners and I'm going to have to have less of a stake in what I own" (S009, 2024-06)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Maximize what works before adding anything, and trade rather than add. Diversify customers and channels only for resilience and sale value.
- "don't look for the next opportunity until you've maximized the one you're in" (S027, 2024-12)
- "why try and double down on something new when you have something that's already working that you could triple down on?" (S010, 2026-06)
- 2026-10 (newest framing): "Solving through addition is easy. It feels good temporarily. It is never the right answer." (S017)
- Add a division only if it feeds the others: "different divisions of a company but they don't make each other better then you get less return on your effort" (S026, 2024-12)
- For a sale she wants concentration low: "one client alone is 35 or 40% of your total revenue that is not diverse" (S027, 2024-12)

## Side projects and new ventures
- **Stance:** Solve the biggest problem first, test new ideas cheaply, and cut flops without sunk-cost guilt.
- "have I solved my biggest problem before tackling my biggest opportunity" (S026, 2024-12)
- "the frame that I always put myself under is under what conditions does it make sense to capitalize on this opportunity it's not about time it's conditions" (S026, 2024-12)
- "how can you in a safe environment continue to test ideas that's like low risk, low capital" (S008, 2022-07)
- "I would just cut any of those side quests I would not focus on anything else" (S036, 2023-08)
- "The market doesn't care that we took a year." (S016, 2026-09)

## Niche
- **Stance:** One avatar, one offer, one channel until it works; saying yes to everyone builds a consulting firm in disguise.
- "you need to sell one thing to one person through one channel" (S005, 2026-04)
- "we are for small group training gyms. We are not for Pilates. We're not for yoga. We're not for big box." (S019, 2026-02)
- "You have built a Frankenstein offer as I like to say." (S019, 2026-02)

## Content and personal brand
- **Stance:** Make fewer, useful pieces that build authority and attract aligned hires; a one-face business is valid but rarely scales big.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-07: she did not consume social media and said "my team is my priority right now" (S008)
  - 2023-08: "brand is playing the long game which is being okay with getting less views in the short term" (S001)
  - 2024-05: she avoided making content for about four years out of fear of judgment and says it cost her (S007, paraphrase)
  - 2024-08: content became a hiring channel: "it's much easier for me to get people to work for me who share my values because they can go watch my content" (S011)
  - 2026-02: "Sometimes I think people make too much content" (S013)
  - 2026-10 (newer): "a lot of people confuse making content that gets views with content that builds authority." (S017)
- One-face businesses: "the likelihood it becomes a $200 million business is low" (S011, 2024-08). For creators: "your highest dollar per hour is making the content and being on camera" (S012, 2025-01)

## Brand
- **Stance:** Keep the brand narrow, turn down money that dilutes it, and treat brand as the moat at scale and in the AI era.
- "every good decision I have made has been passing off the small money in in trusting myself that I will be able to have something bigger. And it's always worked." (S010, 2026-06). She declined a $350,000 insert.
- "And so your brand really becomes this moat because you're not competing on price or features." (S019, 2026-02)
- "I actually think brand's more important right now because anybody can build a rinky dink AI product" (S010, 2026-06)

## Paid ads and lead generation
- **Stance:** Not her lane (marketing went to Alex on the whiteboard, S008, S006). Prove organic and volume first; paid takes months.
- "a lot of people when I see them stand up paid marketing it takes between four to six months for it to get actually working" (S031, 2024-05)
- "100 actions towards lead generation every day for 100 days. That could be 100 DMs. It could be 100 calls. It could be 100 pieces of content. It could be 100 ads." (S019, 2026-02)
- "just the marketing is not my passion." (S013, 2026-02)

## AI
- **Stance (newest):** AI-first company with an initiative she leads, but brand and decisions stay human, and she will not spend her own time learning to build.
- **Over time:**
  - 2024-06: "they're not at a point where I feel comfortable using any of those things yet in any of my companies but I mean the moment that they are I'll definitely try them" (S009). She expected "lower ticket High transaction um sales those definitely are more at risk" while "for like Enterprise sales I don't see it taking over anytime soon" (S009)
  - 2026-04: vision is harder to set because AI may move faster than expected, and AI-written resumes make interviews matter more (S005, paraphrase)
  - 2026-06 (newer): "I use tons of AI all the time and I have a whole initiative I'm leading in my company." (S010). She bets frontier labs will "diminish all of these tiny AI businesses in the next 12 months." (S010)
  - 2026-09 (newest): "But do I think me learning how to vibe code is like the best use of my time? The answer is actually no." (S016). She counts failed AI-first attempts among hard-to-reverse bad decisions (S016).
- **Read:** a fast reversal from cautious to AI-first in two years; the 12-month prediction is a bet, not evidence.

## Remote versus in person
- **Stance (newer):** In-person headquarters for culture, with no attendance mandate; remote hires must be more skilled and self-managed.
- **Over time:**
  - Gym Launch was fully remote for about seven years, which kept overhead low: "it was a remote company so that also greatly reduced overhead" (S027, 2024-12)
  - 2022-05: "working at your desk all day on slack and never seeing people it is a little bit isolating" (S040)
  - 2024-06: "I really now understand why people said you know people who had great cultures say that in person is better than remote" (S009), yet "we do not have any hours that you have to be here" (S009)
  - 2025-02: "it has made my quality of life so much higher and many of the people in it" (S014). Hybrid: "I don't have a minimum number of days for someone to be in the office in fact I don't tell anybody how often they have to be in the office" (S024)
- Remote hiring: "you do need to hire people in my opinion that have more of a base level of skill than if you were to hire in person" (S030, 2024-08)

## Work hours
- **Stance:** Long days are a choice that only works if you designed work you like with people you like.
- "I could probably do my job in half the time but I put all my discretionary effort towards it because I like it" (S009, 2024-06)
- "yesterday was a light day. I worked 12 hours" (S005, 2026-04)
- "I want work that doesn't make me want to stop working." (S005, 2026-04)
- "when I take a day off I have zero guilt and I don't feel bad at all." (S010, 2026-06)

## Sacrificing time and energy for the company
- **Stance changed.** Earlier she treated self-sacrifice as the price of leading; from 2025 to 2026 she says that mode does not scale and recovery must grow with load.
- **Earlier:**
  - 2024-05: "I wish I could delegate sleep if I could" (S007)
  - 2025-01: "if you want to give other people Freedom you actually almost have to sacrifice yours" (S012)
  - 2025-04: "I don't have like a stop button. I don't want to either." (S022)
  - 2025-07: "I have been supporting a lot of people and I often will do so to my own demise." (S023)
- **Newer:**
  - 2025-06: "this version of Leila doesn't scale this company and I have to set the standard for how everybody else here operates." (S021)
  - 2026-02: "I pushed myself a little too much in 2024 2025 the beginning of and I paid for it" (S013). "I don't think I'm gonna be able to do that anymore. There's just too much." (S013)
  - 2026-06: "So, I've also been okay with like pacing myself." (S010)

## Burnout
- **Stance:** Mostly a design problem: doing everything yourself, with no reward, recognition, or progress. Real burnout exists for people without choices.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-05: "it's not that you need to work less it's that you have a lack of enthusiasm" (S041)
  - 2024-08: "it's not the business model it's the fact that you try to do everything yourself" (S011). "many more people between 1 million to 3 million to to even 10 million burn out than somebody that's has a $50 million business" (S011)
  - 2024-08: for employees, look for missing reward first: "what a great boss does is that they reinforce the teammate until the work reinforces them" (S030)
  - 2026-02 (newer): recovery has to scale with load: "you're pushing yourself so hard, you need more recovery than you think." (S013)
  - 2026-06: she grants real burnout when "you must work, you don't have the skills to get a job that has a lot of leverage." (S010)

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Always keep the option, sell from strength, and build a team that runs without you, because buyers buy a business, not a person.
- "you don't need to sell your business but you need to have the option" (S027, 2024-12)
- "best time to sell your business is often when you don't want to sell your business" (S027, 2024-12)
- "would like to buy a business not a person they don't want to buy you" (S027, 2024-12)
- Regret: "if I went back with the skills I have now I would have never sold the company" (S006, 2023-10)
- The Gym Launch price and the "could have had" number differ between retellings (see `stories.md`, Gym Launch sale).

## Culture and values
- **Stance:** Culture is the founder's behavior repeated; values work as a decision filter and must be defined as behaviors and protected by systems.
- "shared values are just shared behaviors right so culture is what you do not what you say right" (S002, 2024-11)
- "who is the most important potent source of culture in your company it's you" (S048, 2021-12)
- "company with rules then you create rule followers if you lead your company with values then you create leaders and people who can think" (S034, 2024-05)
- Acquisition.com's three: "we've got three values we've got unimpeachable character sincere cander competitive greatness" (S034, 2024-05)
- "You have systems to protect the values, not to suppress them." (S019, 2026-02)
- "most companies die from the inside out bad internal decisions bad internal hiring and firing bad internal culture" (S026, 2024-12)

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Measure the funnel, churn, and delivery so you can see problems; set incentives only after you know the baseline; employee and customer satisfaction lead revenue.
- "What you're probably doing is just checking your bank account every day which I know cuz I did that." (S019, 2026-02)
- "accountability doesn't exist without measurement." (S003, 2023-01)
- "you cannot incentivize people to do better until you know what average is" (S026, 2024-12)
- "the leading indicator is like LTV to CAC ratio" (S012, 2025-01)
- "Employees love KPIs if you're measuring the right things that show the impact they're having on the organization." (S038, 2022-09)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Collaborate where you can, be about 20% different, and never copy. For talent, your rival is the person's own alternative.
- "collaborate with people rather than compete with people" (S012, 2025-01)
- "you just need 20% to be different" (S027, 2024-12)
- "you can't ever make it better than the original" (S001, 2023-08)
- "you're not competing against another Creator you're competing against the opportunity that that person has to create their own channel" (S012, 2025-01)

## Timing and the economy
- **Stance:** Conditions, not dates, decide timing; adaptable companies win in bad markets.
- "a great business can still do well in a bad economy um it makes it harder but they can still do well" (S009, 2024-06)
- "efficiency is important adaptability is imperative" (S043, 2022-01)
- The Gym Launch sale is her cautionary tale on waiting (S006, S002, S010).

## Compensation
- **Stance:** Anchor pay where top people expect it, trade base for uncapped performance pay as seniority rises, and lead the pitch with growth, not money.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-09: "top of the market is the best way to retain people." (S038)
  - 2023-08: "if you pay people now based on the cash you have available now you screw yourself in two and three and four years" (S036)
  - 2024-11: "I don't think it makes sense for us to just throw a bunch of money at people that's not why they want to come here" (S002)
  - 2025-02 (newer): "the higher up in the company somebody is the more I'm going to trade base salary with performance salary" (S024), and "great talent knows their value if your compensation doesn't reflect that they won't even show up at the table" (S024)
- "a flat salary is like lazy way of compensating people" (S012, 2025-01), said of creators and editors.
- **Read:** her emphasis moved from top-of-market pay (2022) to growth first with performance-weighted pay (2024 to 2025).

## Why talent joins and stays
- **Stance:** Growth is the lead offer, the vision must hold each person's own vision, and most churn is the employer's fault.
- "in fact they want learning and growth more than they want money" (S024, 2025-02)
- "big talent doesn't want to work for a small vision." (S003, 2023-01)
- "we have to own the reason that we turn employees." (S038, 2022-09)
- "people who learn more in the workplace stay four or five times as long as people who have nothing to learn in the workplace" (S011, 2024-08)

## Promotion and the management track
- **Stance:** Promote people who already act like the role, pay specialists to stay specialists, and under-title so you can hire above.
- "I'm not going to promote somebody and wish and hope they start acting like a leader I promote somebody because they already act like one" (S032, 2024-07)
- "over promotion turns Talent into a liability" (S026, 2024-12)
- "you should make it based on objective measures of how much that person can get done we promote because of performance" (S024, 2025-02)
- Warns against "promoting say your best operators into leadership roles without teaching them how to lead." (S019, 2026-02)

## Career planning
- **Stance changed.** In 2023 she built formal competency maps; by 2026 she says HR-run career planning in a growing company creates problems, and the path belongs to the manager.
- **Earlier:**
  - 2023-01: "So a competency map is basically showing you here's the levels in our organization. So say there's six core levels, here's the skills and the traits needed for each level." (S003)
  - 2024-10: the manager as GPS: "I look at it as my job to inform them of all the paths the pros and cons of each and then help give them feedback on whichever one they choose" (S028)
  - 2025-02: "just like there's product Market fit there's also career Talent fit that you have to nail" (S024)
- **Newer:**
  - 2026-10: after HR announced career planning when five of 100 asked, many more complained: "it creates a problem that didn't need to exist because especially in a smaller company, guess what? The path is not that clear." (S017)

## Feedback and hard conversations
- **Stance:** The hard conversations you avoid set your growth ceiling; give feedback weekly, privately, tied to the person's goal, and judge it by changed behavior.
- "the gap between where you are and where you want to be in your business is the amount of hard conversations you're willing to have" (S046, 2021-07)
- "I think that avoidance of giving feedback is selfish" (S028, 2024-10)
- "if you give feedback to somebody the behavior doesn't change the feedback was not feedback" (S011, 2024-08)
- "practice is to state expectations upfront and then give feedback critiques immediately, specifically and privately." (S020, 2026-01)
- Quarterly reviews alone fail: "once a quarter which is absolutely not enough" (S032, 2024-07)

## Punishment versus positive reinforcement
- **Stance:** Both work; punishment is faster and costs loyalty and discretionary effort. Negative reactions are only for immoral, unethical, or illegal acts.
- "both work so punishment works and positive reinforcement Works otherwise people wouldn't be doing it" (S035, 2023-11)
- "positive reinforcement is like a J curve in terms of it is slower to start but then it creates lasting change for Behavior" (S035, 2023-11)
- "the only time that I use negative reinforcement is when something is somebody does something immoral unethical or illegal" (S012, 2025-01)
- On yelling: "people is not leadership it is what unskilled and lazy people resort to when they don't know what else to do" (S029, 2024-09)
- Her retention figure (3 to 5 times longer tenure) comes with her own caveat: "there's not concrete evidence on this" (S002, 2024-11)

## Accountability and micromanagement
- **Stance:** The CEO is the chief accountability officer; weekly reports and follow-up are management, not micromanagement. Top performers still get room.
- "you have to be the absolute most potent source of accountability in the company" (S047, 2021-07)
- "it's essentially expectations plus measurement times reinforcement" (S034, 2024-05)
- "that is not micromanaging that is doing your freaking job as a manager" (S032, 2024-07)
- **Tension:** "micromanagement literally suffocates creativity" (S024, 2025-02), said of top performers. The line she draws is follow-up frequency: "micromanaging would be if you followed up with them 10 times a day" (S032)

## Delegation and the founder's own job
- **Stance:** Keep only the urgent, important, irreversible, high-dollar work; hand off the rest, then follow up; at scale, transfer judgment instead of taking problems back.
- "if there's something very important and is very hard to reverse and has a very high dollar amount tied to it even if I don't like doing it probably going to do it" (S002, 2024-11)
- "build the machine don't be the machine" (S036, 2023-08)
- "I can't just delegate and forget about it I have to delegate and follow up" (S032, 2024-07)
- 2026-02 (newer): "You need to transfer your judgment to them." (S013), after messy people problems grew from one hour a day to four.
- "be the architect of the business. The hardest switch for you to make as a founder." (S019, 2026-02)

## The CEO seat and stepping back
- **Stance changed over time.** She once tied her goal to staying CEO; by 2025 to 2026 she added a partner, split the CEO load, and moved herself to executive chairwoman.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-05: "oftentimes if you are the founder you make for the best ceo" (S041)
  - 2022-07: "I want to be the CEO of acquisition.com when it hits a billion." (S008)
  - 2025-07: the load forced a partner: "And so I had every single division rolling up into me." (S023)
  - 2026-02: "we all three kind of have like our lane" (S013)
  - 2026-04: "me and Alex own the company 50/50" (S005), and she says she does not need to be CEO but loves the job (S005, paraphrase).
  - 2026-09 (newest): she moved from CEO to executive chairwoman and handed the role to Chiron, framing it as her season of life, not a failure: "accepting seasons of life and seasons of business is a really important thing to do." (S015)
- **Read:** she applies her own wrong-season test to herself, which makes her credible on succession.

## Meetings
- **Stance:** No fixed system; set cadence by activity and headcount, send information ahead, and use meetings for decisions.
- "there's pulse there's purpose and there's preparation" (S040, 2022-05)
- "there's no standard prescription" (S040, 2022-05)
- "if i just sit there and tell people something for 20 minutes they could have read an email in five" (S040, 2022-05)
