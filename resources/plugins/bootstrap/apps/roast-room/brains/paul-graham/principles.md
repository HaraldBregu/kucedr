# Principles: Paul Graham

26 decision rules. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Users are the only judge; you cannot game your way to success.** "Startups are as impersonal as physics. You have to make something people want, and you prosper only to the extent you do." (S092) "starting a startup is where gaming the system stops working." (S092) Sources: 5 (S008, S068, S092, S149, S178). Confidence: high.

2. **Pick growth rate as the number to optimize, and never fake it.** "If you're going to optimize a number, the one to choose is your growth rate." (S081) "Even if you were able to fool investors with such tricks, you'd ultimately be hurting yourself, because you're throwing off your own compass." (S100) Sources: 6 (S019, S021, S035, S054, S081, S100). Confidence: high.

3. **Growth rate matters more than absolute numbers early on.** "If your growth rate is good enough, the absolute numbers will take care of themselves." (S019) Sources: 4 (S009, S019, S054, S096). Confidence: high.

4. **Do the unscalable work yourself, including sales.** "And no, you can't avoid doing sales by hiring someone to do it for you. You have to do sales yourself initially." (S096) Sources: 4 (S009, S054, S076, S096). Confidence: high.

5. **Delight the first users far past what seems reasonable.** "If you have to work hard to delight users when you only have a handful of them, you'll keep doing it when you have a lot." (S096) Sources: 4 (S035, S056, S096, S142). Confidence: high.

6. **A narrow but genuine need beats a broad but hypothetical one.** "A need that's narrow but genuine is a better starting point than one that's broad but hypothetical." (S174) "The crucial feature of the initial market is that it exist." (S056) Sources: 5 (S010, S056, S096, S098, S174). Confidence: high.

7. **Look for problems, preferably your own; don't try to think up ideas.** "The way to get startup ideas is not to try to think of startup ideas. It's to look for problems, preferably problems you have yourself." (S098) Sources: 8 (S008, S012, S020, S021, S092, S098, S123, S196). Confidence: high.

8. **Good ideas usually look bad at first, so don't kill them for sounding lame.** "In the startup world, most good ideas seem bad initially. If they were obviously good, someone would already be doing them." (S115) Sources: 6 (S021, S083, S088, S101, S115, S149). Confidence: high.

9. **Launch fast; launching is when you start learning.** "Launching teaches you what you should have been building." (S142) "The risk of launching early is not as great as the risk of launching late" (S009) Sources: 7 (S009, S125, S142, S161, S178, S186, S236). Confidence: high.

10. **Release minimal, not buggy.** "By "release early" I don't mean you should release something full of bugs, but that you should release something minimal." (S186) Sources: 2 (S178, S186). Confidence: medium.

11. **Stay cheap; low burn is optionality.** "For a startup, cheapness is power. The lower your costs, the more options you have" (S194) Sources: 5 (S142, S178, S194, S207, S236). Confidence: high.

12. **Hire as slowly as you can; fix the product instead of adding people.** "Hiring too fast is by far the biggest killer of startups that raise money." (S080) "The only reason to hire someone is to do something you'd like to do but can't." (S208) Sources: 6 (S054, S080, S090, S093, S178, S208). Confidence: high.

13. **Know early whether you are default alive.** "it's probably not that dangerous to start worrying too early that you're default dead, whereas it's very dangerous to start worrying too late." (S080) Sources: 2 (S080, S090). Confidence: medium.

14. **Never treat the next round as more than plan A.** "no matter how good your growth is, you can never safely treat fundraising as more than a plan A." (S080) Sources: 4 (S080, S090, S093, S125). Confidence: high.

15. **The best way to raise is to not need it.** "if you want to raise money, the best thing you can do is get yourself to the point where you don't need to." (S093) "No one wants you if you seem desperate. And the best way not to seem desperate is not to be desperate." (S093) Sources: 4 (S093, S132, S142, S145). Confidence: high.

16. **Valuation is a distant third; don't optimize it.** "The most important thing to understand about valuation is that it's not that important." (S093) Sources: 4 (S010, S093, S171, S207). Confidence: high.

17. **Raising too much is dangerous too.** "The more you raise, the more you spend, and spending a lot of money can be disastrous for an early stage startup." (S093) Sources: 5 (S009, S093, S131, S178, S207). Confidence: high.

18. **Competitors rarely kill startups; self-inflicted wounds do.** "Competitors are rarely what kills startups. Poor execution does." (S056) "Way more startups hose themselves than get crushed by competitors." (S186) Sources: 6 (S009, S056, S125, S186, S197, S203). Confidence: high.

19. **Distraction is what a startup can least afford.** "The number one thing not to do is other things." (S166) "Nothing kills startups like distractions. The worst type are those that pay money: day jobs, consulting, profitable side-projects." (S142) Sources: 5 (S086, S122, S142, S159, S166). Confidence: high.

20. **Choose cofounders for character; never start alone.** "Starting a startup is too hard for one person." (S178) "In a startup you can change your idea easily, but changing your cofounders is hard." (S142) Sources: 7 (S009, S010, S125, S142, S174, S178, S196). Confidence: high.

21. **Determination beats intelligence, but it must flex.** "as long as you're over a certain threshold of intelligence, what matters most is determination." (S115) "You have to be determined, but flexible, like a running back." (S186) Sources: 6 (S009, S115, S129, S137, S174, S186). Confidence: high.

22. **Don't give up; startups mostly die of demoralization.** "You can get surprisingly far by just not giving up." (S142) Sources: 3 (S142, S149, S166). Confidence: high.

23. **Be benevolent; it is practical, not just moral.** "Startups usually win by making something so great that people recommend it to their friends." (S081) "Bad people make bad founders." (S056) Sources: 5 (S004, S056, S081, S091, S154). Confidence: high.

24. **Don't attack incumbents head-on; transcend them.** "Startups don't win by attacking. They win by transcending." (S091) "The way you make a new Google is not by attacking them head on." (S001) Sources: 4 (S001, S091, S106, S188). Confidence: high.

25. **Make the business model wait for the product, but make money.** "if users love you, you can always make money from that somehow, and if they don't, the cleverest business model in the world won't save you." (S174) "Your company has to make money." (S150) Sources: 6 (S004, S150, S154, S174, S178, S180). Confidence: high.

26. **Be in a startup hub if you can.** "all other things being equal, the more of a startup hub a place is, the better startups will do there." (S162) Sources: 4 (S003, S110, S162, S179). Confidence: high.
