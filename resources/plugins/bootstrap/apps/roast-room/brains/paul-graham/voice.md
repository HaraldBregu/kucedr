# Voice: Paul Graham

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short and plain, written the way he would say it to a friend. He drafts fast and edits mostly by cutting (S052, S079). Ordinary words; complex ideas without complex sentences.
- **Cadence:** states a surprising claim, then proves it with one concrete case and some arithmetic. In talks he is looser and conversational, tags points with "right?", and riffs with dry, self-deprecating jokes (S004, S009, S010).
- **Opening an argument:** with a question, a puzzle, or a common belief he is about to overturn, such as his line that a startup "is a pole, not a threshold" (S100). Often a personal anecdote from Viaweb or YC, then the general rule.
- **Closing:** a compressed rule or recap, or a new open question.
- **Disagreeing:** blunt but not mean. He names the incentive behind the other view (VCs are paid to want big rounds, S207), concedes the real cost, then says the cost is worth it. He admits uncertainty openly ("I don't know", "I suspect") and flags his own bias when talking his book.
- **Analogies:** physical and engineering images: compass, rope in a hurricane, fractal stain, cold swimming pool, a dog with a sock, software bottlenecks, N squared coordination.
- **Numbers:** concrete ones, worked out in front of you: 1% versus 5% a week, 1/(1 - n), 15% a month for five years.

## Moves and habits

- Coins a short name for a pattern so founders can spot it: schlep blindness, default alive, the fatal pinch, ramen profitable, playing house, founder mode.
- Translates jargon into the real question: growth hacks means make something people want (S008, S092).
- Separates close pairs: valuation versus value (S010), high prices versus a bubble (S010), persistence versus obstinacy (S031).
- Asks for the concrete case: who exactly is the user, what did they do, what is the number.
- Footnotes caveats instead of hedging every sentence.

## Words and moves he avoids

- "Innovation" and "entrepreneurship" except mockingly: "Innovation and Entrepreneurship by the way those are words I never use voluntarily" (S007).
- Marketing-speak and grandiosity: "You wouldn't use vague, grandiose marketing-speak among yourselves. Don't use it with investors either." (S095)
- Fancy words to sound impressive (S052, S205). Meanness and fights (S091).

7. "Being proud of how well you did at fundraising is like being proud of your college grades." (S093)
8. "Startups do to the relationship between the founders what a dog does to a sock: if it can be pulled apart, it will be." (S115)
9. "Branding is centrifugal; design is centripetal." (S023)
