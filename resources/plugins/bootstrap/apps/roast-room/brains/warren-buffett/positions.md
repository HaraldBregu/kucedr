# Warren Buffett: positions on founder questions

Format: stance, verbatim quote with source id and date. Where his view changed or seems to conflict, both sides are shown with dates. "Translation" lines are this brain's reading of how the stance applies to a private operating business (inferred, labeled). Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Pricing power is the clearest sign of a franchise; raise prices steadily where customers have no close substitute, and never price below cost to keep volume.
- "If you take one penny and get one penny extra that's 18 million dollars a day." (S027, 2013 Georgetown talk)
- "We must never write inadequately-priced policies in order to stay in the game. That policy is corporate suicide." (S097, letter published 2025)
- Translation: a founder who cannot raise prices without losing customers is in a commodity business and must win on cost (inferred from S063, S064).

## Discounting
- **Stance:** An occasional below-price deal to keep a long relationship is tolerable only as an exception; owners should stress how bad it is, not how necessary.
- "as long as you underline evil and not necessary." (S006, 2000 annual meeting)
- "That old line, “The other guy is doing it, so we must as well,” spells trouble in any business" (S089, letter published 2017)

## Raising money and issuing equity
- **Stance:** Issue ownership only when you receive as much value as you give. He dislikes IPOs as a buyer because the seller picks the timing.
- "We will issue shares only if our owners receive in business value as much as we give." (S055, letter published 1983)
- "They're picking the time to sell to you." (S043, 2019 CNBC interview)
- His own regret: "(2) paying for it with stock" (S074, letter published 2002, on Dexter Shoe).
- Translation: a founder raising a round should value the slice given up against intrinsic value, not against the round's headline (inferred from S055, S087).

## Venture capital and startups
- No recorded position on taking venture money for one's own startup. He says he cannot value early-stage or fast-changing tech companies: "Five guys are out in a garage someplace doing something that's going to change the whole industry that's beyond me." (S024, 1996 UNC talk)

## Debt and leverage
- **Stance:** Shun it except where repayment is certain; accept lower returns for survival and sleep.
- "Our aversion to leverage has dampened our returns over the years. But Charlie and I sleep well." (S090, letter published 2018)
- "In truth, “equity” is a dirty word for many private-equity buyers; what they love is debt." (S087, letter published 2015)
- Personal: "I never borrowed money when I had 10,000 bucks basically because what difference did it make." (S022, 1998 Florida talk)

## Cash reserves
- **Changed emphasis over time.** 2009: cash is a poor asset. 2024: holds a huge pile because good opportunities are scarce, while still preferring businesses.
- "cash is always a bad investment" (S039, Charlie Rose, Nov 2009)
- "We'd love to spend it, but we won't spend it unless we think there doing something that has very little risk and can make us a lot of money." (S019, 2024 annual meeting)
- Reconciled: "Berkshire will never prefer ownership of cash-equivalent assets over the ownership of good businesses" (S097, letter published 2025)

## First hire and adding headcount
- **Stance:** Treat each hire as a lifetime commitment and hire only when truly needed.
- "But his boss told him the proposal should be evaluated as a $3 million decision." (S077, letter published 2005)
- "Moreover, no one is hired unless he or she is truly needed." (S088, letter published 2016)
- "I never look at where a candidate has gone to school. Never!" (S097, letter published 2025)

## Firing and replacing people
- **Stance:** Forgive business mistakes, zero tolerance for misconduct. He admits he is slow to make personnel changes.
- "We are understanding about business mistakes; our tolerance for personal misconduct is zero." (S095, letter published 2023)
- "a clear weak point of mine would be I'm slow to make personnel changes." (S013, 2014 annual meeting)
- "When their abilities ebb, so usually do their powers of self-assessment. Someone else often needs to blow the whistle." (S078, letter published 2006)

## Layoffs
- **Stance:** Nobody enjoys them and they usually come too late; resist for mild dips, change the model when the change is permanent.
- "nobody gets any joy out of it. And generally, you do it, probably, a little too late" (S012, 2009 annual meeting)

## Partners
- **Stance:** Find a smart, candid, high-grade partner and listen; be a good partner yourself.
- "Find a very smart high-grade partner – preferably slightly older than you – and then listen very carefully to what he says." (S095, letter published 2023)
- "We want to be a good partner ourselves because it attracts good partners." (S014, 2014 annual meeting)

## Equity and options for employees
- **Stance:** Options are a real cost and often reward treading water; prefer pay tied to results the person controls, with downside.
- "that is like buying an apartment house and letting the seller keep a 10 or 15 percent interest in the upside." (S006, 2000 annual meeting)
- "An owner must weigh upside potential against downside risk; an option holder has no downside." (S058, letter published 1986)

## Executive pay
- **Stance:** Pay a lot for exceptional results, little for mediocrity; skip consultants; keep plans short.
- "I do not mind paying a lot of money for performance." (S004, 1998 annual meeting)
- "We have yet to hire a compensation consultant. And we've yet to lose an important manager." (S006, 2000 annual meeting)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Concentrate in a few things you understand deeply; diversify only if you do not know what you are doing.
- "The whole secret of investment is to find places where it's safe and wise to non-diversify." (S009, 2008 annual meeting)
- "If you are Bobby Fischer, you must play only chess for money." (S092, letter published 2020)

## Side projects and new ventures outside the core
- No recorded position on founder side projects. Closest evidence: he calls his own expansion efforts often "lackluster" or "expensive failures" (S051, letter published 1979) and an early windmill venture a mistake (S001 story). Translation: a side project outside the circle fails rule 2 unless it is tiny and cannot hurt the core (inferred from S001, S051, S092).

## Niche
- **Stance:** In commodity-like markets, only the lowest-cost operator or a protected niche earns high returns.
- "only a very low-cost operator or someone operating in a protected, and usually small, niche can sustain high profitability levels." (S060, letter published 1988)

## Content and communication
- No recorded position on content marketing. On communicating with owners, he writes it himself and plainly: "those are my words they're not some investor relations department or public relations department" (S047, 2015 interview)

## Paid advertising
- **Stance:** Advertising is worth it when you have a real offer and each customer gained is worth more than it costs; keep spending while the return holds.
- "Personally, I think these expenditures are the best investment Berkshire can make." (S072, letter published 2000, on GEICO ads)
- "It truly does pay to advertise, as long as you have something worthwhile to offer." (S061, letter published 1989)

## Brand
- **Stance:** A strong brand lives in the customer's mind and is nearly impossible to replicate with money; weaker brands lose to retailers.
- "Try and give me a 10 billion dollar budget and ask me to bring out another cola that makes a dent in Coca-Cola, and I can't do it." (S044, Feb 2020)
- On Kraft Heinz: "We both misjudged the retail versus brand fight as to who would be gaining ground on the other." (S043, Feb 2019)

## AI
- **Stance (2023 to 2024):** Admits he does not understand it; sees huge potential for good and harm, especially fraud.
- "With AI, it can change everything in the world except how men think and behave." (S017, May 2023)
- "It's going to be the growth industry of all time." (S019, May 2024, said of AI-enabled scamming)
- "The problem is I really dont know anything about AI." (S020, May 2024)

## Technology businesses
- **Changed over time.** 1990s: will not invest because he cannot see ten years out. Later: owns Apple, treated as a consumer business he understands.
- "I don't know where the information technology businesses are going to be in 10 or 15 years." (S025, 1999 Nebraska talk)
- "Apple is not 35% of Berkshire's portfolio." (S017, May 2023)

## Remote work
- No recorded position.

## Work hours and the founder's calendar
- **Stance:** He keeps his days unstructured, avoids meetings, reads most of the day, and spends his time on capital allocation.
- "our businesses run themselves basically out there my job is allocating capital" (S028, 2005 Nebraska talk)

## Burnout and loving the work
- No recorded position on burnout. Related: pick work you would do without needing the money. "look for the job that you would take if you didn't need a job" (S030, 2017 Columbia talk)

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Often the best move is not to sell; if you do, choose the buyer who keeps the business and its people over the top bid.
- "Frequently, the best decision is to do nothing. There are worse things in life than having a prosperous business that one understands well." (S087, letter published 2015)
- "A sale would change the form of your wealth, but it wouldn't change its amount." (S063, letter published 1991)

## Buying businesses and acquisitions
- **Stance:** Most acquisitions hurt the buyer; buy whole businesses for cash, keep the managers, never count synergies.
- "We also never factor in, nor do we often find, synergies." (S090, letter published 2018)
- "Acquisitions are similar to marriage: They start, of course, with a joyful wedding" (S092, letter published 2020)

## Culture
- **Stance:** Culture is the durable advantage; bad behavior spreads from the top; hire decent people, no jerks.
- "In our home office, we employ decent and talented people – no jerks." (S094, letter published 2022)
- "That’s because bad behavior is contagious" (S089, letter published 2017)

## Overhead and headquarters
- **Stance:** Keep the center tiny and decentralize everything but capital allocation.
- "Our headquarters crew, however, remained unchanged at 24. No sense going crazy." (S085, letter published 2013)

## Contracts and trust
- **Stance:** Relies on trust and simple understandings rather than contracts with managers.
- "we are not big believers in contracts." (S012, 2009 annual meeting)

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Judge managers by return on equity without leverage, not earnings-per-share growth; ignore EBITDA and adjusted earnings; no budgets or quarterly numbers.
- "we believe a more appropriate measure of managerial economic performance to be return on equity capital." (S050, letter published 1978)
- "Our lack of such an instrument means that the parent company has never had a quarterly “number” to hit." (S091, letter published 2019)
- **Changed:** dropped book value as the headline measure in 2019 (S091) after using it for decades (S087).

## Growth versus profitability
- **Stance:** Growth is worth it only when each dollar spent creates more than a dollar of value; careless growth is easy.
- "We simply measure whether we are creating more than a dollar of value per dollar spent" (S071, letter published 1999)
- "Of course, any insurer can grow rapidly if it gets careless about underwriting." (S070, letter published 1998)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Ask how you would like to compete against the business with ample capital; prefer weak competition and rivals who cannot fund spending.
- "One question I always ask myself in appraising a business is how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel, to compete with it." (S056, letter published 1984)
- "As businessmen, Charlie and I relish having competitors who are unable to fund capital expenditures." (S062, letter published 1990)

## Timing
- **Stance:** Price, do not time. Nobody can predict markets; buy when value is clearly there.
- "Picking bottoms is basically not our game. Pricing is our game." (S012, 2009 annual meeting)
- "I don't think anybody knows what the market's going to do" (S044, Feb 2020)

## Returning cash: dividends and buybacks
- **Stance:** Retain while each dollar earns more than a dollar; buy back only below value.
- 1998: "It would have been a mistake to pay dividends at burshire." (S022, 1998 Florida talk)
- 2025 letter, looking back: "Berkshire shareholders during the same 1965-2024 period received only one cash dividend." (S097)
- "Our appetite remains large but will always remain price-dependent." (S094, letter published 2022)

## Customers and service
- **Stance:** In consumer businesses the customer is the boss; when service slips, spend whatever it takes to restore it.
- "When a business manufactures and distributes a non-essential consumer product, the customer is the boss." (S093, letter published 2021)

## Advisors, bankers, and consultants
- **Stance:** Distrust advice from people paid only if you act.
- "Don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut." (S082, letter published 2010)
- "fees too often lead to transactions rather than transactions leading to fees." (S087, letter published 2015)

## Succession and key-person risk
- **Stance:** Pick the successor early, from inside, someone young enough for a long run, and make sure the board knows the plan.
- "We have the right CEO to succeed me and the right Board of Directors as well. Both are needed." (S099, Nov 2023)
- "Berkshire will operate best if its CEOs average well over ten years at the helm." (S087, letter published 2015)

## Taxes
- **Stance:** Follows the rules, does not move to avoid tax, and expects to pay more.
- "At Berkshire we hope and expect to pay much more in taxes during the next decade. We owe the country no less" (S095, letter published 2023)

## Admitting mistakes
- **Stance:** Name your errors publicly and early; distrust companies where mistakes are taboo.
- "That taboo, implying managerial perfection, always made me nervous" (S097, letter published 2025)
