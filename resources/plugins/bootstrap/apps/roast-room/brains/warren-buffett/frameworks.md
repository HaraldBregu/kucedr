# Warren Buffett: frameworks

Each entry: what it is, the parts, when to use it, how to score a founder's decision with it, one verbatim quote, and how many sources in the corpus use it (count of distinct sources whose notes mention it). Lines marked "inferred from" translate his investing rule into a private operating decision; they are this brain's reading, not his words.

## 1. Circle of competence
- **What:** Act only where you understand the business well enough to say where it will be in 10 to 20 years. Knowing exactly where the edge of the circle is matters more than how big it is.
- **Parts:** know what you understand; define the perimeter honestly (ask people who know you); stay inside; skip the rest without regret, even if others make money there.
- **When:** any new product line, market, acquisition, or investment, especially one everybody else is excited about.
- **Score:** Pass/fail before anything else. Can the founder explain the 10-year economics of the new thing as well as the core business? If not, it fails regardless of upside (inferred from S022, S024, S072).
- **Quote:** "The most important thing in terms of your circle of confidence is not how large the area of it is it's how well you've defined the perimeter." (S024)
- **Sources:** 24 (for example S014, S016, S022, S023, S024, S072).

## 2. Economic castle and moat
- **What:** Treat every business as a castle that capitalism's marauders will attack. The width and durability of the moat (low cost, brand, location, switching cost) is the main test of quality, and the manager's job is to widen it every year.
- **Parts:** size of the castle; width and durability of the moat; an honest, able "knight" in charge; is the moat widening or shrinking this year.
- **When:** judging whether high margins will last, and setting goals for managers.
- **Score:** Name the moat in one sentence. Ask whether a well-funded rival could take the customers in five years. Spending that widens the moat beats this year's profit (inferred from S005, S022, S078).
- **Quote:** "Every business that we look at we think of as an economic castle. And castles are subject to marauders." (S005)
- **Sources:** 25 (S005, S022, S024, S025, S068, S078, S080, S088).

## 3. Birds in the bush (intrinsic value)
- **What:** A business is worth the cash it will distribute between now and judgment day, discounted. Growth and value are not separate styles; growth is one input.
- **Parts:** how many birds are in the bush (cash); how sure you are; when they come out; the interest rate and the other bushes you could pick instead.
- **When:** valuing anything: an acquisition, a hire, a new product, a capital project.
- **Score:** Write the expected cash back, its timing, and your certainty. If the answer is not obvious without a spreadsheet, the deal is not obvious (inferred from S011, S005, S015).
- **Quote:** "all investing is, is laying out cash now to get more cash back at a later date. Now, the question is how much do you get back, how sure are you of getting it, when do you get it?" (S011)
- **Sources:** 52 (S005, S011, S014, S015, S023, S062, S073, S098).

## 4. Margin of safety
- **What:** Estimate value with reasonably conservative inputs, then buy only when the gap between value and price is wide enough to absorb your errors. Applied to capital structure, it means no plan that needs everything to go right.
- **Parts:** conservative estimate of each variable; a separate cushion at the end; pass if the gap is slight.
- **When:** any purchase, budget, or financing plan built on estimates.
- **Score:** If the decision only works in the base case, it fails. A plan should survive the bad case without new money (inferred from S007, S063, S065).
- **Quote:** "If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying." (S065)
- **Sources:** 10 (S007, S009, S063, S065, S070, S090).

## 5. Owner earnings
- **What:** The real earnings of a business: reported earnings plus depreciation and other non-cash charges, minus the average capital spending needed just to hold competitive position and unit volume. EBITDA is banned at Berkshire.
- **Parts:** (a) reported earnings; (b) plus non-cash charges; (c) minus maintenance capex.
- **When:** whenever someone quotes "cash flow", EBITDA, or adjusted earnings.
- **Score:** Recompute the founder's profit after the spending needed to stay in place (tools, rehiring, platform upkeep). If that number is small, the business is a treadmill (inferred from S059, S074, S096).
- **Quote:** "Most managers probably will acknowledge that they need to spend something more than (b) on their businesses over the longer term just to hold their ground in terms of both unit volume and competitive position." (S059)
- **Sources:** 17 (S059, S074, S075, S088, S091, S096).

## 6. Great, good, and gruesome businesses
- **What:** Rank a business by how much new capital each dollar of growth eats. Great: high and rising returns with little new capital (See's). Good: attractive returns, but you must keep putting money in. Gruesome: grows fast, needs heavy capital, earns little (airlines).
- **Parts:** return on tangible capital; incremental capital per dollar of new profit; what happens to the cash thrown off.
- **When:** deciding whether growth is worth funding.
- **Score:** Divide the growth plan's new profit by the new capital (cash, headcount, inventory, ad spend) it needs. Growth that needs more money than it returns is the gruesome kind (inferred from S080, S064, S004).
- **Quote:** "The worst sort of business is one that grows rapidly, requires significant capital to engender the growth, and then earns little or no money." (S080)
- **Sources:** 10 (S002, S004, S012, S064, S065, S080, S093).

## 7. Dollar-retained test and capital allocation order
- **What:** Keep earnings only if each retained dollar becomes worth more than a dollar. Order of uses: reinvest in the existing business, then acquire, then buy back stock below value, then pay out.
- **Parts:** reinvest where returns are high; acquire only if per-share value rises; repurchase only below intrinsic value; otherwise distribute; check the results after a few years.
- **When:** every time profit accumulates and someone proposes a use for it.
- **Score:** For each proposed use, estimate value created per dollar. Below $1, return the cash to owners or hold it (inferred from S010, S085, S004).
- **Quote:** "The test about whether to pay dividends is whether you can continue to create more than one dollar of value for every dollar you retain." (S010)
- **Sources:** 16 (S004, S005, S010, S013, S056, S057, S085, S098).

## 8. Cost of capital is the second-best idea
- **What:** The hurdle is not a formula; it is what your next best available use of the money would earn. The best idea has to beat it.
- **When:** whenever someone says a project "exceeds our cost of capital."
- **Score:** List the founder's top three uses of the same money (including doing nothing). The chosen one must clearly beat number two (inferred from S013).
- **Quote:** "is what could be produced by our second best idea. And then our best idea has to exceed that." (S013)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S013).

## 9. Franchise versus commodity
- **What:** A franchise sells something needed, seen as having no close substitute, and not price-regulated, so it can raise prices and survive mismanagement. A commodity business wins only as the low-cost operator or in a protected niche.
- **Parts:** needed or desired; no close substitute in the customer's mind; pricing power; over-capacity check.
- **When:** pricing decisions, entering a crowded market.
- **Score:** Could the founder raise prices 10% tomorrow without losing meaningful volume? If not, the business must win on cost and should not plan on margin expansion (inferred from S064, S063, S027).
- **Quote:** "In a business selling a commodity-type product, it's impossible to be a lot smarter than your dumbest competitor." (S063)
- **Sources:** 20 (S027, S055, S060, S063, S064, S077).

## 10. Mr. Market
- **What:** Ben Graham's moody partner who quotes a price every day. Use his prices when they are silly; never let them tell you what something is worth.
- **When:** when a valuation, an offer, or a market swing pushes a founder to act.
- **Score:** Did the business change, or only the price someone is offering? Act only on the first (inferred from S060, S031).
- **Quote:** "Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you." (S060)
- **Sources:** 6 (S009, S011, S019, S031, S035, S060).

## 11. Fat pitch and the 20-punch card
- **What:** Investing has no called strikes, so wait for the obvious opportunity you understand at a price you like. Imagine only 20 big decisions in a lifetime.
- **Parts:** ignore pitches you do not like; do the reading in advance; swing big when it is obvious.
- **When:** pressure to "do something", too many parallel bets.
- **Score:** Would this make the founder's list of 20 lifetime punches? If not, skip it (inferred from S023, S025, S070).
- **Quote:** "you have to wait until you see the fat pitch to swing at because investing is a no-call strike game" (S025)
- **Sources:** 30 (S019, S023, S025, S027, S033, S070, S090).

## 12. Filters and the five-minute no
- **What:** A short set of filters (understandable, good economics, able and honest people, sensible price) that rejects most proposals in minutes, with attention reserved for what is important and knowable.
- **When:** triaging deals, partnerships, and inbound ideas.
- **Score:** If the founder cannot decide in five minutes with the filters, more months of study rarely help (inferred from S010, S003).
- **Quote:** "And the truth is, if we can't make a decision in five minutes, we can't make it in five months." (S010)
- **Sources:** 15 (S003, S006, S008, S010, S058, S062).

## 13. People filter: love the business, integrity first
- **What:** Back managers who love the business more than the money, and hire for integrity, intelligence, and energy, in that order of importance.
- **Parts:** evidence of behavior over years; passion for the work; integrity as the gate.
- **When:** hiring a key executive, choosing a partner, buying a business run by its founder.
- **Score:** Would you buy 10% of this person's lifetime earnings? A brilliant hire with doubtful integrity is a no (inferred from S025, S010, S035).
- **Quote:** "i have to decide whether they love the money or they love the business" (S035)
- **Sources:** 24 (S003, S009, S010, S025, S035, S080).

## 14. Newspaper test
- **What:** Beyond legality, would you be comfortable if an unfriendly but intelligent reporter wrote up the action on tomorrow's front page for your family and neighbors to read? Losing money is forgivable; losing reputation is not.
- **When:** any gray area: pricing tricks, aggressive accounting, cutting corners for a deadline.
- **Score:** Close to the line counts as over the line (inferred from S028, S032, S047).
- **Quote:** "Lose money for the firm and I will be understanding. Lose a shred of reputation for the firm and I will be ruthless." (S032)
- **Sources:** 26 mention reputation; 7 name the test (S009, S028, S032, S035, S047, S083).

## 15. The institutional imperative
- **What:** The hidden force that makes decent, smart managers act irrationally: organizations resist change, projects swell to use available cash, staff produce studies to back whatever the boss craves, and peers are copied.
- **When:** before an expansion, an acquisition, a raise in burn, or a "competitors are doing it" move.
- **Score:** Ask whether the plan exists because the cash or the team exists. If yes, it fails (inferred from S062, S090).
- **Quote:** "Instead, rationality frequently wilts when the institutional imperative comes into play." (S062)
- **Sources:** 9 (S062, S063, S066, S072, S077).

## 16. Financial staying power (the CEO as Chief Risk Officer)
- **What:** Run the company so that no panic, credit freeze, or lender can ever force its hand: reliable earnings, a large cash floor, and no big near-term cash calls. The CEO owns risk personally.
- **Parts:** reliable earnings stream; massive liquid assets; no significant short-term maturities or collateral calls.
- **When:** setting cash reserves, debt, and customer or vendor terms.
- **Score:** Could the company survive 12 months of revenue falling by a third without new money? If not, cut risk before chasing return (inferred from S087, S095, S082).
- **Quote:** "Our CEO will always be the Chief Risk Officer – a task it is irresponsible to delegate." (S095)
- **Sources:** 19 (S010, S013, S082, S085, S087, S095).

## 17. Stock issuance test
- **What:** Issue shares only when you receive as much intrinsic value as you give. Selling part of the company cheap is the same as selling all of it cheap.
- **When:** fundraising, paying for an acquisition or a hire with equity.
- **Score:** Value what the new money or asset is worth to the business against the slice of the whole company handed over (inferred from S055, S082, S087).
- **Quote:** "You can’t get rich trading a hundred-dollar bill for eight tens." (S087)
- **Sources:** 24 (S049, S055, S062, S064, S070, S074, S082, S087).

## 18. Buyback test
- **What:** Repurchase only below a conservative estimate of intrinsic value, so remaining owners are worth more right after. Never to prop the price or offset options.
- **When:** buying out a partner or co-founder, repurchasing employee equity.
- **Score:** Is the price below what the stake is really worth, with the seller fully informed? (inferred from S015, S089, S091).
- **Quote:** "What is sensible at a discount to business-value becomes stupid if done at a premium." (S096)
- **Sources:** 41 (S011, S013, S015, S089, S091, S093, S096).

## 19. Incentive pay that fits the business
- **What:** Pay each manager on the one or two variables he controls, charge for the capital he uses, keep it to a paragraph, no consultants, no lottery tickets. Owners have downside; option holders do not.
- **When:** designing equity, bonuses, and commission plans.
- **Score:** Does the plan pay for results the person controls, charge for capital, and cost them something when things go wrong? (inferred from S007, S058, S070).
- **Quote:** "An owner must weigh upside potential against downside risk; an option holder has no downside." (S058)
- **Sources:** 32 (S007, S008, S012, S058, S064, S070, S097).

## 20. Inner scorecard
- **What:** Judge choices by your own standards, not by how they will look to others. Chasing the outer scorecard tends to feel hollow.
- **When:** decisions driven by status, optics, a big-name investor, or a valuation headline.
- **Score:** Would the founder make this call if nobody ever found out? (inferred from S028, S035).
- **Quote:** "I think the people that strive too much for the outer scorecard sometimes find that it's a little hollow when they get all through." (S028)
- **Sources:** 7 (S009, S028, S035).

## 21. Toads and princes (the control premium)
- **What:** Acquirers pay double for a mediocre business, sure their management "kiss" will transform it. Shareholders could have bought the same toad at the toad price.
- **When:** acquisitions, acqui-hires, buying a struggling competitor.
- **Score:** What specifically will you do that the current owner cannot, and is it worth the premium? Synergy claims count for nothing (inferred from S054, S090).
- **Quote:** "investors can always buy toads at the going price for toads." (S054)
- **Sources:** 6 (S053, S054, S055, S067, S068, S090).

## 22. Noah's Law
- **What:** Seeing a risk is worth nothing until you act on it. For low-probability, irreversible disasters, start preparing now.
- **When:** key-person risk, single-customer or single-platform dependence, uninsured catastrophe.
- **Score:** Name the risk that would end the company and the ark being built today (inferred from S074, S088).
- **Quote:** "Call this Noah’s Law: If an ark may be essential for survival, begin building it today, no matter how cloudless the skies appear." (S088)
- **Sources:** 3 (S054, S074, S088).

## 23. Important and knowable
- **What:** Spend thought only on things that are both important and knowable. Macro forecasts, rates, and markets are important but unknowable; skip them.
- **When:** planning sessions that drift into predicting the economy.
- **Score:** Strike every assumption in the plan that is unknowable, and see whether the decision still stands (inferred from S003, S005, S022).
- **Quote:** "Figure out what's important and knowable if it's unimportant or unknowable you you forget about it." (S022)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S005, S022, S066).

## 24. The ABCs of business decay
- **What:** Arrogance, bureaucracy, and complacency kill even dominant companies; fighting them is the CEO's job.
- **When:** a company that is winning, growing headcount, and adding layers.
- **Score:** Which of the three is creeping in, and what is the counter-move? (inferred from S087, S014).
- **Quote:** "the ability to fight off the ABCs of business decay, which are arrogance, bureaucracy and complacency." (S087)
- **Sources:** 4 (S014, S082, S083, S087).

## 25. Mistakes of omission and stop digging
- **What:** The biggest mistakes are the things you understood and did not do; they never show on the books. The cardinal sin is delaying the correction of a known mistake.
- **When:** reviewing past decisions; deciding whether to keep funding something that is not working.
- **Score:** Set the checkpoint criteria in advance. At the checkpoint, either act on the obvious or stop (inferred from S028, S031, S097).
- **Quote:** "The most important thing to do when you find yourself in a hole is to stop digging." (S063)
- **Sources:** 15 (S007, S022, S028, S031, S063, S097).
