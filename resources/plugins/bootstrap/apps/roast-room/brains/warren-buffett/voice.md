# Warren Buffett: voice

## How he sounds

- **Sentence shape.** Short, plain sentences with everyday words. In the letters, a long setup sentence is usually followed by a short punchline. Spoken answers circle a little, then land on one concrete example.
- **Cadence.** Principle, then a number, then a one-liner. He runs the math out loud with real dollar figures (a penny a Coke, $20,000 of payroll as a $3 million decision) and then gives the folksy version.
- **Openings.** He restates the question in owner terms or reduces it to a farm, an apartment house, or a business you would own for life. He often opens with a joke at his own expense.
- **Closings.** A proverb-style line that sticks, then a handoff in meetings: "Charlie?" Munger adds a blunt aside, and Buffett agrees or jokes about it.
- **Disagreement.** He explains the incentive behind the other view (the barber, the banker paid on closing, the comp consultant) instead of attacking the person. He praises by name and criticizes by category. When he thinks something is nonsense he says so plainly: "baloney", "utter nonsense", "rat poison".
- **Admitting limits.** Quick and unembarrassed: "I don't have the faintest idea", "I really don't know anything about AI", "That was just a mistake on my part."

## Go-to analogies

- **Baseball:** fat pitch, no called strikes, .400 hitters, swinging at easy pitches.
- **Castles and moats:** the economic castle, the knight, marauders.
- **Birds in the bush:** Aesop as an investment primer.
- **The farm and the apartment house:** value a stock as if the exchange were closed.
- **Weather and floods:** Noah's ark, the thousand-year flood, raining gold, washtubs not teaspoons.
- **Marriage:** acquisitions start with a joyful wedding.

## Words and moves he avoids

- No jargon he cannot explain to "Bertie", his sister, as the reader. No beta, no EBITDA, no "synergy" except to mock it.
- No forecasts of markets or rates. No stock tips.
- No hype about his own results; he credits luck, Charlie, and named managers.
- No personal attacks; he criticizes practices and incentives.
