# Dan Martell: positions on founder questions

Each topic: stance, verbatim quote, source id, date. Where his view shifts, both sides are shown with dates. Web posts (S110 to S128) are undated in the corpus and marked "post"; most are 2025 to 2026 rewrites of his videos.

## People and hiring

### First hire
- An executive assistant who owns 100% of inbox and calendar, before any specialist, operator, or salesperson. Asked what he would do starting today, he said he would hire one on "day one I'd borrow the money" [S014, 2024-01].
- Consistent across 2015 to 2026 (S020, S031, S038, S049, S054, S076, S097, S123).

### Hiring a COO, operator, or VP early
- Against it until the lower rungs are filled. "You're going to pay somebody six figures plus to essentially have a nonrevenue generating role on your team." [S037, 2025-04]
- Exception: when starting something new with resources, hire the person to run it first. "if you're starting something new you should be starting top down" [S108, 2024-08] And past your own capacity, hire CEOs through a recruiter (S109, 2025-08).

### Hiring speed (changed over time)
- **2019:** "I have a belief that it's hire slow fire fast." [S083, 2019-02]
- **2026:** "You need to hire fast, you need to test fast, and you need to fire fast." [S040, 2026-03]
- Constant in both: the process has filters (video, assessment, test project); what speeds up is the decision. Cheap, rushed hires are still a time assassin (S059, 2022-11).

### Hiring process
- Paid ads, 60-second video, profile and cognitive test, same paid test project, short final, team veto. "When it comes to team members, I can't work with them until I work with them." [S037, 2025-04]

### Sales hires
- Founder keeps selling until it eats about half the calendar; sales is rung four. "Most Founders should keep those conversation." [S020, 2023-01]
- Hiring a rep before lead flow and scripts exist fails within about six months (S031, 2024-01). When it is time, hire a real closer and pay well: "You should want your sales people to make a million dollars." [S028, 2023-08]

### Offshore and part-time help
- For admin and early development, yes. "I'm talking 160 hours a month. They're available for $1,200 a month, $1,500 a month." [S066, 2022-05] Virtual assistants at $4 to $6 an hour (S015, 2023-04).

### Firing
- Decide, then do it fast, face to face, short, with access removed in the meeting. "At the end of the day it's not a discussion." [S083, 2019-02]
- Zero tolerance for people who cause anxiety in others, even if productive (S085, 2019-02).

### Compensation and titles
- Real leaders cost six figures; never pay with an inflated title. "Somebody that can actually lead and run a team is six figures" [S053, 2023-07]
- Tie raises to quarterly demonstrated skills, through a learning and development plan (S053, 2023-07).
- Rich early commissions get re-cut once margins are known; warn people up front (S063, 2022-07).

### Promotions into management
- Ask first; some people want to stay individual contributors. "You gotta ask though 'cause some people actually like to be sole contributors and not lead teams." [S075, 2020-11]

### House manager and personal staff
- Among the best uses of money once the business has leverage. "I would sell every one of my super cars before I ever got rid of Betty." [S011, 2025-11]

## Managing and delegating

### Span of control
- "My rule now is 7s the max. I had at one point a dozen" [S088, 2018-08] Five is the target, seven the ceiling (S052, 2023-08).

### Micromanagement
- It feels safe and defeats delegation. "They micromanage because it feels control" [S059, 2022-11]

### Meetings
- Few, standard, purposeful. "See, a business is the byproducts of its meetings." [S036, 2025-11] No meetings before 10 or 11 a.m. (S041, 2026-02; S110, post).

### AI in delegation and SOPs (changed over time)
- **2025:** Do not let ChatGPT write the SOP from your video. "But then you missed the feedback loop of the person processing the video to see if they got it." [S036, 2025-11]
- **2026:** Recordings go to AI to draft the SOP (S017, 2026-09; S121, post). Inferred from both: AI drafting is fine, but the hire still has to prove understanding.

## Founder time and life

### Work hours and hustle
- Against 100-hour weeks; he burned out doing them. "It also cost me my relationship. It cost me my health." [S013, 2025-07]
- **Tension:** "You can't be a world-class friend, a world-class husband, a world-class entrepreneur, a world-class dad, a world-class everything" [S029, 2025-06] versus "I don't think there's a work-life balance. It's work-life integration." [S039, 2026-04]

### Four-hour workweek
- Rejects it. "I am not a 4-hour work week guy." [S017, 2026-09]

### Burnout
- The founder falling out of love is what kills companies; build a daily release valve such as exercise (S070, 2021-12). What kills companies, he says, is when "the founder falls out of love the founder gets frustrated the founder gets bored" [S076, 2020-04]

### Remote work
- No recorded position on remote versus office. He says he has "literally scaled remote teams from zero to dozens of employees" [S091, 2017-06] and treats work-from-home as a perk, not culture (S115, post).

## Money and capital

### Raising venture capital
- Not before a product with traction. Raising first can "build a product for a market that doesn't exist or a customer that doesn't have the need" [S099, 2016-07]
- Look at non-dilutive options first; early equity can cost 30 to 40% annualized (S100, 2020-05). "Until the money is in your bank account, there's no funding deal that is closed." [S100, 2020-05]

### Debt
- Borrow for earning power, never lifestyle. "Borrow to invest in yourself don't borrow to invest in your lifestyle that's where you're going to get yourself in trouble." [S005, 2024-05]

### Capital constraints
- "You should not be capital constraint. You have to fix that." [S017, 2026-09] Prefers customer-financed growth: "I want to finance my growth through my customer financing" [S030, 2024-12]

### Equity for employees, partners, and co-founders
- Equity is the scarcest asset; always vest, including co-founders. "Equity is the most valuable thing you have in your software business" [S048, 2024-07] A co-founder is one of the most expensive "equity dilution decisions you're gonna make" [S098, 2020-12].
- Pay promoters affiliate fees, not equity (S048); offer equity only per result delivered (S063, 2022-07).

### Partners and commissions
- Partners with non-competing audiences are the strongest customer source. "That's what a partner does and it is the most powerful way to get customers." [S036, 2025-11] Fair-feeling commission is about 30%, but the market decides (S063, 2022-07).

## Strategy and growth

### Pricing
- Founders undercharge; pricing is the first lever a buyer would pull. "Most people do not charge enough for their software or their products in their business or services. Period. Full stop." [S056, 2023-01]

### Discounting
- Never discount to save a cancelling customer. "offer pauses downgrades or help instead of discounts" [S124, post]

### Focus versus diversification
- One business until it scales. "Any wealthy person made it because of one business that they focused on until they got it to scale 10 million plus in Revenue." [S050, 2023-11]

### Side projects
- A distraction until you are good. "I have 17 side businesses that's not focused that's a distraction and you're not that good yet" [S030, 2024-12] If a new idea must run, give it a separate team and calendar blocks (S105, 2024-05).

### Niche
- Go narrow first, broaden after traction. "In the early days is you pick a niche, and you go deep." [S099, 2016-07]

### Content and personal brand
- Give the best material away; get paid for implementation. "information is free implementation is paid give away your best stuff to attract people then they'll pay you to help Implement that" [S007, 2024-07]
- Brand is the top-end lever. "Systems will help you become a millionaire, but brand will help you become a billionaire." [S043, 2025-07]

### Paid ads
- Likes them for speed. "I love paid cuz it's fast." [S006, 2024-08] Outsource execution, never the strategy (S119, post).

### AI and headcount (tension)
- **Lean side:** "The days of bragging about how big your team is are gone. Now, it's bragging about how much revenue you make with the least amount of people." [S021, 2026-03]
- **Team side:** "Your team is your competitive advantage. AI should make them 10 times better, not replace them." [S127, post] On layoffs: "As a leader, if I had to do that, it's because I didn't see and upskill everybody." [S012, 2026-04]
- Reconciled (inferred from S012, S112, S125): add AI before adding heads, and upskill existing staff into agent operators rather than cutting them.

### Culture
- Designed standards, not perks. "most people think culture is ping pong tables pizza parties and work from home it's not" [S115, post]

### Metrics
- Weekly, owned by the people who report them. "If you want to, you know, 5x, 10x your growth in the next 12 months, you need to measure weekly to know if you're on track." [S087, 2018-09]

### Competition
- Focus beats competitors. "If somebody's focused on one they're going to be able to compete against you because they don't have the distractions." [S074, 2021-05] Monetization wins markets: "Whoever makes the most from a customer wins the market." [S022, 2026-03]

### Selling the company
- Most sell to escape complexity and should not. "Most people sell when they shouldn't sell." [S017, 2026-09] Still build it sellable: "A company that could sell is a great company to operate." [S114, post]

### Timing and decision speed
- Decide at about 70% certainty; most decisions are reversible. "I can always reverse a decision, but 70% is good enough for me." [S029, 2025-06]

### Coaching and advisors
- Pay for coaching early. "until you pay most people will not pay attention" [S107, 2024-10] Boards and investors are there to work: "The board is there to do work." [S072, 2021-11]
