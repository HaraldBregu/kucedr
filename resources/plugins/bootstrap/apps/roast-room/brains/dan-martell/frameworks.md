# Dan Martell: frameworks

Ordered by how central they are to the seat (founder time and people first, then operating rhythm, then SaaS growth). Source counts are the sources in this corpus where the framework appears by name or in substance. Most of the time-and-hiring frameworks are taught in his book Buy Back Your Time (2023); here they come only from his own public talks, interviews, and posts.

---

## 1. The Buyback Principle (calendar over capacity)

- **What it is:** You hire to free the founder's calendar of low-value work, not to add capacity. Growth follows because the founder's freed hours go into high-value work.
- **Parts:** Look at your calendar, not the org chart; find what is not your core skill; hire for that first; reinvest the hours.
- **When to use:** Any hiring or outsourcing decision, and any time a founder says they need more people.
- **How to score a decision:** Does this hire remove hours from the founder's calendar (or a leader's), and do we know what those hours will be spent on? Yes and yes passes. A hire that adds output but leaves the founder's calendar as full fails, and he predicts overwhelm around 12 employees (S073).
- **Quote:** "You don't hire people to grow your business. You hire people to buy back your time. It's a calendar over capacity problem." [S014, 2024]
- **Sources:** 20 (S014, S017, S018, S020, S027, S031, S036, S037, S038, S039, S049, S054, S059, S061, S062, S073, S074, S107, S108, S122).

## 2. Buyback Rate (and Effective Hourly Rate)

- **What it is:** The most you should pay to get one hour of your time back.
- **Parts:** Add salary, profit, dividends, and personal expenses run through the business; divide by 2,000 hours; divide by 4 for a 4x return. $100K gives $12.50 an hour; $500K gives $62.50 (S018). An older version divides revenue by 2,000 and calls it E.H.R. (S073); a stretch version prices the hour from your goal, $500 an hour for a $1M goal (S019, S038, S109).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a task is worth doing yourself and what to pay for help.
- **How to score:** List every recurring task. If someone can do it for less than the Buyback Rate, the founder doing it is a loss. If no task falls below the rate, the founder is undercharging (S019).
- **Quote:** "The buyback rate is how much money you're willing to invest to buy back an hour of your time." [S036, 2025]
- **Sources:** 14 (S013, S015, S017, S018, S019, S020, S036, S037, S038, S039, S062, S073, S076, S122).

## 3. Time and Energy Audit

- **What it is:** Two weeks of calendar data on where time goes and what each activity does to your energy.
- **Parts:** Alarm every 15 minutes, log what you did; highlight green (gives energy) or red (drains); mark $ to $$$$ for what it would cost to pay someone; the red, $1 bucket is the hit list and the next hire (S037, S057). An early version sorts tasks into $10, $100, $500, $5,000 tasks (S076, credited to Todd Herman).
- **When to use:** Before any hire, and again whenever you hit a new ceiling; he reruns it about three times a year (S014).
- **How to score:** A decision passes if it moves red $1 work off the founder and keeps green, high-dollar work on.
- **Quote:** "you highlight in green everything that gives you energy, and you highlight in red anything that sucks your energy" [S057, 2022]
- **Sources:** 20 (S013, S014, S015, S017, S018, S019, S020, S036, S037, S038, S039, S041, S043, S052, S057, S060, S076, S122, S126, S128).

## 4. The DRIP Matrix

- **What it is:** A 2x2 of money (makes you money) against energy (lights you up).
- **Parts:** Delegate (low money, low energy: delete, defer, delegate); Replace (makes money, drains you: hand off with ownership); Invest (lights you up, not paying yet: skills, relationships); Produce (money and energy: aim for five to six hours a day here, S061).
- **When to use:** Sorting the audit; deciding what the founder keeps.
- **How to score:** Place the task. Delegate and Replace go to someone else, Invest gets protected time, Produce gets the best hours. He warns against hiring yourself out of Produce work you love (S043, S076).
- **Quote:** "So delegate, replace, invest and produce. Produce is where you want to be." [S061, 2022]
- **Sources:** 10 (S013, S014, S019, S037, S046, S047, S052, S059, S061, S062).

## 5. The Buyback Loop (Audit, Transfer, Fill)

- **What it is:** The repeatable cycle to run each time growth creates calendar pain.
- **Parts:** Audit (time and energy audit); Transfer (camcorder method, playbook, 10-80-10); Fill (put freed time into money-making work first, then skills, beliefs, and character).
- **When to use:** At the pain line, or whenever saying yes to an opportunity would create chaos.
- **How to score:** A plan without the Fill step fails. Clients who freed 35 hours and had no plan felt lazy and guilty (S052, S108).
- **Quote:** "Once you experience the pain then you audit you transfer and fill." [S020, 2023]
- **Sources:** 15 (S006, S013, S014, S017, S018, S019, S020, S031, S036, S037, S038, S057, S108, S122, S123).

## 6. The Replacement Ladder

- **What it is:** The order of hires that buys back the most time for the least money.
- **Parts:** 1 Admin (executive assistant owns 100% of inbox and calendar); 2 Delivery (onboarding, support, fulfillment); 3 Marketing (someone who runs traffic and campaigns daily); 4 Sales (once selling takes about half your calendar); 5 Leadership (department heads, a flow leader or operator).
- **When to use:** "Who do I hire next?" Also to block a premature COO hire.
- **How to score:** Is every lower rung already filled? A COO or VP before an assistant is the classic fail: "You're going to pay somebody six figures plus to essentially have a nonrevenue generating role on your team." [S037, 2025] Sales comes fourth because the founder sells best early (S031).
- **Quote:** "Sales is four because you as a CEO will always be able to sell better than anybody else you hire." [S031, 2024]
- **Sources:** 17 (S006, S013, S014, S017, S018, S019, S020, S031, S037, S038, S047, S049, S061, S062, S105, S108, S112).

## 7. The Camcorder Method

- **What it is:** Train your replacement by recording yourself doing the work while you do it.
- **Parts:** Record 3 to 5 narrated screen recordings; give them as week-one onboarding; the hire writes the SOP; read their SOP to check understanding; reuse the videos if they leave (S020, S057). He says do not have AI write the SOP from the video, because you lose that comprehension check (S036, S122), though one 2026 post suggests ChatGPT for it (S121).
- **When to use:** Every transfer of a recurring task.
- **How to score:** Can a new person get 70 to 80% ramped from the documents alone (S097)? If not, the transfer is not done.
- **Quote:** "I use this thing called the camcorder method I literally record myself doing the work while I'm doing the work so it takes me no extra time." [S020, 2023]
- **Sources:** 13 (S006, S013, S020, S031, S036, S037, S038, S057, S061, S108, S121, S122, S123).

## 8. The 10-80-10 Rule

- **What it is:** Delegating creative or complex projects: you own the first 10% (ideation, outcome, resources) and the last 10% (integration, launch); the team owns the 80% execution.
- **When to use:** Projects the founder is tempted to author, such as content, a book, product launches. Examples he uses: Steve Jobs and Jony Ive, Tom Clancy's ghostwritten books (S041, S126).
- **How to score:** Is the founder only in the first and last 10%? Editing, not authoring, passes.
- **Quote:** "You want to be involved when you're delegating any project in the first 10%. I call that the ideation phase." [S041, 2026]
- **Sources:** 9 (S011, S036, S037, S041, S112, S122, S123, S126, S128).

## 9. Delegate the outcome, not the task (definition of done)

- **What it is:** State what done looks like (who is notified, where files live, how customers should feel) and let the person build the to-do list.
- **When to use:** Every handoff and every new hire.
- **How to score:** Can the owner state the outcome and their one number back to you? If you wrote their task list, you hired a tenant.
- **Quote:** "When you assign a task, you create a tenant. But when you assign an outcome, you create an owner." [S113, undated post]
- **Sources:** 9 (S014, S031, S037, S047, S055, S092, S113, S121, S123).

## 10. Transformational leadership: Outcome, Measure, Coach (versus the tell-check-next doom loop)

- **What it is:** Transactional leaders tell, check, then tell the next thing, which caps the company around 10 to 14 people (S017, S031, S038). Transformational leaders give an outcome, one number, and coaching to the violated principle.
- **Parts:** Outcome (what a 10 out of 10 looks like); Measure (one number per person, such as assistant response time); Coach (principle, personal story, takeaway, commitment, kept on a running list for 1-on-1s, S031).
- **When to use:** When the founder is jumping in on every problem or managing more than about ten people directly.
- **How to score:** Does every person have one outcome and one number they report? Is feedback given on principles in private, not tasks in public?
- **Quote:** "We start with the outcome, where are we going, then we go to measure, what is the one number, and then we coach them." [S031, 2024]
- **Sources:** 10 (S009, S017, S019, S031, S037, S038, S047, S081, S108, S115).

## 11. The 1-3-1 Rule

- **What it is:** Anyone bringing a problem brings one defined problem, three options they researched, and one recommendation. He says he approves the recommendation about 98% of the time (S031). Learned from Brad Peterson (S081).
- **When to use:** Whenever the team routes decisions to the founder.
- **How to score:** Count decisions that reached you without a recommendation. Each one is a training gap.
- **Quote:** "One specific problem, what are your three viable options that you've considered, I want to hear them, tell me how you research them, and then give me your one specific recommendation." [S031, 2024]
- **Sources:** 11 (S007, S014, S020, S031, S036, S037, S038, S052, S081, S121, S122).

## 12. 50 to Fix It (the Decision Ladder)

- **What it is:** Pre-approved spending authority so problems get fixed without the founder: frontline $50, managers $500, directors $5,000, executives $50,000; spend, then tell your leader.
- **When to use:** When approvals bottleneck on the founder.
- **How to score:** Is there a written authority limit at each level, and do people use it?
- **Quote:** "It’s 50 to fix it. Anybody on the team that runs into an issue with a customer in the business can spend up to $50, no questions asked, to solve a problem." [S122, undated post]
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S014, S030, S031, S036, S037, S038, S122, S123).

## 13. The Pain Line, the Three S's, and the Complexity Ceiling

- **What it is:** The point where more growth creates more pain than the founder can handle, usually about $1M to $1.5M and 12 employees (S020, S037, S060), or at each tripling: $300K, $900K, $2.7M (S028). Founders respond by stalling, selling, or sabotaging. The complexity ceiling is the most complexity a person can tolerate before pulling back.
- **When to use:** A founder who wants to sell, coast, or keeps "forgetting" to answer the big opportunity.
- **How to score:** Ask if they would still sell if their list of complaints were not true (S031). If not, it is a pain line problem, and selling only resets them at the same ceiling (S038).
- **Quote:** "The pain line is this point where any more growth will create more pain in your life then you're able to deal with." [S020, 2023]
- **Sources:** 14 for the pain line (S013, S014, S017, S018, S019, S020, S028, S031, S037, S038, S057, S061, S062, S108); complexity ceiling in 8 (S011, S017, S031, S036, S038, S047, S052, S122).

## 14. The Five Time Assassins

- **What it is:** Habits that eat the time a founder buys back. Staller (waits for perfect), Speed Demon (hires the cheapest fastest option, then cleans up), Supervisor (micromanages), Saver (steps over dollars to pick up dimes), Self-Medicator (vices that cost days).
- **When to use:** Before spending on help, and when a founder has delegated but still feels stretched.
- **How to score:** Name the assassin and remove it before hiring more.
- **Quote:** "I call these the five assassins and I want to break them down for you so you can potentially see yourself in them" [S059, 2022]
- **Sources:** 8 (S019, S020, S037, S052, S059, S061, S108, S109).

## 15. The Perfect Week and the Preloaded Year

- **What it is:** A designed week built around energy, set before other people's requests fill it; the Preloaded Year puts big rocks (vacations, family dates, planning) into the full year first.
- **Parts:** Big rocks first; deep work in the morning, no meetings before 10 or 11 (S041, S110); meetings batched on set days; admin late; personal time non-negotiable; cut 15 minutes from meetings; NET time.
- **When to use:** Calendars that start blank and fill reactively; founders working nights.
- **How to score:** Does the calendar show the stated priorities? "your calendar and bank account tell me what's important to you" [S007, 2024]
- **Quote:** "design a perfect week that if you executed exactly what was on there, you would walk away feeling energized and productive" [S039, 2026]
- **Sources:** Perfect Week 15 (S004, S015, S019, S026, S037, S039, S041, S047, S050, S052, S054, S086, S110, S126, S128); Preloaded Year 7 (S019, S033, S037, S039, S042, S054, S062).

## 16. NET Time (No Extra Time)

- **What it is:** Pair two activities so one costs no extra time: 1-on-1s on scooters, team calls on the bike trainer, calls while driving, a weekly founders hike instead of coffee meetings.
- **How to score:** Can this meeting ride along with something already on the calendar?
- **Quote:** "Net time stands for no extra time." [S033, 2024]
- **Sources:** 12 (S004, S013, S015, S020, S033, S037, S039, S044, S051, S057, S108, S128).

## 17. Test-First Hiring (the talent pipeline)

- **What it is:** A funnel that simulates the job before an offer.
- **Parts:** Paid job ads at about 5% of the role's total comp (S040); 60-second video answering set questions, archive anyone who ignores instructions; profile and cognitive assessment as data, strict only on cognitive ability (S108); the same paid test project, about 10 hours, for the top three; a 15-minute final on childhood, toughest challenge, and five-year vision (S108); team veto (S040); in the final, try to talk them out of the job (S043).
- **When to use:** Every role, including assistants and house managers. Director level and above he sources outbound or through recruiters and the IKEA-effect advice session (S101, S108).
- **How to score:** Did the test simulate the actual work? A blog-ideas test for a TikTok hire failed (S013).
- **Quote:** "Everybody I hire has to complete a test project. I'll pay them for it if they want 10 hours and they all do the same test project." [S074, 2021]
- **Sources:** 14 (S013, S020, S030, S037, S040, S048, S054, S055, S066, S074, S080, S083, S108, S123).

## 18. Next Hire Focuser (hire scoring)

- **What it is:** Choose between candidate roles by scoring each 1 to 3 on: will it make me money, how much time will it save me, how affordable is it. Score for the CEO, not the business. Then avoid three mistakes: single-role thinking, no documented first two weeks, and not giving full access.
- **How to score:** Highest total wins; a salesperson scores 3 on money, a project manager 1 (S060). Do not make the offer until the first two weeks of onboarding are written (S060).
- **Quote:** "These are for you, the CEO, not for your business. That's the biggest mistake people make." [S060, 2022]
- **Sources:** 3 (S060, S073, S076).

## 19. Five Steps to Fire Gracefully

- **What it is:** Prep the accounts and transition list; sit down face to face, short, never email; it is not a discussion; remove access during the meeting; explain it to the team in terms of values. Hire slow, fire fast (S083).
- **How to score:** Was the person ever surprised, and was access cut the same hour? A VP of Sales who left with the customer database is the failure case (S083).
- **Quote:** "So five steps to fire an employee gracefully. Number one, prep the accounts so you know what projects they were working on and who needs to be transitioned access." [S083, 2019]
- **Sources:** 1 dedicated (S083); fire-fast rule also in S040, S052, S081.

## 20. Company OS: the five meetings and the CIAF one-on-one

- **What it is:** Daily 15-minute stand-up (done, doing, stuck), weekly sync (wins, rocks, scorecard), monthly review, quarterly 90-day planning, yearly strategic planning. One-on-ones follow CIAF: Connection, Inspiration, Alignment, Feedback, about five minutes each (S079).
- **When to use:** Around 20 people, when the founder is running around and nothing gets done to standard (S078).
- **How to score:** Are all five rhythms on the calendar with a standard agenda, and does every direct report get a structured one-on-one?
- **Quote:** "I call it the company OS." [S078, 2019]
- **Sources:** 9 (S014, S036, S078, S079, S085, S095, S096, S122, S123).

## 21. Six core functions, org by stage, and the direct-report cap

- **What it is:** Every SaaS company has product, engineering, marketing, sales, customer success, and ops/admin. Under 12 people, founders each own a pair; $120K to $500K MRR adds player-coach leads; $500K+ MRR adds a CRO, CTO, and COO. Every function has one owner and its own metrics. Nobody, including the CEO, has more than five to seven direct reports.
- **How to score:** One name per function; no shared ownership ("When you have two people accountable for something you have nobody accountable for it." [S090, 2018]); count direct reports.
- **Quote:** "You really don't wanna have anybody have more than five to seven direct reports." [S075, 2020]
- **Sources:** 9 for the cap (S007, S031, S052, S058, S070, S073, S075, S079, S117); org model in S075, S081, S088, S117.

## 22. The 10-5-20 pricing rule (early SaaS)

- **What it is:** Customers must perceive 10x the value of the price; raise the price 5% at a time; keep going until about 20% of prospects push back.
- **When to use:** Early days, before enough data for packaging and segmentation. Later he uses Van Westendorp surveys, three tiers, and value metrics (S056).
- **How to score:** Zero pushback means underpriced.
- **Quote:** "Number three, 20% pushback, so yes, increase your prices by five percent" [S089, 2018]
- **Sources:** 2 (S089; pricing follow-up in S056).

## 23. Six Phase Scaling System and the 8 levels

- **What it is:** Buy back your time; clarify strategy and offer (Value Creation Venn: what customers pay most for, what the team does best, what is most profitable); build a predictable growth engine (inbound, outbound, partners); systematize delivery (playbooks, people, platforms); install leadership and management (50 to Fix It, meeting rhythms); scale culture and vision. The 8-levels post maps tools to revenue bands, from FOCUS (one customer, one offer, one channel for a year) at $100K to $300K up to 40-40-20 time allocation at $100M+ (S123).
- **How to score:** Do not skip ahead; a founder installing leadership before buying back time or fixing the offer is out of sequence.
- **Quote:** "And look, don’t skip ahead because the concepts we talk about in each phase are needed to understand the one after that." [S122, undated post]
- **Sources:** 3 (S036, S122, S123).

## 24. The Four C's of Leverage

- **What it is:** Output equals time times leverage, and leverage comes from content (playbooks and media), code (software, automation, AI), capital, and collaboration (people). Credited to Naval Ravikant.
- **How to score:** Which C does this decision add? Doing it yourself adds none.
- **Quote:** "those four C's content Capital code and collaboration if you've mastered those four skills you have unlimited amount of scale" [S007, 2024]
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S017, S020, S031, S032, S033, S037, S047).
