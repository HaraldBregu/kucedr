# Positions: Naval Ravikant

39 topics, built from the batch notes on 35 sources (nav.al posts 2009 to 2011, the How to Get Rich and angel investing series 2019 to 2020, and podcasts and talks 2016 to 2026). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (post date, or upload date for videos; see `sources.md`). Where his view moved, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked **(newer)**. Topics with no evidence say so. S017 and S030 to S035 are auto-captions without speaker labels, so their quotes keep caption spelling and were attributed to him by content. He warns that "business is very very very contextual" (S030, 2025-03), so treat these as his defaults, not a playbook.

## Leverage, equity, and the seat's question

### 1. Renting out your time versus owning equity
- **Stance:** You do not get rich selling hours. Own a piece of a product, a business, IP, or a brand, and put your name on it.
- **Evidence:**
  - "You’re not going to get rich renting out your time." (S024, 2019-11)
  - "So everybody who really makes money at some point owns a piece of a product, or a business, or some kind of IP." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "need to have equity either as an owner an investor shareholder or a brand that you're building that accrues to you to gain your financial freedom" (S017, 2019-06)
  - "Essentially, taking accountability for your actions is the same as taking an equity position in all of your work." (S001, 2019-12)
  - **(newer)** Knowledge is now the bigger part of what he owns: "knowledge is the big multiplier. It's not the capital." (S032, 2026-01)
- **Read:** Consistent from 2009 to 2026. The 2025 to 2026 version adds David Deutsch's definition of wealth, so the asset that matters most is know-how, with capital as stored potential (inferred from S028, S032).

### 2. Services, agencies, and high-paid hourly work
- **Stance:** A high hourly rate is still rented time. A services business is a rung on a ladder (laborer, contractor, developer, tech company), and the question is what it leaves you owning.
- **Evidence:**
  - "So, what that means is when you’re sleeping, you’re not earning." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "You really want to stay out of labor-based leverage." (S001, 2019-12)
  - Lifestyle exception: "If you’re doing that, your number one priority is not getting wealthy." (S001, 2019-12)
- **Read:** He does not call service work wrong. He asks whether it is climbing toward equity, code, or media (inferred from S001, S017).

### 3. Headcount and team size
- **Stance:** More people is a weak signal of impact. Use the smallest team that can do the job.
- **Over time:**
  - 2019-12: "We just automatically assume that more people is better." (S001)
  - 2025-11: "Scale is your enemy." (S007) and "It just takes a small group of people to create something great." (S007)
  - 2026-04: "You truly can have one-person, two-person software companies now that can scale to millions upon millions of users and make billions upon billions of dollars." (S006)
  - 2026-05: "So you want to be in the minimum-sized group to do the scope of the job that you set out to do." (S011)
  - **(newer, economy level)** 2026-06: "Basic economics says that when productivity is higher, you’re wealthier and you hire more people, not fewer." (S003)
- **Read:** Small teams at the company level, more hiring across the economy. He holds both without calling it a tension.

## AI, code, and venture capital over time

### 4. AI
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: on general AI, "I think we are barely any closer to that than we were 20 or 30 years ago." (S016) Narrow AI was the bet: "The specific AI companies, the ones that are solving a very specific problem, like the computer vision example. Those I think could be very real." (S016)
  - 2019-06: "we're nowhere near close to general ai not in our lifetimes" (S017)
  - 2019-08: the singularity "is religion for nerds you know it's got all the same characteristics it's unfalsifiable until it happens" (S031)
  - 2019-12: "You’re much more likely to be doing a job that can be eventually replaced by a robot, or by an AI." (S001)
  - 2025-03: LLMs are real: "They make English a programming language." (S030) But "I think ASI is a fantasy." (S030)
  - 2026-01: "No entrepreneur is going to be replaced by an AI. They might be replaced by another entrepreneur who uses AI better." (S032)
  - 2026-05: "As for AGI, I don’t know. I don’t want to be in the futurist business." (S009)
  - 2026-06: "human plus AI, we’re in that era, and I’m betting we stay there longer than people think." (S003)
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: "But that's all true until AI can totally and completely replace you, which I don't see around the corner, but I'm more open-minded about it now." (S035)
- **Read:** He moved from AGI skeptic (2016 to 2019) to daily AI builder who will not forecast AGI (2026), and by mid-2026 he is less sure humans stay in the loop. The constant: answers get commoditized, so the edge is judgment, taste, and desire. "you get the AI answer, everybody gets the AI answer." (S032, 2026-01)

### 5. Code and software as leverage
- **Over time:**
  - 2009-11: "Most web products have no marginal cost of replication, so adding a new customer is pure profit." (S018) and "What used to cost $1M-$2M to set up, now costs $10K." (S018)
  - 2019-06: "coding is thinking it's automatic structure thinking" (S017)
  - 2019-12: "Coding is such a great superpower because now you can speak the language of the robot armies and you can tell them what to do." (S001) He also calls the slogan learn to code "This is glib. This is a little overused." (S001)
  - 2025-08: "So this whole idea that you don't need to study software engineering is nonsense." (S034) and SaaS "feels quite saturated" because "AI is making software dev so easy" (S034)
  - 2026-01: "software engineers are getting richer and more powerful than ever." (S032)
  - 2026-02: "Vibe coding is the new product management." (S002)
  - 2026-04: "pure software is uninvestable. I would just full stop right there." (S006)
  - 2026-05: "I went from not having written code in twenty years to coding all the time now" (S012)
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: "The moment you can specify it, an AI can oneshot it or twoshot it" (S035)
- **Read:** Code went from the best permissionless leverage (2009 to 2019) to a commodity as a product (2026). Knowing how software works got more valuable at the same time. He holds both: engineers are the most leveraged people, and a pure software company is not a moat.

### 6. Venture capital and raising money
- **Over time:**
  - 2010-04, as a market analyst: "It is rational for private investors to move in herds." (S020)
  - 2010-12: "whose money you’re investing is less relevant than on what terms it comes on" (S022). He names downstream financing risk as the biggest risk an investor carries (S021).
  - 2016-05: "Don’t take your advice from investors if you can help it" (S016)
  - 2019-11, as an angel: "As the hits become bigger and bigger and the returns become more nonlinear, it makes more sense to play as an investor and a little less sense to play as a founder." (S024) and "There’s extremely little innovation in the venture capital business." (S024)
  - 2019-12: "It is a good form of leverage, but the hard part with capital is how do you obtain it?" (S001)
  - 2025-03: self-funding his new company: "I'm doing a new business. I'm self-unding it." (S030)
  - 2025-11: "I won’t take money from any VC." (S007) Most VCs "they’re just money managers pushing money around." (S007) Instead, "if your product shows well, to build a killer launch video and a great demo of the product and try to get it to go viral online" (S007)
  - 2026-01: "most of the great companies are either self- financed or they break out very early before they really need venture capital." (S032)
  - 2026-04: pure software is not venture-investable (S006). 2026-05: "VCs are now being forced to fund more hardware, rockets, drones, AI" (S009)
- **Read:** Three eras. 2010: he explains how VC works and builds AngelList. 2019 to 2020: he argues investing beats founding on returns. **(newer)** 2025 to 2026: he founds again with his own money, refuses VC, and says iconic companies rarely come from round after round.

### 7. When and how to raise
- **Stance:** Raise when your own excitement crosses a threshold, six to 12 months early so you can walk, and never through a banker. The founder does it personally.
- **Evidence:**
  - "when my own excitement level gets above a certain threshold, then I know I’m ready to go raise the money" (S011, 2026-05)
  - "preferably six to 12 months before we need it." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "companies that raise money through bankers are always starting off on the wrong foot." (S007, 2025-11)
  - On recruiting, fundraising, strategy, and product vision: "I don’t think you can outsource any of those four." (S007, 2025-11)
  - On rejection: "I don’t fixate on the person who didn’t get it, because most people won’t get it." (S011, 2026-05)
  - Approach investors in writing first: "Written always beats a video or screencast, since most intelligent people can read a lot faster than they can listen." (S023, 2010-04)

### 8. Investor terms and control
- **Stance:** Judge money by its terms. Do not take capital with board seats, preferred stock, and vetoes you cannot unwind.
- **Evidence:** "you just don’t take the bad deal." (S011, 2026-05); "It is better not to be trapped in a bad deal." (S011, 2026-05); terms over source (S022, 2010-12, see topic 6).

### 9. Debt
- **Stance:** Debt is the capped slot in the capital stack. He talks about it as an investor's return, not as a way to fund a company.
- **Evidence:** "They might be making 5, 10, 15, 20, 25% a year, but that’s what their upside is limited to." (S001, 2019-12) On ruin: "So, don’t gamble everything on one go. But take rationally optimistic bets with big upside." (S001, 2019-12) On his own money: "I never set out to save anything; I reinvested almost everything." (S024, 2019-11)
- **Read:** No recorded position on borrowing to fund a business. His ruin rules suggest he would refuse debt that can wipe you out (inferred from S001).

## Money in the business

### 10. Pricing
- **Stance:** Charge by willingness to pay, but only by giving rich customers something extra. Keep the product line simple.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Price discrimination is important. It means you can charge people based on their propensity to pay." (S001, 2019-12) His example is business class.
  - "if if you're selling multiple versions of your product, you better have a really good reason for that." (S032, 2026-01)
  - On his own wealth content: "everything that i've ever created on this topic of how to make money i will never charge a dollar for because that would ruin it" (S017, 2019-06)
- **Read:** No recorded position on raising prices or premium versus cheap positioning.

### 11. Discounting
- No recorded position on price discounts. The discounting he does talk about is valuing a promised payoff: "You should get very comfortable doing rough net present value calculations in your head." (S001, 2019-12)

### 12. Risk and ruin
- **Stance:** Take asymmetric bets with capped downside, never an all-in bet, and never a reputation risk.
- **Evidence:**
  - "on the downside you can only lose 1 X but on the upside you can make 10,000 X so I tried to rig the game" (S031, 2019-08)
  - "The number one way people get ruined in modern business is not by betting too much; it’s by cutting corners and doing unethical or downright illegal things." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "Generally, you don’t want to invest money that you’re not OK losing." (S025, 2020-06)
  - **(newer)** He now funds his own company at scale: "right now we're burning a million and a half dollars a month of my money." (S032, 2026-01)
- **Read:** The 2026 self-funded burn is a large bet, made from wealth he can lose. His rule is about ruin relative to what you have (inferred from S001, S032).

### 13. Business size and the 100x bar
- **Stance:** A small business takes as much effort as a large one, so only spend time and capital where 100x is possible.
- **Evidence:** "One thing about business that people don’t realize: it takes just as much effort to create a small business as it does to create a large one." (S001, 2019-12); "it only makes sense to do that for something that has that opportunity to be 100x or 1,000x on your time or on your capital." (S011, 2026-05)

## People

### 14. First hires
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-12: "Your first two engineers? They’re just late founders. Treat them as such. Expect as much." (S019)
  - 2025-11: geniuses only. "So collect geniuses. Warehouse them. You’ll never regret it." (S007) Hire them without a slot (S007).
  - 2026-01: "you need more technical builders at the beginning and less of the marketers" (S032)
  - 2026-06: "If someone’s really good with AI and really smart and creative, I want to hire them more than ever, for the leverage." (S003)
- **Marketing hires:** "if you want to hire someone to do your social media, they’d better have a great social media account for themselves." (S007, 2025-11)

### 15. Hiring bar and recruiting
- **Stance:** Never outsource recruiting. Intelligence, energy, integrity, low ego. Let candidates interview anyone on the team.
- **Evidence:** "So we really cannot outsource recruiting." (S007, 2025-11); "You’ll be able to manage 30 or 40 low-ego people when you might only be able to manage five high-ego people, because you’re always massaging their egos." (S007, 2025-11); "People who are better than you don’t want to work for you for long." (S007, 2025-11); he resists hiring and hires slowly (S034, 2025-08).

### 16. Pay and stock
- **Stance:** Pay well, give a lot of stock, and expect far more output.
- **Evidence:** "pay twice as much but but expect 10 times the output." (S034, 2025-08); "I like to pay people well and treat them well and give them a lot of stock and high salaries and good compensation." (S034, 2025-08); "always overpay and expect the best." (S030, 2025-03)

### 17. Firing
- **Over time:**
  - 2025-08: "Yeah, I fire people much much faster." (S034)
  - 2025-11: "If you’re not firing, it means that you’re deluding yourself." (S007)
  - **(newer)** 2026-05: "Like I’m very bad at firing people." (S011) He leans on co-founders, and only fires once "I have to be convinced that they’re going to be more effective somewhere else in some other role" (S011)
- **Read:** He preaches fast firing and admits he is bad at doing it himself.

### 18. Co-founders and partners
- **Stance:** Intelligence, energy, and integrity with no compromise, read from actions. One builder and one seller. If not for life, not for a day.
- **Evidence:**
  - "In terms of picking people to work with, pick ones that have high intelligence, high energy, and high integrity, I find that’s the three-part checklist that you cannot compromise on." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "If you can’t see yourself working with someone for life, then don’t work with them for a day." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "The most under-recognized reason startups fail is because the founders fall apart." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "Seek integrity over niceness." (S025, 2020-06)
  - Failure causes he looks for: "your co-founder is not in it for the right reasons or you don't have the right kind of co-founder or your vesting schedule is wrong" (S030, 2025-03)
  - Builder and seller: "There’s a builder and there’s a seller." (S005, 2019-03)

### 19. Equity splits
- **Stance:** Early people get founder-size stakes, and nobody should fight over small slices.
- **Evidence:** "Throw out the old cap tables. A founder doesn’t get 30% and an early engineer shouldn’t get 0.25%." (S019, 2011-12); "Generally, I think people spend too much time splitting hairs on small stakes and not enough time focusing on the big upside." (S011, 2026-05); at Impossible his 23-year-old co-founder holds equal stock (S034, 2025-08); for the right person he breaks cap table rules: "But we break every rule." (S007, 2025-11)

### 20. Culture and management
- **Stance:** Every good company is idiosyncratic and founder-shaped. No consensus building, no big-company process, no Slack.
- **Evidence:**
  - "the founder’s personality is the company because your principles and your non-negotiables and your values dictate who you’re going to hire." (S007, 2025-11)
  - "Anyone who tells you to listen to others and build consensus and gather feedback is implying that you’re weak, you’re not good enough at what you do, or that you have the wrong approach." (S007, 2025-11)
  - "I don’t like the word management because great people don’t need to be managed." (S007, 2025-11)
  - "I actually hate organizational management because I hate organizations." (S009, 2026-05) Hierarchy is "a requirement of size, and we’re just not at that size" (S009, 2026-05)
  - "We don’t even use Slack" (S009, 2026-05)

## Focus and time

### 21. Founder time and delegation
- **Stance:** Set an absurd hourly rate and outsource anything below it. Decline meetings. Keep the four things you cannot delegate.
- **Evidence:** "Set a very high aspirational hourly rate for yourself, and stick to it. It should seem and feel absurdly high. If it doesn’t, it’s not high enough." (S001, 2019-12); "If you can hire someone to do it for less than your hourly rate, hire them." (S001, 2019-12); "You have to be able to delete emails and text messages without flinching if you want to scale." (S030, 2025-03); "Guard your time. It’s all you have." (S016, 2016-05)

### 22. Focus versus diversification
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: "Just focus on the one or two really really important things, and everything else, just surrender to it." (S016)
  - 2019-06: "specialization is for insects" (S017)
  - 2025-03: explore first, then say no by default: "You have to say no to everything by default." (S030) and "if you can't decide, the answer is no." (S030)
  - 2025-08: "I don't think in this age of specialization, it's worth covering your weaknesses." (S034)
- **In investing:** "In some sense, diversification is a hedge against the lack of knowledge." (S025, 2020-06)
- **Read:** Broad as a person, narrow as a company. Explore widely, then commit for years: "go find the thing you can commit to for 10 years" (S031, 2019-08).

### 23. Side projects and obsessions
- **Stance:** Follow obsessions fully. He supports employees starting their own companies.
- **Evidence:** "I don’t look for balance. I look to feed my obsessions." (S011, 2026-05); "vibe coding is just my latest obsession." (S011, 2026-05); "I’m never going to get in the way of you starting a company." (S001, 2019-12); principles can "inform your weekend projects." (S001, 2019-12)

### 24. Work hours
- **Over time:**
  - 2019-12: "Nobody really works 80 to 120 hours a week at high output, with mental clarity." (S001) But "If getting wealthy is your goal, you’re going to have to work as hard as you can." (S001)
  - 2020-06: "If you’re doing a startup, you and the rest of the core team will have to work your tails off." (S025)
  - 2024-10: "you should live your life like an athlete or like a lion right which means that most of your time you're resting" (S033)
  - 2025-08: for his team, "I expect them to always 247 be either working or thinking about work because that's the same bar I hold myself to." (S034)
  - **(newer)** 2026-01: "the guy running the corner grocery store puts more hours in the grocery store than Elon puts into any one of his companies." (S032)
- **Read:** Hours are not the variable he measures. Direction, people, and intense sprints are. His 2025 always-on bar for the team sits awkwardly next to his lion-resting line.

### 25. Burnout
- **Stance:** Burnout means the work is wrong or not working, so a break does not fix it.
- **Evidence:** "Usually burnout is a sign you’re working on something that either isn’t working or you don’t enjoy the work fundamentally." (S007, 2025-11); "burnout to me is a clear case that like you're not enjoying this anymore and if you're not enjoying this anymore you should probably stop working on it." (S034, 2025-08); "Busy is a death of productivity and happiness." (S016, 2016-05)

### 26. Remote work and location
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: "I think the gig economy is going to be much more the future." (S016) In the same talk: "Unfortunately, I’d say, move to SF." (S016)
  - 2019-06: "we're seeing an atomization of the firm we're seeing the optimal size of the firm shrinking" (S017)
  - 2024-10: "where you live is the single most important decision you make" (S033)
  - **(newer)** 2025-08: his own team is under 20, mostly in person in San Francisco: "Small team. There's less than 20 of us." (S034) and "the Bay Area is a huge cheat code" (S034)
- **Read:** Work is going independent for individuals, but his own company sits in one hub, in person.

## Market and growth

### 27. Niche and winner-take-all
- **Stance:** Be the best at something, and keep redefining it until you are. The middle gets crushed between aggregators and the long tail.
- **Evidence:** "So, whatever niche obsession you have, the internet allows you to scale." (S001, 2019-12); "Become the best in the world at what you do. Keep redefining what you do until this is true." (S002, 2026-02); "And remember: there is no demand for average." (S002, 2026-02); "it creates one big hegemon for everything and it creates an atomized long tail of millions and millions of individuals" (S017, 2019-06)
- **Investor brand caveat:** "It’s best to build a brand around your unique capabilities, platforms and assets, but not around verticals." (S024, 2019-11)

### 28. Content and building in public
- **Over time:**
  - 2019-12: "Coding, writing books, recording podcasts, tweeting, YouTubing, these kinds of things, these are permissionless." (S001)
  - 2019-11: "the media airwaves are now crowded, so you need top-quality content and distribution" (S024)
  - 2024-10: "I just did it for me and it was deeply selfish it was just notes to self" (S033) and "if you're doing anything for followers your followers are worthless" (S033)
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: write human-facing work yourself. "If I want to create something that's meant to be consumed by a computer, I will use a computer." (S035)
- **Read:** Content is leverage and brand, but made for its own sake, and "you are only judged by your best work in posterity" (S033, 2024-10).

### 29. Paid ads and marketing
- **Stance:** He does not discuss buying ads. He refuses to carry ads on his own show, and for launches he prefers a viral demo video.
- **Evidence:** No ads on his show "because it would completely destroy the credibility of the enterprise." (S001, 2019-12); launch video over VC (S007, 2025-11, see topic 6); "If you’re optimizing your company to end up on the cover of a magazine, or to win an industry award, you’re failing." (S029, 2025-10); "the best social media people are not even hireable." (S007, 2025-11)
- **Read:** No recorded position on paid acquisition spend.

### 30. Brand and fame
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: "Build an independent brand around it, with your name, not a company’s name or other people’s names around it." (S016)
  - 2019-06: "the most powerful money makers are actually individual brands" (S017)
  - 2019-12: "It’s better to be anonymous and rich than to be poor and famous" (S001)
  - 2024-10: "it'd be better not to be famous to be honest if I had to do it over again it might be better to be anonymous" (S033)
  - **(newer)** 2025-03: "getting rich first. Um and then go for your fame afterwards would be my advice." (S030)
- **Read:** A personal brand is leverage. Fame for its own sake is a status game he regrets playing.

### 31. Sales
- **Stance:** Credibility over technique. If it feels like selling, you are selling the wrong thing.
- **Evidence:** "Credibility is way more important than sales." (S011, 2026-05); "If it feels to you like you’re selling, then you’re probably selling the wrong thing." (S011, 2026-05); "don't sell too hard because it'll just come unstuck later." (S032, 2026-01); "So, sales skills actually scale better over time." (S005, 2019-03)
- **Limit he names:** "But I’m not the guy you would talk to for making the sales quota, where you have to sell like 50 pieces of software this week or this month." (S011, 2026-05)

### 32. Deals, negotiation, and partnerships
- **Stance:** Walk from suboptimal deals, prefer aligned incentives over advantage, and play long-term games.
- **Evidence:** "I’ve just learned over the years that compromise is the enemy of building a great business." (S011, 2026-05); "I definitely lean on the side of walking away from suboptimal deals rather than doing something just to get it done." (S011, 2026-05); "When you do business deals, it’s better to have an aligned partnership where you both have the same incentives than a partnership where you have the advantage in the deal." (S001, 2019-12); "Negotiation is about not wanting it too badly." (S001, 2019-12)

### 33. Competition
- **Stance:** Do not watch competitors. Be authentic, and in a network effects market be number one or stay out.
- **Evidence:** "Generally, people will make the mistake of paying too much attention to the competition." (S001, 2019-12); "Just do your own thing. No one can compete with you on being you." (S001, 2019-12); "competition reduces profits to zero" (S007, 2025-11); "You want to be in a network effects business, assuming you’re not number two." (S001, 2019-12)

### 34. Moats
- **Over time:**
  - 2019-12: network effects, near-zero marginal cost, and scale economies, which "tend to go together." (S001)
  - 2025-08: "I think design is always a moat." (S034)
  - 2026-01: "technology has deeply winner take all network effects." (S032)
  - 2026-05: "Hardware, I think, is going to undergo a renaissance" (S009) and "So where’s the moat for a founder? Hardware? It’s a boon." (S012)
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: "hardware is the moat that buys you time to build a software castle" (S035)
- **Read:** As software got cheap, he moved the moat to hardware, network effects, design, and AI models.

### 35. Metrics and feedback
- **Stance:** Measure outputs, not hours, and trust feedback from markets and physics over people.
- **Evidence:** "The entire structure of rewarding people comes from the agricultural and industrial ages, when inputs and outputs matched up closely." (S001, 2019-12); "Physics is harsh: either your product worked, or it didn’t. Free markets are harsh: either people buy it, or they don’t." (S029, 2025-10); at seed, judgment is about people and potential more than "your ability to size up cashflows, customer acquisition costs or virality metrics" (S025, 2020-06)
- **Read:** No recorded position on specific operating metrics like CAC or churn.

### 36. Timing and hot markets
- **Stance:** Do not chase hot markets, be early, and expect a decade or more.
- **Evidence:** "Anyone in this business who’s chasing hot markets gets killed." (S025, 2020-06); "By the time you see conferences about it and read TechCrunch articles about it, it’s probably too late." (S025, 2020-06); "It will be gradual, and then it will be sudden." (S016, 2016-05); "there's a lot of luck required right place right time market forces timing" (S031, 2019-08); "It's not going to be two years. There's no get-rich quick schemes." (S032, 2026-01); in deep tech, "it's better to start early because when you're early your failures are more forgivable" (S034, 2025-08)

### 37. Selling the company
- **Stance:** As an investor, a team that looks likely to sell early is a red flag. As a founder, he sold a company that did not work and made investors whole.
- **Evidence:** "If a founding team hints, signals or even just appears likely to sell the company early, it’s a strong negative indicator." (S025, 2020-06); on Airchat, "It didn’t quite work, so we sold it off, got the investors their money back, and got the team some nice packages." (S006, 2026-04); on a 1999 stake, "Of course, I held on too long, and I lost the whole thing." (S016, 2016-05)
- **Read:** No recorded position on when to sell a company that is working. He tends to restart rather than scale: "I have a history of doing things once and then starting over because I get bored." (S011, 2026-05)

### 38. Advice from investors and experts
