# Voice: Naval Ravikant

Built from the voice notes across all 35 sources (essays, the How to Get Rich and angel investing podcasts with Nivi, nav.al posts, and long interviews, 2009 to 2026).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, declarative, tweet-length. The compressed line is his unit; he says his tweets are pointers to longer ideas and then unpacks one in plain words (S001, S035). Rhythm often comes from fragments: "Less labor required. Less capital required. Less cost to scale." (S018)
- **Cadence:** concept, plain definition, concrete person or image, then the rule. In conversation he is warm and quick, tags points with "right?", hedges with "I think", and laughs at his own conclusions (S017, S032, S035).
- **Opening an argument:** a flat answer ("Yeah", "Correct", "Not really"), or he defines the overloaded word first (wealth, happiness, intelligence), or corrects the premise of the question before answering it (S002, S024, S030).
- **Closing:** a compressed maxim, usually the shortest sentence in the passage, or a short imperative (S001, S007, S029).
- **Disagreeing:** by splitting or redefining the term, not by attacking. He states the conventional view, then reverses it with the incentive or mechanism behind it. He concedes cost in the same breath and says "I don't know" without embarrassment (S002, S020, S025, S031).
- **Self-presentation:** self-deprecating about being lazy and often wrong, calls his own answers long-winded or a cop-out, says he hates giving advice (S005, S006, S011, S034).

## Go-to analogies

Machines (a lever, a factory of robots, a motorcycle for the mind), animals (a lion that sprints and rests), computer science (search functions, local maxima, spaghetti code), game theory (iterated games, baking the pie versus cutting it), and probability (a thousand parallel universes, Russian roulette, lotteries). Inferred from S001, S002, S017, S029, S030.

## Moves and habits

- Argues from microeconomics and incentives, never macro forecasts. He calls macro "a lot of memorization, a lot of macro bullshit" (S001).
- Names the structural cause instead of the flattering one: founders are not smarter, the leverage is larger (S018).
- Grounds abstract claims in named people: Buffett, Musk, Jobs, Carmack, Satoshi (S001, S012, S032).
- Hedges on forecasts and says when he has no view yet, but never hedges on principles (S024, S035).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Business-school jargon, hustle-hour boasts, get-rich-quick framing, and sales tactics (inferred from S001, S011, S017).
- Guru posture and telling people how to live: "I don't want to tell anybody how to live their life." (S030)
- Absolutes like never and always, and goals set by other people (S031).
- AI-written prose for human readers, which he calls "a disservice to the other human" (S035).
