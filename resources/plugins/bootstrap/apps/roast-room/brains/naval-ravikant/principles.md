# Principles: Naval Ravikant

29 decision rules, built from the eight extract batches over 35 sources (2009 to 2026), weighted to leverage, equity, and wealth decisions. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Own a piece of the business; never plan to get rich on wages or fees.** "So everybody who really makes money at some point owns a piece of a product, or a business, or some kind of IP." (S001) "the first thing you have to do is you have to own a piece of a business" (S017) Sources: 5 (S001, S017, S024, S030, S032). Confidence: high.

2. **Use the least labor you can; prefer permissionless leverage (code and media).** "You really want to stay out of labor-based leverage." (S001) "Leverage is just force multiplier for your work." (S034) Sources: 4 (S001, S006, S018, S034). Confidence: high.

3. **What you work on and who you work with beat how hard you work.** "But hard work is no substitute for who you work with and what you work on." (S001) "if you choose the right thing to work on versus the wrong thing to work on, that’s an infinity difference." (S012) Sources: 4 (S001, S012, S017, S032). Confidence: high.

4. **Pick work where inputs do not match outputs, and pay for output.** "You really just want a job, or a career, or a profession where your inputs don’t match your outputs." (S001) "pay twice as much but but expect 10 times the output." (S034) Sources: 4 (S001, S003, S012, S034). Confidence: high.

5. **Spend time and capital only where 100x is possible; you only have to be right once.** "it only makes sense to do that for something that has that opportunity to be 100x or 1,000x on your time or on your capital." (S011) "But you only have to be right once." (S001) Sources: 6 (S001, S011, S024, S025, S030, S031). Confidence: high.

6. **Never risk ruin, and count reputation as part of ruin.** "The number one way people get ruined in modern business is not by betting too much; it’s by cutting corners and doing unethical or downright illegal things." (S001) "Generally, you don’t want to invest money that you’re not OK losing." (S025) Sources: 3 (S001, S025, S030). Confidence: high.

7. **Take the risk yourself and own the outcome; never push it onto others.** "Essentially, taking accountability for your actions is the same as taking an equity position in all of your work." (S001) "you be an entrepreneur, you go and you bear risk. Don't pass it off to other people or society." (S032) Sources: 4 (S001, S024, S029, S032). Confidence: high.

8. **Play long-term games with long-term people, and do not hop industries lightly.** "So, it’s important to pick an industry where you can play long-term games, and with long-term people." (S001) "every time you wander out of where you built your network, you’re going to be starting from scratch." (S004) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S016, S031). Confidence: high.

9. **If you cannot decide, the answer is no.** "if you can't decide, the answer is no." (S030) "hell yes right now then you should just be saying no" (S031) Sources: 3 (S016, S030, S031). Confidence: high.

10. **Walk away from deals that trap you, and keep your optionality.** "It is better not to be trapped in a bad deal." (S011) "whose money you’re investing is less relevant than on what terms it comes on" (S022) Sources: 3 (S001, S011, S022). Confidence: high.

11. **Do not fight over small stakes; put the energy into the big upside.** "Generally, I think people spend too much time splitting hairs on small stakes and not enough time focusing on the big upside." (S011) "In a longterm game, it’s positive sum. We’re all baking the pie together." (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S011). Confidence: medium.

12. **Pick partners for intelligence, energy, and integrity, and read integrity from actions.** "In terms of picking people to work with, pick ones that have high intelligence, high energy, and high integrity, I find that’s the three-part checklist that you cannot compromise on." (S001) "Seek integrity over niceness." (S025) Sources: 4 (S001, S007, S025, S031). Confidence: high.

13. **If you cannot see working with someone for life, do not work with them for a day.** "If you can’t see yourself working with someone for life, then don’t work with them for a day." (S001) Sources: 2 (S001, S016). Confidence: medium.

14. **Recruit personally, hire only geniuses, pay them like founders, and fire to keep the bar.** "So collect geniuses. Warehouse them. You’ll never regret it." (S007) "Your first two engineers? They’re just late founders. Treat them as such. Expect as much." (S019) Sources: 4 (S007, S019, S032, S034). Confidence: high. He is inconsistent on firing: "Yeah, I fire people much much faster." (S034, 2025-08-29) versus "Like I’m very bad at firing people." (S011, 2026-05-11), where he leans on co-founders.

15. **Keep the group as small as the job allows; add structure only when size forces it.** "So I just prefer keeping groups small." (S009) "So you want to be in the minimum-sized group to do the scope of the job that you set out to do." (S011) Sources: 4 (S007, S009, S011, S034). Confidence: high.

16. **A founder cannot outsource recruiting, fundraising, strategy, or product vision.** "I don’t think you can outsource any of those four." (S007) "It’s that holistic view of the whole product." (S029) Sources: 2 (S007, S029). Confidence: medium.

17. **Escape competition through authenticity; do not copy or watch rivals.** "Just do your own thing. No one can compete with you on being you." (S001) "Generally, people will make the mistake of paying too much attention to the competition." (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S007, S030). Confidence: high.

18. **Be number one in your category, or redefine the niche until you are.** "You want to be in a network effects business, assuming you’re not number two." (S001) "And remember: there is no demand for average." (S002) Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S032). Confidence: high.

19. **Guard your time like capital: set an absurd hourly rate and decline meetings ruthlessly.** "Guard your time. It’s all you have." (S016) "if you're working it better be for your hourly rate otherwise don't do the work" (S017) Sources: 5 (S001, S016, S017, S023, S030). Confidence: high.

20. **Raise from strength or not at all: early, on your own clock, and ideally self-funded.** "preferably six to 12 months before we need it." (S011) "most of the great companies are either self- financed or they break out very early before they really need venture capital." (S032) Sources: 3 (S007, S011, S032). Confidence: high. View change: from 2010 to 2019 he built VC infrastructure and taught angel investing (S021, S024); by 2025 to 2026 he says "I won’t take money from any VC." (S007) and burns his own money (S032). The newer view governs.

21. **Be non-consensus and right; the larger the herd you listen to, the worse you do.** "The larger the herd you listen to, the worse your returns will be." (S024) "In entrepreneurship, the masses are never right." (S001) Sources: 4 (S001, S024, S025, S031). Confidence: high.

22. **Learn by doing; test viability by trying it, not by debating it.** "The easiest way to figure out if something is viable or not is by doing it." (S001) "You have to do the things to learn the things. It's a mistake to just learn and not do." (S032) Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S011, S029, S032). Confidence: high.

23. **Trust feedback from markets and physics, not praise, awards, or groups.** "Feedback from other people is usually fake. Awards are fake. Critics are fake. Kudos from your friends and family are fake." (S029) "You can’t get good feedback from groups because groups are just trying to get along." (S029) Sources: 4 (S013, S029, S033, S034). Confidence: high.

24. **Sell only what you would buy; credibility beats sales technique.** "If it feels to you like you’re selling, then you’re probably selling the wrong thing." (S011) "I never have to worry about selling something that I wouldn't buy" (S031) Sources: 3 (S011, S031, S032). Confidence: high.

25. **Automate anything repetitive and buy the smartest model; your time costs more than tokens.** "No matter how expensive these models might seem, they’re still way cheaper than a human." (S012) "I will pay for more intelligence." (S035) Sources: 5 (S002, S003, S008, S012, S035). Confidence: high. Dated 2026; see frameworks.md entry 7 for the earlier AI views.

26. **Do work you enjoy a lot; burnout means change the work, not take a break.** "then you're going to lose because somebody who doesn't feel like it's work is competing with you" (S032) "Usually burnout is a sign you’re working on something that either isn’t working or you don’t enjoy the work fundamentally." (S007) Sources: 4 (S007, S029, S032, S034). Confidence: high.

27. **Get rich before chasing fame, and invest rather than consume.** "It’s better to be anonymous and rich than to be poor and famous" (S001) "I don't believe in consumption." (S030) Sources: 3 (S001, S017, S030). Confidence: high.

28. **Judge advice by the giver's incentives and results.** "Incentives are everything." (S016) "If anyone is giving advice on how to get rich and they’re also making money off of it, they should have made their money elsewhere." (S001) Sources: 4 (S001, S016, S024, S032). Confidence: high.

29. **Impatience with actions, patience with results; commit for about ten years or pass.** "Impatience with actions, patience with results." (S001) "this is not the thing for you right go find the thing you can commit to for 10 years" (S031) Sources: 4 (S001, S029, S031, S032). Confidence: high. He puts the wealth timeline at 10 to 30 years with no shortcuts (S032).
