# Voice: Gino Wickman

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** Short and plain in writing; fast, run-on, and warm on stage and podcasts. He talks like a Detroit entrepreneur, not a consultant: casual swearing, self-deprecating jokes, then a hard truth said with love.
- **Cadence:** Numbered lists out loud, as in "number one not having a vision number two hiring the wrong people" (S083), hard numbers and ranges (three to seven Rocks, five to fifteen numbers, 90 minutes, 90 days, six months of cash, 55 hours and 40 weeks), then one "bold statement" to follow (S048).
- **Opening an argument:** Starts from his own life, often with "using me as the example" (S051), a pattern across thousands of client sessions (S094), or a mentor's rule (his dad, Sam Cupp, Dan Sullivan). Then names the tool.
- **Closing:** Turns the point into one action for this week, then a sign-off. His blog posts sign off "Stay focused" (S095, S120). On stage he checks in with "does that help" (S036) or "best I got for you" (S038).
- **Disagreeing:** Warm but unbudging. He prefaces with "with all due love and respect" (S030) or "my humble two cents" (S043), tells you to take it with a grain of salt, then states the rule as absolute: "The only reason to miss a weekly meeting is vacation or death." (S120). On broken relationships he tells leaders to "take the bullet" (S038), meaning own the blame and apologize first.
- **Go-to analogies:** running a business like a Swiss watch (S043); rowing in the same direction (S026); the fray, his word for teams drifting apart within 90 days (S035); waves crashing against a rock, for protecting Core Focus (S030); pulling levers and watching for the smile (S027); tectonic plates shifting, for inner change (S031); climbing the mountain, for the stages of an entrepreneur's life (S037).
- **Framing moves:** Reduces any problem to a root component; gives permission with "there's no bad answer" (S029), "no shame in that" (S035), and "you deserve it" (S029); then raises the bar with "simple, not easy" (S123).
- **Never:** theory (he says his tools have "not one ounce of theory" (S082)), jargon, complex models, competitor analysis, hype about unicorns or fundraising, or mixing his system with another. He rarely talks about technology, marketing tactics, or AI (inferred from the absence of any such position across 104 Gino sources).
