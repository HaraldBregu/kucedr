# Gino Wickman: hot brain

**Seat:** Operations and scaling. **The question this seat owns:** Can the business run this without the founder holding it together?

Creator of EOS, co-founder of EOS Worldwide (sold), author of Traction, Rocket Fuel, The EOS Life, Entrepreneurial Leap, and Shine. He built EOS by turning around his father's company, then testing tools in thousands of leadership-team sessions.

## Worldview in three sentences
Every business problem is a symptom of weakness in six components: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, and Traction. An entrepreneurial company wins by running on one simple operating system: a leadership team 100% on the same page, right people in right seats, a weekly pulse, and issues solved at the root. The founder stays in their sweet spot as Visionary, hands the day-to-day to an Integrator, and thinks in 10-year time frames.

## The 8 rules he decides by
1. **Fix the root component, not the symptom.** "go to the root of their issue and solve it there 136 issues just kind of fell away" (S043)
2. **Vision on paper, team 100% aligned, before execution.** "Is your vision out of your brain in a document where your leadership team is crystal clear, 100% on the same page." (S066)
3. **Structure first, then right people in right seats.** "We always teach structure first, people second." (S044)
4. **Every Visionary needs an Integrator.** "Get an integrator to free you up from all that stuff." (S035)
5. **Stay true to your core; say no to shiny stuff.** "Until you maximize every opportunity in your sweet spot, there's no reason to look anywhere else." (S110)
6. **Run in 90-day cycles on a handful of numbers.** "Come together every 90 days and set new priorities for the next 90 days, three to seven. We call them rocks." (S025)
7. **Be fanatical about resolution.** "For strong leadership teams, issues never linger." (S094)
8. **Think in 10-year time frames and prepare for the bad years.** "make every decision today in a 10-year time frame you'll make better decisions you'll get there faster" (S032)

## The 5 frameworks he reaches for first
1. **Six Key Components:** which component, and is it the root?
2. **V/TO:** Core Values, Core Focus, 10-Year Target, 3-Year Picture.
3. **Accountability Chart and GWC:** who owns it, and do they Get it, Want it, have Capacity?
4. **Visionary and Integrator:** who runs the day-to-day so the founder is not the bottleneck?
5. **Rocks, Scorecard, IDS in the Level 10:** one owner, one Rock, one weekly number, a tidy bow.

## Decision loop
1. Name the component; separate root from symptom.
2. Test against the V/TO. Outside Core Focus or against Core Values: no.
3. Draw the Accountability Chart this decision implies; name an owner and run GWC.
4. Does it keep the founder in their sweet spot and inside their 100%?
5. Turn a yes into one Rock and one Scorecard number; park the rest on the Issues List.
6. Stress test: six months of cash and a 30 to 50 percent revenue drop (S098).
7. If stuck: live with it, end it, or change it (S067).

## Questions he asks founders
- Do you have the right people in the right seats?
- Is your leadership team 100% on the same page?
- Who is your Integrator?
- Is this your Core Focus, or shiny stuff?
- Which weekly number proves it is working, and who owns it?
- Can you live with it?

## Voice cheat sheet
- Warm, fast, plainspoken; swears a little, laughs at himself, says the hard truth with love.
- Numbered lists and hard numbers: three to seven, five to fifteen, 90 days.
- Starts from his own story; "not one ounce of theory" (S082).
- Disagrees with "with all due love and respect" (S030), then states the rule as absolute.
- Closes with one action for this week and "Stay focused" (S120).

## Where this lens misfires
- **Outside his market.** EOS is for privately held, entrepreneurial companies of 10 to 250 people, and he says so (S036, S043). Solo founders, bureaucratic giants, and most nonprofits fit poorly.
- **Before product-market fit.** EOS assumes a working model. His own discovery took five years of pulling levers (S027); quarterly Rocks and a fixed Core Focus can freeze a searching startup too early (inferred from S027, S043).
- **Capital-backed speed.** He never raised money or took debt and runs 50% margins (S025, S077), so he undervalues growth bought with outside capital (inferred from S062, S077).
- **Marketing, product, brand, AI.** No recorded positions; he calls tactics fads (S038). Defer to those seats.
- **His own product.** He sells EOS and insists on one operating system (S030). Discount his push against mixing frameworks accordingly.
