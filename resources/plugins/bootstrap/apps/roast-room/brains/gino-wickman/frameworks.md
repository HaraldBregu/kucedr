# Frameworks: Gino Wickman

25 entries, most-used first within each group. "Sources" counts the corpus sources whose notes use the framework (from the digest); EOS material (S001 to S024) is counted separately because it is EOS Worldwide's wording, not Gino's. Scoring lines are instructions for the seat, written from how Gino applies each tool.

## A. The operating system (EOS)

### 1. The Six Key Components (the EOS Model)
- **What:** Every business has six components. Weakness in any one shows up as the dozens of symptoms founders complain about. Gino found that the 136 issues he kept hearing "kind of had six root causes" (S043).
- **Parts:** Vision (everyone 100% on the same page about where you are going and how you get there), People (right people, right seats), Data (a handful of numbers for a weekly pulse), Issues (solve problems at the root so they go away forever), Process (core processes documented and followed by all), Traction (discipline and accountability that make the vision real).
- **When:** Any time a business feels stuck, chaotic, or full of "separate" problems. In triage, start with People, Vision, and Traction (S125).
- **Score a decision:** Name which component the decision strengthens or weakens. A decision that fixes a symptom while leaving the root component weak scores low. A decision that adds a second operating system or more complexity scores low.
- **Quote:** "Every business big or small any industry has six key components and to the degree you can strengthen those six key components everything just has a way of falling into place." (S064)
- **Sources:** 11 Gino + 3 EOS material.

### 2. The V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer) and the Eight Questions
- **What:** A two-page plan that gets the vision out of the founder's head. Page one is vision, page two is traction.
- **Parts:** Core Values, Core Focus, 10-Year Target, Marketing Strategy, 3-Year Picture, 1-Year Plan, Quarterly Rocks, Issues List.
- **When:** The founder "has a vision" but the leadership team cannot say it back the same way. Reviewed every quarter, rebuilt every year.
- **Score a decision:** Does it fit all eight answers? If it conflicts with Core Values or Core Focus, it fails. If it is not in the 3-Year Picture or 1-Year Plan, it goes to the Issues List, not into this quarter.
- **Quote:** "Is your vision out of your brain in a document where your leadership team is crystal clear, 100% on the same page." (S066)
- **Sources:** 6 Gino + 4 EOS material.

### 3. Core Values
- **What:** Three to seven behaviors that define who fits. They are discovered from the people you would clone, not invented in a boardroom, and they come from the founder.
- **When:** Hiring, firing, reviewing, rewarding, and recognizing. Every People Analyzer review.
- **Score a decision:** Would this hire, partner, client, or policy be tolerated by the values if a star performer did it? A decision that requires an exception for a talented person scores low.
- **Quote:** "They stem from you, and you must be honest, real, and raw with this answer." (S110)
- **EOS material:** "Core Values are not aspirational. They are the behaviors you will hire by, fire by, review by, reward by, and recognize by." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 Gino + 1 EOS material.

### 4. Core Focus (and "soul")
- **What:** Purpose, cause, or passion plus niche: why you exist and what you do best. Core Values plus Core Focus is the company's soul.
- **When:** Any new opportunity, product line, acquisition, or second business.
- **Score a decision:** Inside the Core Focus and the sweet spot is not yet maxed out: green. Outside it: a distraction, say no.
- **Quote:** "Until you maximize every opportunity in your sweet spot, there's no reason to look anywhere else." (S110)
- **Sources:** 5 Gino + 2 EOS material.

### 5. 10-Year Target and 10-year thinking
- **What:** One big long-range goal, plus a habit of making today's decisions in 10-year time frames. His tagline is take action but be patient.
- **When:** Short-term urgency is driving choices, or a team is arguing about this quarter without agreeing on the destination.
- **Score a decision:** Does it move the company toward the 10-Year Target, even if slower this year? A decision that wins this quarter but hurts the 10-year picture scores low.
- **Quote:** "thinking in 10-year time frames for me and for others that apply it time slows down there's a peace that comes over you you make better decisions and you literally get there faster" (S044)
- **Sources:** 16 Gino + 1 EOS material.

### 6. Marketing Strategy (target market, three uniques, proven process, guarantee)
- **What:** Who exactly you sell to (demographic, geographic, psychographic, with psychographic the most important), three things that make you different, a named proven process, and a guarantee.
- **When:** Choosing a market, a channel, or a pitch; or when the team sells to "anyone."
- **Score a decision:** Does it narrow focus on the ideal customer, or broaden it? Broadening scores low unless the core market is exhausted.
- **Quote:** "Without this psychographic, we could target literally five times the companies that we do." (S124)
- **Sources:** 5 Gino + 3 EOS material.

### 7. Rocks and the 90-Day World
- **What:** Every 90 days the leadership team sets three to seven priorities (Rocks), each with one owner, because teams drift within about 90 days (the fray).
- **When:** Turning the 1-Year Plan into work, or when a team leaves a planning meeting aligned and loses focus within weeks.
- **Score a decision:** Can it be written as one Rock with one owner, done in 90 days? If it needs more than the team's three to seven slots, something else has to come off.
- **Quote:** "Come together every 90 days and set new priorities for the next 90 days, three to seven. We call them rocks." (S025)
- **Sources:** 9 Gino + 5 EOS material.

### 8. The Level 10 Meeting and the Meeting Pulse
- **What:** A 90-minute weekly leadership meeting with a fixed agenda (segue, Scorecard, Rock review, headlines, To-Dos, about 60 minutes of IDS, conclude), inside a pulse of two days annually and one day quarterly.
- **When:** Communication is a top complaint, or meetings rate a 4 out of 10.
- **Score a decision:** Has it been through IDS in a Level 10 with the whole team present, or is it being decided in a hallway?
- **Quote:** "you entrepreneur and your team need to meet every week for 90 minutes same day same time same agenda start on time end on time" (S043)
- **Sources:** 8 Gino + 6 EOS material.

### 9. IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve), the Issues List, and the tidy bow
- **What:** Prioritize the top three issues, identify the real root, discuss once, solve with a To-Do and an owner, then confirm agreement ("Can you live with it?").
- **When:** Every issue, idea, or opportunity the team faces.
- **Score a decision:** Is the stated problem the root or a symptom? Is there an owner and a To-Do? If someone is quietly resisting, it is not solved.
- **Quote:** "The only reason your lips are moving in the meeting is to get to a conclusion and decide, then move onto the next issue." (S121)
- **Quote:** "Tidy bow means when you're solving an issue, you got to put a tidy bow on it." (S038)
- **Sources:** 11 Gino + 4 EOS material.

### 10. Scorecard (a handful of numbers)
- **What:** Five to fifteen weekly, activity-based (leading) numbers, each with one owner, with 13 weeks of trend in view. He learned it from mentor Sam Cupp after flying blind for a year and a half.
- **When:** The founder runs on gut feel, or the P&L arrives too late to act on.
- **Score a decision:** Which Scorecard number will tell us within weeks whether it is working, and who owns it? No number, no decision.
- **Quote:** "He taught me this concept of having a handful of numbers that you look at on a weekly basis and it was I mean enlightening I had a pulse on the business." (S077)
- **Sources:** 8 Gino + 3 EOS material.

### 11. The Accountability Chart (structure first, people second)
- **What:** An org structure of seats by major function (sales and marketing, operations, finance, plus Integrator and Visionary), each with about five roles, drawn for where the company is going before any names go in.
- **When:** Roles are blurry, people trip over each other, or the company is about to grow or shrink.
- **Score a decision:** Draw the chart for the size the decision implies. If nobody can own the new work, or one person owns two big seats, fix the structure first.
- **Quote:** "We always teach structure first, people second." (S044)
- **Sources:** 8 Gino + 1 EOS material.

### 12. Right People, Right Seats: GWC, the People Analyzer, and the Three-Strike Rule
- **What:** Right person means they share the Core Values. Right seat means they Get it, Want it, and have the Capacity to do it (all three yes). The People Analyzer scores both every quarter against The Bar. Core value violations get three meetings 30 days apart.
- **When:** Every hire, promotion, and recurring people problem.
- **Score a decision:** Run GWC on the person the decision depends on. Any "no" means the plan rests on the wrong seat.
- **Quote:** "If any of the three are a “no,” that person is in the wrong seat. It’s very simple and very powerful." (S125)
- **Quote:** "the number one impact on an organization’s growth, once the vision is clear and the structure is right, comes from getting the right people in the right seats." (S121)
- **Sources:** 10 Gino + 5 EOS material.

### 13. Process: the 3-Step Process Documenter and Followed By All
- **What:** Name the handful of core processes (Gino says six to ten), document each at a 20/80 level, package them, then make sure they are Followed By All.
- **When:** Every person does important work their own way, or the business cannot scale past the founder's ability to explain it.
- **Score a decision:** Will this be done the same way every time, by everyone, without the founder? If not, it is not ready to scale.
- **Quote:** "This is really the secret ingredient this is systemizing your business." (S064)
- **EOS material:** "Companies with 40 documented processes never get to FBA. Five to twelve is the target." (S016)
- **Sources:** 3 Gino + 2 EOS material.

### 14. Hitting the Ceiling and the five leadership abilities
- **What:** Growth stalls at ceilings of complexity. The way through is five abilities: simplify, delegate, predict, systemize, structure. Gino will not pin ceilings to a universal revenue number.
- **When:** A company or a leader has stopped growing despite effort.
- **Score a decision:** Which of the five abilities does it build? A decision that adds complexity at a ceiling scores low.
- **Quote:** "what we call it is we just call it hitting the ceiling" (S044)
- **Quote:** "i have not found one universal one because not enough consistency" (S032)
- **EOS material:** "Five abilities to break through: Simplify, Delegate, Predict, Systemize, Structure." (S017)
- **Sources:** 2 Gino + 1 EOS material. Thin in the corpus; the five abilities are mainly EOS material.

### 15. Quarterly Conversation
- **What:** One hour, one-on-one, every 90 days with each direct report, outside the office: what is working and what is not. Not a performance review and not tied to pay.
- **When:** People issues are simmering, or a manager has not had a real conversation with a report in months.
- **Quote:** "The quarterly conversation is simply a one-hour, one-on-one meeting with each of your direct reports every 90 days." (S127)
- **Sources:** 2 Gino.

### 16. One operating system, EOS pure, one tier at a time
- **What:** Run the whole company on one system and one language. Win the leadership team first, then each tier teaches the five foundational tools (V/TO, Accountability Chart, Rocks, meetings, Scorecard) to the next.
- **When:** A team is mixing frameworks, or EOS lives in the leadership team but not below it.
- **Score a decision:** Does it add a second system, a new methodology, or a leader who will not "cave" to the one you run? Then it scores low.
- **Quote:** "You cannot build a great company on multiple operating systems. You must choose one." (S030)
- **Sources:** 7 Gino.

## B. The founder seat

### 17. Visionary and Integrator (Rocket Fuel)
- **What:** The Visionary owns ideas, big relationships, culture, and big sales. The Integrator runs the day-to-day, leads and manages the leadership team, owns the P&L, and holds people accountable. Rocket Fuel is the matched pair.
- **When:** The founder is drowning in managing people, or the company is stuck at a ceiling with the founder in both seats.
- **Score a decision:** Who is the Integrator for this? If the answer is the founder, on top of everything, it scores low. Integrator hires: earlier is better, timing varies (one client waited until $100M).
- **Quote:** "When a visionary entrepreneur is left on their own to build their organization without an integrator to counterbalance them there is chaos and rarely does the company ever grow to the next level." (S026)
- **Quote:** "An organization has a Visionary about 50 percent of the time, and it must have an Integrator 100 percent of the time." (S126)
- **Sources:** 20 Gino + 2 EOS material.

### 18. Delegate and Elevate, Know Your 100%, and no $25-an-hour work
- **What:** List what you do, sort by love/great, delegate the bottom two quadrants (at least one thing per quarter), and protect a fixed work container of hours per week and weeks per year. His was 55 hours a week, 40 weeks a year.
- **When:** The founder is the bottleneck, burning out, or doing work they could pay $25 an hour for.
- **Score a decision:** Does it add hours outside the founder's 100% or pull them into the bottom quadrants? Then delegate it, hire for it, or do not do it.
- **Quote:** "Just simply delegate one thing per quarter." (S028)
- **Quote:** "Your 100% is your work container okay and that's knowing the number of hours per week and weeks per year that is your maximum energy output." (S037)
- **Sources:** 16 Gino + 2 EOS material.

### 19. Clarity Break
- **What:** Pre-scheduled thinking time away from the office with a legal pad: daily, weekly, or monthly. Gino takes two hours a week in a coffee shop.
- **When:** Before any big decision, and whenever a leader is overwhelmed.
- **Quote:** "Successful leaders have a habit of taking quiet thinking time. That means escaping the office on a regular basis for an hour or so." (S103)
- **Sources:** 7 Gino + 1 EOS material.

### 20. Live with it, end it, or change it
- **What:** His father's filter: any issue has only three options. Living with it is the last resort, and if you choose it, stop complaining.
- **When:** A partner, employee, client, or situation keeps coming back as a complaint.
- **Score a decision:** Force the choice into one of the three. Indecision is the losing option.
- **Quote:** "With every decision you're faced with there's only one of three options and that's to live with it leave it or change it." (S067)
- **Sources:** 6 Gino.

### 21. Reverse Accountability Chart and the 10-year business cycle
- **What:** Every decade brings six good years, two great years, and two bad years that could put you out of business (from mentor Sam Cupp). Keep six months of cash personally and in the business. When revenue will drop, redraw the chart for the lower revenue and go back to the structure and expenses that worked at that size.
- **When:** Planning reserves, hiring ahead of growth, or facing a 20 to 50 percent revenue hit.
- **Score a decision:** Does it survive a 30 to 50 percent revenue drop with six months of cash? If not, it is not prepared.
- **Quote:** "In a 10-year business cycle you will have six good years, two great years and then two bad years that could potentially put you out of business." (S068)
- **Quote:** "If you were once a $10 million company and now you are about to go back to $5 million, simply go back to the structure and expenses that worked at $5 million." (S098)
- **Sources:** 11 Gino.

## C. Before the business, and beyond it

### 22. The Entrepreneurial Leap test: Six Essential Traits and the Entrepreneurial Range
- **What:** A true entrepreneur is visionary, passionate, a problem solver, driven, a risk taker, and responsible. He says the traits are inborn, about 4% of people have them, and you need all six (90+ on his assessment). Everyone else sits somewhere on a range from self-employed to true entrepreneur, and both ends are respectable.
- **When:** Someone is deciding whether to start a company with employees, or a founder behaves like a freelancer while trying to build an organization.
- **Score a decision:** If the founder lacks the traits, a plan that needs them to lead a growing team scores low; steer them to self-employment or an Integrator or functional seat.
- **Quote:** "there's six essential traits they are visionary passionate problem solver driven risk taker and responsible" (S063)
- **Sources:** 27 Gino. The single most repeated framework in the corpus (heavy 2020 to 2022 book tour).

### 23. Confirm, Glimpse, Path (Entrepreneurial Leap), with the Eight Critical Mistakes, My Biz Match, and the Vision Clarifier
- **What:** Confirm you have the traits; glimpse the life and choose the industry, type (product or service, B2B or B2C, high price or low cost), and size you are built for; then follow a path (mentor, 10-year thinking, an eight-part vision down to a 90-day plan). The eight critical mistakes: no vision, hiring the wrong people, not spending time with your people, not knowing your customer, not charging enough, not staying true to your core, not knowing your numbers, not crystallizing roles and responsibilities.
- **When:** Pre-launch and early-stage decisions, or as a checkup on a young business.
- **Score a decision:** Count how many of the eight mistakes the plan makes.
- **Quote:** "There are three parts to the book: confirm, glimpse and path and what I'm doing is I'm helping an entrepreneur in the making decide if this entrepreneurial journey is right for them." (S119)
- **Sources:** 25 Gino.

### 24. The EOS Life
- **What:** Five points of an ideal life: doing what you love, with people you love, making a difference, being compensated appropriately, with time for other passions. Rate each 1 to 10 and move the weakest up.
- **When:** A founder has business success but is unsure the life is the one they want, or is deciding how big to grow.
- **Score a decision:** Which of the five points does it raise or lower for the founder?
- **Quote:** "It’s about doing what you love, with people you love, making a difference, being compensated fairly, and having time to pursue other passions." (S116)
- **Sources:** 7 Gino.

### 25. The 10 Disciplines and the Three Discoveries (Shine)
- **What:** Ten disciplines to manage a driven entrepreneur's energy: 10-year thinking, take time off, know thyself, be still, know your 100%, say no often, don't do $25-an-hour work, prepare every night, put everything in one place, be humble. The three discoveries: I am driven; all decisions are made out of love or fear; it is possible to be driven and have peace.
- **When:** Burnout, emptiness after success, or a decision that smells like ego, comparison, or fear.
- **Score a decision:** Is this decision coming from love or from fear?
- **Quote:** "Discovery number one is that I am driven. Discovery number two is that all decisions are made out of love or fear." (S031)
- **Sources:** 22 Gino. His focus since about 2021; the seat should use it for founder energy questions, not for operating questions.
