# Gary Vaynerchuk: frameworks

Ordered by how often he reaches for them. Source counts are the sources in this corpus where the framework appears, named or unnamed. Dates matter: platform-level parts age within months; the frameworks themselves have held since about 2011.

---

## 1. Day Trading Attention

- **What it is:** The core skill of studying what people pay attention to, where, what it costs, and where it is underpriced, then using it for brand and sales. Named in his 2020 posts and the 2024 47-page deck, practiced since the 1990s.
- **Parts:** Understand attention; find where it is; find where it is underpriced; leverage it for brand and sales; re-check constantly because it shifts daily.
- **When to use:** Any channel, budget, or launch decision.
- **How to score a decision:** (a) Is the target customer's attention actually consumed there today, not "potential reach"? (b) Is its price below its value versus the default channels? (c) Can we make creative native to it? Three yeses means go hard now; any no means you are buying yesterday's attention.
- **Quote:** "Eyes and ears are the only thing you should give a fuck about. And wherever they are matters." [S078, 2016]
- **Sources:** 7 (S011, S055, S064, S078, S096, S099, S145).

## 2. Underpriced attention (attention arbitrage)

- **What it is:** Attention is priced like real estate; new or under-positioned platforms deliver more attention than they cost until the crowd arrives and bids it up. Then you move.
- **Parts:** Spot where attention is growing faster than ad prices; go early, even before ROI is visible; go all in while it is cheap; squeeze it fully; leave unemotionally when it saturates.
- **When to use:** Picking channels, influencers, ad buys, or new markets.
- **How to score:** Ask what 90% of people would do with the same budget (the bottled water test, S097). If your plan matches the crowd, it is probably overpriced. Price per actually consumed second of attention beats CPM.
- **Quote:** "Underpriced attention lies in platforms and areas where most people who make advertising decisions don’t fully understand yet." [S097, 2020]
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S010, S013, S015, S018, S041, S054, S072, S075, S079, S097, S107, S113, S137). Examples he gives across time: email 1997, Google AdWords at five cents (2000s), YouTube 2006, Twitter 2007, Facebook ads 2013 to 2019, Instagram, LinkedIn organic 2019, TikTok 2018 to 2020, Snapchat Spotlight and live shopping 2025 to 2026.

## 3. The GaryVee Content Model (reverse pyramid, pillar to micro)

- **What it is:** One long-form pillar piece (vlog, keynote, Q&A show, podcast, interview) is cut by a team into dozens of platform-native micro pieces that drive back to the pillar; comments decide the second round.
- **Parts:** Document or create the pillar; repurpose best moments into clips, quotes, memes, articles, stories, GIFs; distribute natively on every relevant platform; listen to comments (ask for timestamps); make community-driven second-round micro content; distribute again.
- **When to use:** Any founder or brand that needs volume without inventing 30 ideas a day.
- **How to score:** Count pieces per pillar and platforms covered. One keynote became 30+ pieces and 35M views (S144). If a pillar yields fewer than a dozen native pieces, the machine is not running.
- **Quote:** "allow my team to analyze and repurpose it into 30 other pieces of content that are designed to over index on the platforms they are distributed to." [S144, 2019]
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S027, S038, S112, S144, S085).

## 4. Document, Don't Create

- **What it is:** Capture what you already do, think, and learn instead of inventing polished content or a persona. Frame it as your journey, not instruction.
- **Parts:** Pick up the phone and record real work; talk from "my intuition says," not "you should"; don't front or oversell; post at high volume for years; if you can't create today, facilitate others' stories.
- **When to use:** Founder says "no time," "not creative," or "nothing to say."
- **How to score:** Is the content a byproduct of work already happening? If producing it requires a separate shoot or a script, it is creating, and it will not survive at volume.
- **Quote:** "It's my paramount thesis of no matter who you are or what you do, your job is to tell your story and document vs create is the way to get there." [S142, 2018]
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S005, S022, S032, S037, S040, S051, S080, S085, S119, S142, S143).

## 5. Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook

- **What it is:** Give value repeatedly (jabs) to earn the right to ask (right hook). His book of the same name (2013); he later clarified that jabs earn the chance to ask, not a guaranteed conversion, and that social must also be used for direct response.
- **Parts:** Jabs: free value with nothing behind it; right hook: a firm, honest, frictionless ask; tone of hook matches jabs; mix ratio so the audience does not learn to duck.
- **When to use:** Setting a content mix, an email cadence, or a launch.
- **How to score:** Audit the last 15 posts: what share is pure selling? If most are hooks, conversions fall. If a hook fails, check whether the jabs laddered up to it (S123).
- **Quote:** "if you're trying to sell and throw a right hook all the time, eventually the other person knows how to duck" [S011, 2020]
- **Sources:** 14 (S004, S008, S009, S011, S033, S045, S047, S050, S051, S076, S121, S122, S123, S137).

## 6. Organic mid-funnel to Brandformance (validate, then pay)

- **What it is:** His 2022 to 2026 marketing model. Organic social is the testing lab ("mid-funnel"). Pieces that over-index get a call to action and paid media (down-funnel, "brandformance"), or become the brief for TV and big campaigns (up-funnel).
- **Parts:** Define 20 to 30 cohorts; post across 7 to 10 platforms; compare views to baseline (for example 47,000 versus a normal 500); add performance elements (free shipping, CTA) lightly; run media only on validated creative.
- **When to use:** Any paid media budget decision.
- **How to score:** Did this creative earn its media dollars organically? If not, do not spend. Working media should amplify good creative, not hide bad creative.
- **Quote:** "when there's been true validation of creative, the creative has earned the right to get working media dollars." [S043, 2026]
- **Sources:** 12 (S019, S026, S041, S043, S045, S046, S054, S057, S062, S076, S080, S145). **Changed over time:** in 2018 he told a seller to put 20% into organic and 80% into ads (S010); by 2025 he says only organic winners get media.

## 7. Modern Advertising Framework (from the Day Trading Attention deck)

- **What it is:** Six variables for winning in an interest-graph world (S145, 2024).
- **Parts:** Cohort development; PAC (Platforms and Culture, later Platforms, Algorithms, Culture); SOC (Strategic Organic Content); Amplification (brandformance); videos people want to watch (modern commercials); Post-Creative Strategy (read comments to decide what to make next), plus CasC (comments as creative).
- **When to use:** Building or rebuilding a marketing team or content system.
- **How to score:** Which of the six is missing? Most companies have amplification and nothing else.
- **Quote:** "We have an acronym internally at Vayner called sock strategic organic content and all day long I just walk around on slack email and in the halls and say it's the s in sock that everything lives and dies by here" [S026, 2022]
- **Sources:** 10 (S026, S035, S037, S043, S071, S080, S083, S099, S100, S145).

## 8. Baskin Robbins, not vanilla (context over consistency)

- **What it is:** Make many contextual versions of a message for narrow cohorts and each platform's mindset instead of one vanilla asset everywhere. "Content is king, but context is God."
- **Parts:** Pick narrow segments (job title, city, team, life stage); reference them in the first second; adjust to platform psychology (LinkedIn business mindset, Instagram entertainment); respect the audience, respect the platform, put your agenda third.
- **When to use:** Whenever someone wants to post one video everywhere or protect "brand consistency."
- **How to score:** Number of cohort-specific variants and native edits per message. One asset posted identically on five platforms scores zero.
- **Quote:** "This is not television where you make a piece of vanilla and you hope that everybody on Earth wants it this is Baskin Robbins and we need to make a lot of different flavors" [S026, 2022]
- **Sources:** 9 (S008, S026, S032, S033, S095, S104, S113, S122, S145).

## 9. The 79/21 rule (and 70/20/10)

- **What it is:** Diversify attention: about 80% on the platform with the most attention now, about 20% tasting rising ones. Rotate as attention moves. A budget version: move most spend to underpriced social, keep a slice for weird new things.
- **Parts:** Rank platforms by current value; go all in on the top; keep a real presence on emerging ones; promote a platform from the 20% to the 80% when it rises.
- **When to use:** Channel mix; especially when one channel is working and you feel complacent.
- **How to score:** What share of revenue rides on one channel? He warns against any single source carrying the business (S008, 2019: 63% of business from one search term, and then "the whole thing breaks").
- **Quote:** "I spend about 80% of my time on the hottest platform, go as hard as I possibly can on it, and spend 20% of my time getting a feel for everything else." [S110, 2019]
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S105, S110, S021).

## 10. Interest media (interest graph over social graph)

- **What it is:** Since about 2020, feeds serve content by what you are interested in, not who you follow. Followers matter less; each piece of creative earns its own reach.
- **Parts:** No follower base needed; volume and variation of hooks and thumbnails; new handles for new topics; the grid matters little.
- **When to use:** When someone says they have no audience, or debates brand grid consistency.
- **How to score:** Measure views per piece and per month across platforms, not followers.
- **Quote:** "Because we live in interest media, not social media, you do not need followers to get views." [S043, 2026]
- **Sources:** 12 (S019, S023, S038, S041, S043, S045, S046, S047, S048, S054, S062, S064). He traces the idea to his early Tumblr bet (S026, S054).

## 11. Clouds and Dirt

- **What it is:** Invest only in the extremes: the clouds (vision, philosophy, the why) and the dirt (hands-on craft, the how). Ignore the middle: politics, process, minutiae.
- **Parts:** Clouds: big belief and end goal; dirt: work ethic and practitioner experience; skip the middle meetings.
- **When to use:** A leader or team that is busy but not moving, or a successful founder coasting on reputation.
- **How to score:** How many hours this week were spent in the middle (status meetings, politics) versus vision or hands-on work?
- **Quote:** "Know the philosophy, know the details, and ignore everything in the middle." [S124, 2016]
- **Sources:** 4 (S020, S023, S124, S134). Related: "I don't play in the middle" (S073, S048).

## 12. Macro patience, micro speed

- **What it is:** Be fast every day and patient over years. Most people do the reverse: rush the outcome and dawdle on the day.
- **Parts:** Micro: respond fast, fill every minute, ship; macro: expect 6 to 10 years, think in 20 to 40 year terms; no arbitrary age deadlines.
- **When to use:** Founder impatient for results, or tempted by a shortcut that trades long-term upside.
- **How to score:** Is the decision optimizing the next 90 days at the expense of the next 10 years? Is daily execution as fast as it can be?
- **Quote:** "Micro speed, macro patience are the only thing that matters, because opinions predicated on people’s insecurities and ego just slow things down." [S118, 2017]
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S017, S088, S089, S118, S037).

## 13. Media company first (Wine Spectator, not QVC)

- **What it is:** Every business is a media company first, then whatever it sells. Content should replace the trade magazine of your industry, not be a disguised commercial.
- **Parts:** Hire editorial talent; publish information and entertainment, not commercials; serve the ecosystem (praise others, educate); sell openly elsewhere; distribute by buying ads or hacking culture.
- **When to use:** A brand that posts only promotions, or treats content as a cost center.
- **How to score:** Would the audience consume this if it carried no logo? Is any of it about the industry rather than the product?
- **Quote:** "intuitively I needed to become the Wine Spectator not QVC and it changed the course of my life" [S034, 2019]
- **Sources:** 10 (S006, S010, S034, S038, S046, S079, S106, S108, S119, S122).

## 14. The Thank You Economy and scaling the unscalable

- **What it is:** Win retention and word of mouth with human, one-to-one gestures at scale: reply to every comment and DM, thank existing customers, surprise and delight. His 2011 book.
- **Parts:** Reply to everyone early (he did on Twitter 2007 to 2011); call every new customer just to say thanks; map customers' public interests and send a specific gift (the Jay Cutler jersey); reach out to past clients with no pitch.
- **When to use:** Retention, soft demand, local businesses, or a small business competing with a Goliath.
- **How to score:** Who owns retention and lifetime value? How many past customers were contacted this month with no ask?
- **Quote:** "the thank you department is playing offense customer service on social media is playing defense" [S077, 2011]
- **Sources:** 8 (S010, S015, S019, S037, S045, S064, S075, S077).

## 15. Kind Candor

- **What it is:** His rebrand of candor: honest critical feedback delivered early, privately, and with care. Built after he admitted his biggest leadership weakness was praising people Friday and firing them Monday.
- **Parts:** Tell the truth early; deliver it humanely; intent is to let the person fix it; if it is not fixed, let them go without surprise; publicly reward people who tell the leader the truth.
- **When to use:** Underperformers, especially people you like.
- **How to score:** Would this person be surprised if fired today? If yes, you owe them candor now.
- **Quote:** "There’s a reason why I added the word kind in front of candor. How you deliver the medicine matters." [S086, 2021]
- **Sources:** 9 (S035, S039, S058, S063, S065, S070, S081, S086, S090). **Changed over time:** "That cander is good. I used to think it was bad." [S081, 2026]

## 16. Practical optimism (maybe, skewing toward yes; the cynicism tax)

- **What it is:** Meet new platforms, technologies, and opportunities with "maybe," then do the work to test it. Saying no before testing is a cynicism tax. Optimism is not delusion; the market's verdict is the check.
- **Parts:** Notice the reflexive no; change it to maybe; spend 5 to 50 hours of real homework; accept wasted time on failed hypotheses; quit if reps show it is not working.
- **When to use:** Any new channel or technology (AI, live shopping, a new platform) that the team wants to dismiss.
- **How to score:** Has anyone on the team personally used it for 20 hours of homework and 50 hours of doing (S030)? If not, the no is not earned.
- **Quote:** "When you're in a practical optimism framework you're saying maybe to everything." [S044, 2026]
- **Sources:** 9 (S030, S044, S049, S050, S052, S056, S068, S071, S150).

## 17. Self-awareness: triple down on strengths, hire around weaknesses

- **What it is:** Know what you are actually good at and like, invest disproportionately there, lift fatal weaknesses only to "not fatal," and hire the rest.
- **Parts:** Likes, dislikes (laziness as signal), and market facts; the self-awareness dinner with five people closest to you; staff around gaps; choose your content format (written, audio, video) by strength.
- **When to use:** Founder role design, first hires, choosing a content format.
- **How to score:** Is the founder spending most hours in their top 3 to 5 strengths? Is a weakness being "fixed" that could be hired?
- **Quote:** "I believe that the entrepreneurs that have gone on to quadruple down on their strengths and then hire around their weaknesses have had much better success than the ones that dwell on their shortcomings" [S012, 2019]
- **Sources:** 10 (S006, S012, S013, S014, S029, S034, S067, S091, S128, S139).

## 18. Cash is oxygen (three things when starting up)

- **What it is:** Sales and cash flow come first; your strength becomes business DNA second; white space third.
- **Parts:** Get to the first check before planning four years out; build sales into the company from day one; double down on the one or two things you do best; find the channel or story nobody in your sector is using.
- **When to use:** Prioritization in an early or small business; deciding whether to raise money.
- **How to score:** Does this decision help cash in the next months? If not, does the business have enough cash to afford it?
- **Quote:** "Cash is what allows you to do everything else. Cash is oxygen." [S128, 2015]
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S128, S141, S116).

## 19. The barbell (extreme tech and extreme analog)

- **What it is:** His 2025 to 2026 view: the world splits into extreme technology (AI, AR, agents) and extreme real life (events, pop-ups, running clubs, collectibles). Nothing wins in the middle; ambitious people play both ends.
- **Parts:** Become a hands-on AI practitioner; build human, in-person, surprise-and-delight programs; film every analog event as a content production day.
- **When to use:** Strategy in the AI era; brand defense against AI content.
- **How to score:** What is the plan on each end? Doing neither is not an option (S045).
- **Quote:** "The world is about to be a barbell. We're going extreme digital, AI, VR is not far away, and AR." [S047, 2026]
- **Sources:** 6 (S045, S047, S048, S071, S073, S069).

## 20. Jockey and horse

- **What it is:** His investing filter. Judge the operator (jockey) and the business (horse); he lost money when he fell for ideas he imagined running himself.
- **Parts:** Jockey: emotional intelligence, kindness, stomach for the game, operator DNA; horse: thesis he personally believes; need both.
- **When to use:** Angel bets, partnerships, acquisitions, backing a founder.
- **How to score:** Two for two or pass. Separate charity (rooting for the person) from the thesis.
- **Quote:** "I'm looking for the jockey and the horse now. The jockey being the entrepreneur, the horse being the business. I need to like both and understand both." [S075, 2025]
- **Sources:** 6 (S035, S046, S072, S075, S076, S092).

## 21. The steak and side dishes (and the cookie jar rule)

- **What it is:** One main business gets about 80% of effort; side projects stay small enough that losses can be absorbed. If overwhelmed for months (not a week), pull your hand out of some cookie jars.
- **Parts:** Perfect the steak; keep side dishes small; promote a side dish when it outgrows the steak; check prior yeses before a new yes (power of no).
- **When to use:** A founder juggling several ventures.
- **How to score:** Is there a steak? "Too many people have all side dishes, and no steak." [S004, 2017]
- **Quote:** "I call it the meat, the main dish. You can go and have side dishes, and if your meat, if your steak is perfect, you'll always be able to absorb the losses, because they're smaller losses." [S004, 2017]
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S031, S059, S060, S127). **Tension:** he personally runs many businesses and says he would earn more with fewer but enjoys it more (S049, S051, S070).

## 22. 10/80/10 delegation (also stated as 15/80/5)

- **What it is:** The leader shapes the first slice of an important project, disappears for the middle, and closes the last slice.
- **Parts:** Open (mold the strategy); team owns the middle; close (cherry on top).
- **When to use:** Founder bottlenecking projects or sitting in too many middle meetings.
- **How to score:** How much founder time sits in the middle of projects?
- **Quote:** "I open and close. The middle is the problem." [S075, 2025]
- **Sources:** 2 (S038, S075).

## 23. Hire fast, fire fast (judge the judgers)

- **What it is:** Hiring is guessing; firing is knowing. Move fast on both, and weight feedback only from leaders whose judgment you trust.
- **Parts:** Hire on intuition and personality (A+ personality, C skills is fine); audit from day one; fire within months when you know, humanely and without surprise; keep a 30-day out in agency contracts.
- **When to use:** Hiring and performance decisions.
- **How to score:** How long has a known bad fit stayed? He kept one four months after knowing in a day (S014).
- **Quote:** "Hiring isn't what you should be caught up on. It's firing when you know." [S060, 2026]
- **Sources:** 5 (S014, S035, S060, S072, S075).

## 24. Twelve and a Half (emotional ingredients)

- **What it is:** His 2021 book: 12 emotional ingredients plus the "half" (your weakness). Soft skills decide business outcomes as hard skills get commoditized.
- **Parts:** Gratitude, self-awareness, accountability, optimism, empathy, kindness, tenacity, curiosity, patience, conviction, humility, ambition, plus kind candor as his own half.
- **When to use:** Diagnosing why a capable founder stalls; leadership culture.
- **How to score:** Which ingredient is the founder's half, and is it named publicly?
- **Quote:** "It’s about mastering soft skills, identifying your weaknesses or “halves,” and boosting your EQ, aka your emotional quotient or intelligence." [S152, 2021]
- **Sources:** 14 (S086 to S091, S146 to S152, S037).
