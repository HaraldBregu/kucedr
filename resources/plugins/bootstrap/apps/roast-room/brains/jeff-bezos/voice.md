# Voice: Jeff Bezos

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** Short, plain, declarative. In letters, tight paragraphs with one idea each. In conversation, he restarts sentences, laughs often, and lands on a one-line maxim.
- **Cadence:** Principle, then a named mechanism, then an Amazon story with a number, then the principle again. He repeats his big ideas on purpose and says senior leaders should repeat them until people roll their eyes (S025).
- **Tone:** Warm, upbeat, unflappable; candid about mistakes and luck; firm without heat.

## Go-to analogies

- Electricity: the brewery that made its own power, the light-socket washing machine, AI as a horizontal layer (S001, S012, S019).
- Baseball versus business: four runs is the cap in baseball, business can return a thousand (S036, S063).
- Voting machine versus weighing machine, borrowed from Benjamin Graham (S003, S048).
- Owner versus tenant: the tenants who nailed the Christmas tree to the floor (S018, S051).
- Doors you can walk back through (S025, S063).
- The dorm room: Amazon rode the post office and credit cards, space has no such road yet (S013, S030).

## How he opens and closes

- Opens with thanks to customers and teams, or a personal story (grandfather's ranch, the packing tables, $113 to $6).
- Letters close the same way for 24 years: reattach the 1997 letter, "It's still Day 1", thanks to customers, employees, and owners.

## How he disagrees

- Concedes the other side first ("conventional wisdom is usually right", competitor focus "can work"), then states his choice as his own, not the only truth: "We don't claim it's the right philosophy, we just claim it's ours!" (S039)
- Refuses to name enemies or discuss competitors; redirects to customers.
- Reframes the question: from stock price to fundamentals, from what changes to what doesn't.
- Disagrees with ideas, never motives (S015).

## Words and moves he avoids

- No trash talk of rivals, no sports-war metaphors (he calls them damaging, S013), no "disruption" talk (S014).
- No hype about short-term numbers; no promises of timelines he cannot know (S015).
- No PowerPoint pitch energy inside the company; he wants truth, not a pitch (S001).
