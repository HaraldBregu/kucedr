# Positions: Jeff Bezos

40 founder topics. Each has his stance, a verbatim quote, the source id, and the date (see `sources.md`). Where his view moved over time, both ends are shown with dates. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; do not invent one.

## Money and pricing

**1. Pricing.** Lower prices as scale and efficiency allow, even when short-term math says raise them; maximize dollar margin, not percentage.
- "just because you could raise prices doesn't mean you should" (S009, 2000)
- "When we lower prices, we go against the math that we can do, which always says that the smart move is to raise prices." (S053, 2006)

**2. Discounting and promotions.** Against narrow, temporary discounts. Low prices should be everyday and across the whole range.
- "Our pricing objective is not to discount a small number of products for a limited period of time, but to offer low prices everyday and apply them broadly across our entire product range." (S050, 2003)

**3. Growth versus profitability.** His stance moved with the stage of the company.
- 1998: "At this stage, we choose to prioritize growth because we believe that scale is central to achieving the potential of our business model." (S038, 1998)
- 2000, looking back on 1995: "we were profitable in December of 1995 6 months after opening and that was the worst mistake" (S009, 2000)
- 2001, after the bust: "we've set the goal of achieving a pro forma operating profit in the fourth quarter." (S048, 2001)
- 2002: growth first, then cost, then both once affordable: "we could afford to balance growth and cost improvement, dedicating resources and staffed projects to both." (S049, 2002)

**4. Get big fast and the land rush.** Changed. Right in 1998, retired by 2001.
- "we would adopt a strategy of get big fast so that we could eventually level the playing field in terms of purchasing power" (S004, 1998)
- "that metaphor was an extraordinarily useful decision aid for several years starting in 1994, but we now believe its usefulness largely faded away over the last couple of years." (S048, 2001)

**5. Financial metric that matters.** Free cash flow per share, not earnings, EPS, or EBITDA.
- "EBITDA isn't cash flow." (S052, 2005)

## Capital

**6. Raising early money.** It should be hard; tell backers the honest odds.
- "It's supposed to be hard to raise a million dollars." (S043, 2001)
- "I would always tell people I thought there was a 70% chance they would lose their investment." (S001, 2024)

**7. When to take outside investors.** Only once you are sure it is a good investment for them. Changed for Blue Origin: self-funded in 2018, raising outside money by 2026.
- 2018: "I am liquidating about $1 billion a year of Amazon stock to fund Blue Origin." (S047, 2018)
- 2026: "it's a good time actually to start thinking about the future and bring on some other outside investors." (S033, 2026) He adds that he waited until he saw Blue's financial future with clarity (S006, 2026).

**8. Choosing investors.** Seek long-term owners and say plainly what kind of company you are.
- "this is a company which is inappropriate for short-term investors" (S027, 2001)
- "we meet with investors who have low portfolio turns" (S018, 2014)

**9. Debt and dilution.** Limited evidence. Cares about share count more than leverage; raised and later repaid convertible debt.
- "Efficiently managing share count means more cash flow per share and more long-term value for owners." (S052, 2005)

**10. Bet-the-company risk.** Never.
- "I don't believe in bet the company bets that's when you're desperate" (S018, 2014)

## People

**11. Hiring bar and first hires.** The bar is the most important element; hire the long-lead technical leader early; test whether candidates can hire well.
- "when I interview somebody I actually spend about a third of the interview asking questions designed to ascertain whether or not they can hire great people" (S004, 1998)
- "We want to fight entropy. The bar has to continuously go up." (S039, 1999)

**12. Hiring friends.** Depends on your age.
- "When you're under 40, never hire your friends. When you're over 40, only hire your friends." (S007, 2026)

**13. Firing and exits.** Exit leaders who blame instead of fixing root causes; offer pay-to-quit so people stay for the right reasons; cut by data but protect the core.
- On executives who blame instead of fixing: "all they did is point fingers and blame people, we would exit them quickly." (S006, 2026)
- "Don't follow the data on investigative reporting." (S033, 2026)

**14. Equity and pay.** Weight pay to stock so people think like owners; as a major owner he took no equity comp.
- "Each of whom must think like, and therefore must actually be, an owner." (S038, 1998)
- "I asked the comp committee of the board not to give me any comp." (S001, 2024)

**15. Culture.** Set it early; it is discovered more than written, and it self-reinforces through self-selection.
- "You can write down your corporate culture, but when you do so, you're discovering it, uncovering it - not creating it." (S063, 2016)

**16. Work hours and burnout.** Long hours are fine; burnout means something is wrong with the work, not the hours. Protect sleep for decision quality.
- "60 hours a week that's completely sustainable for most people" (S025, 2018)
- "As a senior executive, you get paid to make a small number of high-quality decisions." (S044, 2018)

**17. Work-life balance.** Rejects "balance" for "harmony".
- "Reject balance. Seek harmony. Balance implies a tradeoff." (S024, 2025)

**18. Remote work.** No recorded position. (He chose an urban headquarters over the suburbs to keep Amazon vibrant and attract talent (S061, 2014); any view on remote work would be inference.)

## Strategy and focus

**19. Focus versus diversification.** Small startups: as narrow as possible. Large companies: diversify if one cultural thread runs through everything.
- "be as narrow and as focused as is possible to be" (S018, 2014)
- "We could have stuck to the knitting. I'm glad we didn't. Or did we? Maybe the knitting has as much to do with our approach as the arena." (S063, 2016)

**20. Side projects and new ventures.** No direct statement on founder side projects. Closest: a big new idea deserves its own company and full focus, and he left the CEO role partly because it consumed all attention.
- "It deserves its own special focus." (S033, 2026)

**21. Niche.** Start narrow with the "first best product"; in content, be someone's favorite rather than everyone's third favorite.
- "On streaming services like like amazon prime you really want to be somebody's favorite show." (S030, 2016)

**22. Entering a crowded market.** Only with a differentiated, delightful offer. Changed on physical stores once Amazon had invented one.
- 2007: "That's an expansion opportunity we've resisted. It fails all but one of the tests outlined above." (S054, 2007)
- 2019: "For many years, we considered how we might serve customers in physical stores, but felt we needed first to invent something that would really delight customers in that environment." (S066, 2019)

**23. Competition.** Customer obsession over competitor obsession, while conceding competitor focus can work, especially in mature industries.
- "I like the customer obsession model. I think it's the right one. I think it's better than product obsession." (S014, 2017)
- "We don't take a view on which of these approaches is more likely to maximize business success." (S060, 2013)

**24. Winner-take-all markets.** Avoid them; most markets have many winners.
- "I don't like winner-take-all industries because your odds of winning in a winner-take-all industry is low." (S013, 2018)

**25. Timing.** Rapid change favors startups; act when a trend grows extraordinarily fast or enabling technologies converge.
- "Stability favors incumbents and rapid change favors small, dynamic, nimble startup companies." (S023, 2025)

**26. Starting right out of school.** Consistently advises working first at a best-practices company.
- "I think that that extra 10 years of experience actually improved the odds that Amazon would succeed." (S023, 2025)

**27. Contrarian bets.** Contrarians are usually wrong, but the occasional win is huge.
- "Contrarians are usually wrong." (S030, 2016)
- "Outsized returns come from betting against conventional wisdom, but conventional wisdom is usually right." (S041, 2020)

**28. Acquisitions.** Prefers organic growth; will not buy what he cannot improve. Bought The Washington Post on trust with no diligence.
- "we wouldn't know how to improve that experience" (S009, 2000)
- "I did zero due diligence i did not negotiate." (S030, 2016)

**29. Selling the company and going public.** No intention to sell Amazon; going public is case by case; mercenaries flip, missionaries build.
- "We have absolutely no intention of doing anything but building this as an independent stand-alone company." (S008, 2001)
- "I think it's very case specific." (S010, 2016)

**30. Partners.** Choose partners on customer experience; every partnership must work for all three parties.
- "we simply won't build a partnership with any company that does not share our passion for serving customers." (S040, 2000)

## Customers, brand, and growth

**31. Content and indirect investments.** Judge them by their effect on the core membership, and keep checking.
- "Among other things, we watch Prime free trial starts, conversion to paid membership, renewal rates, and product purchase rates by members entering through this channel." (S062, 2015)

**32. Paid ads versus product.** Put most money into the product; word of mouth is amplified online. You cannot buy a brand in a quarter.
- "put the vast majority of my energy attention and dollars into building a great product or service and put a smaller amount into shouting about it marketing it" (S037, 2012)

**33. Brand.** Brand follows reality, and the best brands stand for an idea, not a category.
- "We hold as axiomatic that customers are perceptive and smart, and that brand image follows reality and not the other way around." (S039, 1999)

**34. Negative information about your product.** Show it; helping customers decide is the job.
- "Though negative reviews cost us some sales in the short term, helping customers make better purchase decisions ultimately pays off for the company." (S051, 2004)

**35. Metrics.** Manage controllable inputs; treat metrics as proxies and check them against anecdotes.
- "In the 452 goals, the terms net income, gross profit or margin, and operating profit are not used once." (S057, 2010)

**36. Customer data and privacy.** Be transparent and show the benefit. Evidence shifts: a 1999 report says he would not rule out selling data (narrator's words, not his); in 2001 he said Amazon does not sell it.
- "When you collect and store data you have to be clear about what you're doing." (S010, 2016)
- "you can't collect information under one policy and then somehow avoid that policy by transferring the information" (S027, 2001)

## Technology and the outside world

**37. AI.** A horizontal layer like electricity that will improve every application; the funding may be a bubble, but an industrial one.
- "I guarantee you there is not a single application that you can think of that is not going to be made better by AI." (S001, 2024)
- "The ones that are industrial are not nearly as bad." (S023, 2025)

**38. AI and jobs.** Expects a labor shortage, not mass unemployment.
- "I think in fact AI is going to create a labor shortage because it's going to make it possible for people to identify more problems." (S007, 2026)

**39. Hype cycles and busts.** Pick the right analogy: electricity, not a gold rush. Judge fundamentals, not the stock.
- "With innovation there isn't last nugget. Every new thing creates two new questions and two new opportunities." (S019, 2003)

**40. Regulation.** Light hand early; regulate harmful uses, not the tool; regulation tends to favor incumbents.
- Regulate the harmful use, not the tool: "You want to regulate murder." (S006, 2026)
- "One of the unintended consequences often of regulation is that it really favors the incumbents." (S047, 2018)
