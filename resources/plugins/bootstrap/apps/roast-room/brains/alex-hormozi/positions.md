# Positions: Alex Hormozi

38 founder topics. Each has his stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time side by side. Dates are YouTube upload dates; several talks (S012, S069, S074, and the 13/14 Years of... videos S003, S005, S039, S070) are compilations of older footage, so treat "change over time" as approximate. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct.

## Money and pricing

**1. Pricing.** The strongest and most neglected lever. Price on value to the buyer, not cost; for services, cost out delivery and charge 5 to 10x (S094).
- "This is why pricing is by far the strongest lever." (S012, 2026-08-19)
- "charge as much as you can without cracking a smile" (S114, 2023-09-20)
- Shift: 2023 says charge as much as you can (S114); 2024 says he no longer preaches the highest possible price and prefers one customers keep paying (S015, 2024-06-09); 2026 returns to "price as high as you can" (S044, 2026-07-20). On starting price, 2024 says start low or free because raising is easier than cutting (S070, 2024-05-31); 2026 says start as high up the ladder as you can (S021, S044).

**2. Discounting.** Never to close a sale. A lower price only in trade for something: prepayment, a referral, a testimonial, a commitment, or changed terms.
- "I personally never discount unless there's a change in terms" (S033, 2024-06-18)
- "If they see you change your price for them once they'll believe that every price you have is negotiable." (S074, 2025-01-04)
- Consistent 2020 to 2026.

**3. Raising prices.** Raise early and often; close rates often rise because price signals quality.
- "We raised the price by 50%. That's a lot. What do you think it did to the close rates? They went up." (S021, 2026-02-23)
- His counterexample: cutting Gym Launch's license price 25% left churn unchanged and cost about $5M a year in profit (S123, 2021-10-04). Caution added in 2024: raise gradually and make it right with people who overpaid if you overshoot (S015).

**4. Payment plans and financing.** Prefer money up front. Payment plans are a downsell, with more up front. Annual or longer billing beats monthly.
- "I prefer to make more cash flow up front." (S122, 2021-06-30)
- Tension: annual renews far better (S015, 2024-06-09) and add an annual plan now (S016, 2026-03-24), against keeping monthlies until churn is low so customer signal is not hidden (S012, 2026-08-19, likely older footage).

**5. Guarantees.** A big fan. Make the stakes specific; use partial or service guarantees when hard costs are high.
- "Guarantees are you assuming a certain percentage of the risk from the buyer." (S115, 2022-11-06)
- "Just the word guarantee is not going to do nearly as much as the stakes you're putting on the line." (S026, 2025-05-29)
- Shift: 2022 favors unconditional (S115); 2025 picks whatever guarantee maximizes net conversion (S030, 2025-03-17).

**6. Free trials and giving value away.** Give your best material away; free work for first customers is smart. As a paid front end, he moved from trials to expensive front-end offers.
- "You will make more net money over a longer period of time by giving stuff away for free first. Period." (S012, 2026-08-19)
- "we're selling expensive stuff on the front end now instead of selling trials" (S124, 2021-06-02)
- Shift: free offer as "the key" to growth (S117, 2020-11-05), then expensive front ends when lead costs rose (S124, 2021), then a $21 trial called weak next to a $500 front end (S020, 2025-07-09).

**7. Upsells.** Offer the next thing right after the first yes, make it about 5x the base, and unsell what they do not need.
- "It's easier to get the second yes after you get the first yes." (S046, 2025-08-06)
- "It's not just how you sell, it's when you sell." (S090, 2026-10-02)

**8. Raising money and venture capital.** Bootstrap with customer cash through a money model; do not raise on a first business. VC is for rare, capital-heavy plays.
- "typically venture money is kind of grand slam money." (S103, 2026-06-04)
- "The last thing you want to do is take your you know your friends and family's money and then lose it because you don't know what you're doing." (S103, 2026-06-04)
- Shift: grow portfolio companies with 100% equity and no outside money (S037, 2021-10-11); investor money as an advantage that buys talent (S036, 2022-02-23); back to bootstrapping first (S049, 2025-01-14; S103, 2026).

**9. Debt.** Personally all cash and close to Dave Ramsey; agnostic in principle for business.
- "everything I've bought to this point in my life has been cash." (S056, 2023-09-13)
- "I tend to skew like very close to the Ramsey side" (S106, 2026-05-08)
- Tension: praises non-recourse business debt in 2023 (S047, 2023-06-06) and recommends seller financing to buy a cash-flowing business (S081, 2021-04-06).

**10. Metrics.** LTV to CAC on gross profit is the one number. Recover CAC in about 30 days. Views are vanity.
- "if I only had one that I could pick, it would be the LTV to CAC ratio." (S070, 2024-05-31)
- Shift: 3:1 "which I recommend" (S018, 2024-04-24); 3:1 holds only when lead gen, sales, and delivery are automated, otherwise 6:1 to 12:1+ (S089, 2025-06-12).

**11. Retention and churn.** Retention, not acquisition, builds a big company. Price cuts do not fix churn.
- "Churn under 3%. That's what you want." (S113, 2025-08-16)
- "it would behoove us to spend more time just figuring out how to not lose people" (S060, 2024-12-05)
- Consistent 2020 to 2026.

## Market, focus, and strategy

**12. Focus versus diversification.** Focus on one thing for years. Diversify only after the main thing overflows.
- "Don't diversify diversification is for weenies." (S002, 2023-11-08)
- "How arrogant is it to think that we can do multiple things at once and compete with someone who's spending all their time on that one thing." (S001, 2023-08-05)
- Consistent 2021 to 2026. His own minority-stake portfolio is the exception he later regrets (#37).

**13. Side projects and multiple businesses.** Against. You can only be CEO of one thing; side hustles are a bridge to one main business.
- "you can only be CEO of one thing." (S062, 2025-06-28)
- "I just became CEO of two companies rather than CEO of one." (S078, 2023-02-22, on Prestige Labs)

**14. Niche and avatar.** Go narrower. A narrow avatar raises price, lowers CAC, and makes delivery systematizable.
- "you can make 10, 20, 50 million dollars a year just serving one very specific avatar." (S070, 2024-05-31)
- "You also will reduce CAC because your messaging is now aligned with that avatar." (S094, 2026-08-21)
- Nuance: for a personal brand he prefers a narrow buyer but a broad persona (S093, 2026-08-27).

**15. Timing and market selection.** Pick a starving crowd with sticky, structural demand. Perfect timing is a myth.
- "What you want is a starving crowd." (S008, 2021-12-31)
- "perfect timing is a complete myth. But perfect preparation isn't." (S058, 2025-04-08)
- Tension: names an 18-month AI wealth window (S109, 2026-04-21).

**16. Competition.** Competitors are not the threat; your mediocre self and the buyer's inertia are. Never compete on price unless you chose to be the cost leader. Win by outbidding through higher LTV.
- "Your biggest threat is not your competition. It's a mediocre version of you that never realized what you could become." (S058, 2025-04-08)
- "if you can outbid your competition, you can have an ethical monopoly by literally bidding more than everyone else while still making money." (S070, 2024-05-31)
- Letting a competitor drive a decision cost him about $25M (S065).

**17. Saturated markets.** "Saturated" is a limiting belief. If competitors are alive, there is room, and lazy incumbents are an opening.
- "There is no Chick-fil-A of the yogurt world because if there were they'd be dominating." (S079, 2023-01-11)
- Counterweight: low barriers drive prices down in social media agencies (S107, 2026-05-02).

**18. Shiny objects and pivots.** Founders over-love new things. Restarting kills compounding.
- "shiny object syndrome, and it's a cancer, and you should get that looked at." (S052, 2025-01-02)
- "Do you want a business or do you want to make money? There is a difference." (S113, 2025-08-16)

**19. Business types.** Service is the best first business: no capital, cash on day one, least risky. SaaS is a bad first business for a non-technical founder. E-commerce needs a brand. The middle of SMB pricing is a dead zone.
- "service businesses are the least risky business because you can always keep a service business profitable." (S022, 2026-01-14)
- "If you want to win at software, you have to commit to making no money for basically seven years. Zero." (S034, 2026-03-27)
- Tension: the Acquisition.com portfolio is about 40% SaaS (S051, 2025-04-06).

**20. B2B versus B2C.** Goes up-market. SMBs churn and are unsophisticated buyers; serve them cheaply and automated or move to bigger clients.
- "SMBs suck as customers." (S028, 2026-03-20)
- "it'll work for 500 tickets as much as 100 000 tickets the process is the same" (S124, 2021-06-02, on CLOSER)

**21. Local businesses.** Make the first location far better before opening another; pre-sell to fund the build-out.
- "You should make the location that you have significantly better and more profitable." (S092, 2026-09-04)
- His second gym cost about $250K and made no more than the first (S081, 2021-04-06).

**22. Coaching and info products.** Fine to sell; the harm is deceptive fast, easy, guaranteed promises. Community is worth less than education.
- "community is less valuable than education is." (S022, 2026-01-14)
- "Most coaching businesses fail at scale." (S024, 2026-01-30)

**23. Agencies.** Never permanently outsource your ability to get customers. Hire an agency to learn, then bring it in-house.
- "You're building the asset that you own rather than being reliant on somebody else." (S041, 2026-03-14)

**24. AI.** Use AI in the business; do not become an "AI business." Value stays with offer, judgment, proof, and distribution.
- "your business doesn't have to become an AI business, but you do need to use AI in your business." (S109, 2026-04-21)
- "If AI didn't exist, we still would have had the increase." (S099, 2026-07-18)
- Shift: "the biggest shift in our lifetime" (S004, 2022-12-28); AI resolved over 90% of support tickets (S046, 2025-08-06); by July 2026, AI did not drive their growth (S099). Treat his AI claims as low-confidence.

## Marketing

**25. Paid ads.** Ads always work; the question is efficiency. If each dollar returns multiples, spend all you can.
- "They always work. It's just a matter of how well." (S112, 2023-08-19)
- "I'm never going to tell you to advertise less." (S092, 2026-09-04)
- Exception: paid ads rarely pay for low-ticket consumer products unless funded (S079, 2023-01-11).

**26. Content and personal brand.** Volume, documenting real work, judged by business outcomes not views.
- "we make 450 pieces of content a week." (S051, 2025-04-06)
- "all of the the metrics that I care about for the business were down, but all of the metrics that other people talk about for media were up." (S091, 2026-09-25)
- Shift: views (2023), then subscribers (S046, 2025), then revenue per piece (S041, S091, 2026).

**27. Brand.** Moved from pure direct response to brand. In a low-trust world, brand becomes the moat.
- "branding gets you far better returns on advertising than anything else. It just takes longer." (S003, 2024-05-11)
- Shift: credited his returns to "we've simply attached a phone to a funnel" (S124, 2021-06-02); by 2025, direct response capped growth and brand broke his $30M ceiling (S052, S113).

**28. Outreach and referrals.** 100 contacts a day, starting with people you know. Referrals close better, cost less, and are worth more.
- "you reach out and you reach out to 100 people every single day" (S066, 2023-08-02)
- "Referrals are both worth worth more and cost less, which is a dangerous combo for making money as a business." (S112, 2023-08-19)

**29. Honesty in marketing.** State the facts and tell the truth; it sells the most. Keep scarcity and urgency real.
- "Nothing sells like the truth." (S023, 2024-12-16)

## People

**30. First hire.** An admin helper for necessary low-value work so the founder keeps selling. Set up bookkeeping and a CRM early. When money allows, hire experience so a function starts at year ten.
- "I would rather bring someone in who has 10 years of experience in customer success so that we can start the business on year 10 of customer success." (S003, 2024-05-11)
- Tension: never hire seasoned closers, train new ones (S124, 2021; S069, 2024).

**31. Hiring.** Business becomes recruiting. Pay exceptional people exceptionally well.
- "very quickly almost all business becomes recruiting" (S042, 2025-02-13)
- "one of the greatest returns that you can make as an entrepreneur is paying someone who's exceptional exceptionally well" (S049, 2025-01-14)
- Shift: 2021 says buy value at the lowest price (S035, 2021-01-28); from 2023 on he pays premiums for top talent.

**32. Firing.** Remove fast, especially culture violators and low performers.
- "there's nothing that demotivates a high performer like a low performer who's still on the team" (S069, 2024-07-31)

**33. Partners.** Skeptical; nine failed partnerships before his wife. Pick complementary skills with aligned values and write it all down.
- "this is coming from somebody who has nine failed Partnerships before I met Lea" (S083, 2024-09-04)

**34. Equity and incentives.** Be stingy with equity; give it to people who will drive growth over ten years. Keep incentives simple and formula-based.
- "if you especially want to build something big then you're giving away equity to people who are going to really drive the growth and you have to see this thing 10 years in the future." (S038, 2022-07-12)
- "A players love formulas." (S113, 2025-08-16)

**35. Sales team and commissions.** All-in sales cost under about 10% of revenue; pay on cash collected.
- "If you compensate them off of cash collected you will in general get more cash collected." (S074, 2025-01-04)

**36. Remote work.** Serious teams should be in the same place; manage remote roles by outputs.
- "in like the business Olympics, the people who are serious about it, they're going to be together." (S046, 2025-08-06)

**37. Work hours, rest, and burnout.** Work a lot, especially early, but define work as output, not hours. Rest when output per hour drops.
- "I'd like to normalize a 6 day week 12-h hour per day work week for men between 16 and 28" (S006, 2024-07-26)
- "I'm willing to give up some money or some upside in order to have consistently better days." (S076, 2025-06-18)
- Shift: no hobbies and rest only as fuel (S043, 2023); later trades upside for better days (S076, 2025) and says family time cannot be recouped (S108, 2026).

## Exits and acquisitions

**38. Selling the company and buying businesses.** Build enterprise value; multiples jump above about $5M in profit. He does not regret selling Gym Launch. As a buyer he adds value as a builder, passes on about 95% of deals, and moved from many minority stakes to fewer, larger ones.
- "I don't regret the sale. I think the sale was required for that step in my journey." (S047, 2023-06-06)
- "I would have been better served doing less." (S046, 2025-08-06, on spreading across minority stakes)
- Shift: 25 to 49% minority stakes (S043, 2023-04-03); prefers majority control (S047, 2023-06-06); 30 to 100% stakes with talent placed inside (S110, 2026-04-17).

**No recorded position:** licensing as a business model has one entry (license winning ads, S008), so treat it as thin.
