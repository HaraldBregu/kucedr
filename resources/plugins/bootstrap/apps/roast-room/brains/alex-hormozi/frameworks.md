# Frameworks: Alex Hormozi

24 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (offer, price, money model, leads, sales, focus). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym of it. Book tags show where the framework sits in his book series, as he teaches it in public; no book text is used. Quotes come from auto captions, so they keep caption quirks.

---

## Offer

### 1. Value Equation *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** Value = (dream outcome x perceived likelihood of achievement) / (time delay x effort and sacrifice). Raise the top, shrink the bottom.
- **Parts:** Dream outcome (what they actually want). Perceived likelihood (proof, reputation, guarantees). Time delay (speed to the result). Effort and sacrifice (done for them, remove every step that sucks). He says the fortunes are on the bottom half: rich buyers pay for fast, easy, and certain (S021, S115).
- **When to use:** Designing or repricing any offer, explaining why a similar product sells for far more, and before anyone suggests cutting price.
- **How to score:** Rate each option 1 to 10 on all four variables against the status quo. An option that only changes price and moves none of the four scores low. If value is not clearly above price, fix the offer before spending on marketing.
- **Quote:** "Remember this is the top side that creates value the bottom side of this fraction are the things that destroy value our goal is to increase the top side and decrease the bottom side." (S115)
- **Sources:** 20 (S002, S003, S012, S017, S021, S039, S043, S044, S050, S051, S054, S060, S065, S066, S070, S101, S114, S115, S118, S120)

### 2. Grand Slam Offer *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** An offer so good people feel stupid saying no, and one a buyer cannot compare apples to apples on price.
- **Parts:** (1) Start from the dream outcome. (2) Break the customer's journey into every step and list every problem they could hit. (3) Write a solution for each, ignoring constraints. (4) Pick delivery vehicles (one-to-one, group, one-to-many; do it yourself, done with you, done for you). (5) Trim and stack: keep high value, low cost pieces, cut the rest. (6) Add enhancers (#4). Bad-offer symptoms: leads are hard to get, most people do not want it, and buyers compare your price to others (S114).
- **When to use:** Weak sales, stuck pricing, or a bloated offer. Rework the offer before changing anything else.
- **How to score:** Run the three bad-offer symptoms. Any symptom present means the offer, not the marketing, is the problem.
- **Quote:** "I just have to make things that are so good that people will feel stupid saying no and if we can't get enough people to say yes we need to make the offer better." (S043)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S008, S020, S024, S043, S061, S090, S114, S115, S117)

### 3. Starving crowd market screen *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** A pass or fail test for a market. Market beats offer beats persuasion.
- **Parts:** Massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing. All four must be true. Later he adds five business traits as continua: sticky (revenue retention), expensive (gross margin), expanding, low complexity and capex, unique (S067, S107).
- **When to use:** Choosing a niche or avatar, or when current customers pay little.
- **How to score:** Four yes or no gates; one "no" kills the option. Score the survivors on the five traits.
- **Quote:** "You want to make sure that you have all four of these things you can't just have three of them or two of them or one of them you need to have all four." (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S008, S067, S107, S114, S121)

### 4. Offer enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, naming *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** Levers layered on a valuable core offer to lift conversion and price.
- **Parts:** Scarcity limits quantity and urgency limits time, and both must be real. Controlled burn: sell about 20% of known demand (S027). Bonuses beat discounts: $400 of service may cost you $40 to $80, versus $200 off (S033). Guarantees (#5). Naming states the dream outcome; test names with small ad tests or polls (S116, S118). The MAGIC naming acronym from the book does not appear in the corpus.
- **When to use:** After the core value is right, to get buyers to act now at a higher price.
- **How to score:** Count the enhancers that are real. An offer with no real scarcity or urgency and a feature-based name loses to the same offer with them.
- **Quote:** "You can enhance it by adding scarcity you can enhance it by adding urgency you can enhance it by adding bonuses or you can enhance it by adding guarantees." (S115)
- **Sources:** 15 (S001, S011, S027, S032, S033, S043, S112, S113, S114, S115, S116, S118, S119, S120, S122)

### 5. Guarantees *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** A stated consequence that moves risk from the buyer to you. The goal is more net sales, not the wildest promise.
- **Parts:** Formula: if you don't X in Y time, we will Z (money back, partial refund, your gross profit, or more service). Types: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, implied (revenue share, pay per show). Build conditions from milestones your best customers actually hit (S115). When hard costs block a refund, keep working until they hit it (S026).
- **When to use:** Buyers fear they won't get the result.
- **How to score:** Estimate net sales after refunds. If a 25% refund converts like 100%, offer 25% (S030).
- **Quote:** "The point of a guarantee is not to have the craziest guarantee the point of a guarantee is to maximize net conversion." (S030)
- **Sources:** 18 (S001, S026, S027, S030, S045, S046, S051, S061, S069, S072, S073, S074, S094, S108, S114, S115, S117, S118)

## Price

### 6. Price raising rules (close-rate headroom, conversion x price) *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** Rules for whether and how far to change price.
- **Parts:** Close-rate headroom (S021): 60 to 80% close means 2 to 3x headroom; 50 to 60% means 1.5 to 2x; 40 to 50% means 1.25 to 1.5x; 30 to 40% is about right; under 30% means fix selling, the offer, or lead quality, never cut price. Stop rule: keep raising until close rate x price falls (S015, S092). Never discount the same package; change terms or components (S046, S051). Raise mechanics: announce the date and close the pipeline at the old price (S122).
- **When to use:** Any time price is on the table.
- **How to score:** Compute close rate x price per sales conversation at the old and new price; the higher number wins. A discount with no change in terms scores as a loss.
- **Quote:** "I encourage everyone to continue to raise prices until your conversion rate times price equal less money" (S015)
- **Sources:** 13 (S003, S012, S015, S016, S021, S046, S051, S074, S090, S092, S106, S122, S123)

### 7. Virtuous cycle of price *($100M Offers)*
- **What it is:** A higher price raises the buyer's commitment and results, and the margin buys better delivery and talent, which supports a higher price again.
- **Parts:** Charge more, better customer, more investment, better results, more margin, better talent, better reputation, higher price. Strongest under about $3 to $5M in revenue (S106). Under a supply constraint, raise 20 to 40% backed by a guarantee and hire with the cash (S108).
- **When to use:** Competing on price, high close rates, or demand above capacity.
- **How to score:** Does the higher price buy better delivery or talent, and does demand exceed capacity? Two yeses favor the raise. A price cut scores against the cycle unless you chose to be the volume player.
- **Quote:** "The more you charge, the better the customer you attract, the more you can over-deliver for them, the more profit you make, the better the talent you can bring on, the more you can over-deliver." (S092)
- **Sources:** 5 (S021, S092, S106, S108, S114)

### 8. Price anchors and the 5 to 10x tier ladder
- **What it is:** Profit follows a power law, so the menu needs much higher tiers. Build from the top down.
- **Parts:** Each tier is 5 to 10x the one below and about 20% take it (S021). An anchor at 10 to 100x the core lifts core conversion (S005). Customers are fractal 80/20: 20% bring 80% of profit, 4% bring 64% (S021). Premium or volume, pick one (S003); the middle is where people die (S050).
- **When to use:** Designing a menu, an upsell, or a new business.
- **How to score:** Can the top 1 to 20% pay 5 to 10x more? If the top price is under 5x the core, the option leaves the Pareto profit unclaimed.
- **Quote:** "My rule of thumb is that for every new tier is that you want to 5 to 10x your price and expect 20% of people to take it." (S021)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S005, S021, S044, S050, S101, S110, S113)

## Money model and unit economics

### 9. Money Model *($100M Money Models)*
- **What it is:** A deliberate sequence of offers that gets customers to spend more than twice what they cost you within 30 days.
- **Parts:** Attraction offers (get the first purchase), upsells (raise LTV), downsells (raise conversion), continuity (retention and recurring cash). Diagnose first: demand problem, attraction; LTV problem, upsell; conversion problem, downsell; retention problem, continuity (S046). The model beats the method: whoever makes the most per customer can outbid everyone for attention (S089).
- **When to use:** Cash caps growth, or ad costs rise.
- **How to score:** Name the slot the change fills and the problem it fixes, then check whether 30-day gross profit moves toward 2x (CAC + COGS).
- **Quote:** "A $100 million money model is a sequence of offers that make customers spend twice as much on you as you do on them in 30 days or less." (S113)
- **Sources:** 7 (S012, S020, S046, S061, S075, S090, S113)

### 10. Client-financed acquisition (the 30-day cash rule) *($100M Money Models)*
- **What it is:** The first 30 days of cash from a customer pays to acquire and serve them, and the next customer too.
- **Parts:** 30-day cash > 2 x (CAC + COGS). Worked example: $100 CAC and $50 COGS means collect $300 in 30 days (S060). Levers: money up front, setup fees, an immediate upsell, third-party financing (S014).
- **When to use:** Bootstrapped growth, offer structure, payment terms, and any question about raising money to grow.
- **How to score:** 30-day gross profit / (CAC + COGS). Under 1, you fund growth. 1 to 2, self-funding. Above 2, each customer funds the next.
- **Quote:** "30-day cash must be greater than two times the cost of acquiring customer and the cost of fulfilling that customer." (S037)
- **Sources:** 10 (S014, S020, S037, S045, S046, S060, S070, S078, S089, S113)

### 11. Attraction, upsell, downsell, and continuity tactics *($100M Money Models)*
- **What it is:** The named offer mechanisms that fill each slot of the money model.
- **Parts:** Attraction: win your money back, giveaways, decoy (he calls it a dummy offer), buy X get Y free, pay less now or pay more later (S113). Upsell: classic ("you can't have X without Y"), menu (unsell, prescribe, A or B, easy pay), anchor, rollover (credit the first payment toward the next) (S046, S113). Downsell: payment plan, trial, feature downsell; the first downsell re-sells the main offer (S046). Continuity: continuity bonus, continuity discount, waived fee for a commitment (S046, S113). Timing: sell at greatest deprivation, not greatest satisfaction (S030).
- **When to use:** Once the money model diagnosis names the weak slot.
- **How to score:** The tactic should land at a deprivation point, use the card on file, and solve a problem the first purchase created. Every downsell concession must buy something back (term, testimonial, referral, removed component).
- **Quote:** "you unsell what they don't need, prescribe what they do need, ask if they prefer A or B, then make it easy to pay" (S113)
- **Sources:** 20+ across the four slots (S011, S013, S015, S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S026, S033, S039, S046, S061, S069, S073, S086, S089, S097, S098, S100, S113, S117)

### 12. LTV to CAC (lifetime gross profit to CAC)
- **What it is:** His master number. Lifetime gross profit per customer divided by fully loaded cost to acquire one.
- **Parts:** LTV means gross profit, not revenue. CAC includes ads, sales and marketing payroll, commissions, and software (S013, S089). Floor 3:1, 5:1 great (S014, S070). Automation-adjusted floors: 3:1 if lead gen, sales, and delivery are all automated; about 6:1 with two; 9:1 with one; over 12:1 with none (S089). Spend at most a third of LTGP to acquire (S070).
- **When to use:** Before scaling spend, choosing which offer to push, or buying a business.
- **How to score:** Compute the ratio for each option against its floor. Below the floor, don't scale. Between options, the higher ratio and faster payback win.
- **Quote:** "the rule of thumb is that you want to have at least three to one on LTV to CAC." (S070)
- **Sources:** 12 (S013, S014, S018, S019, S023, S025, S051, S060, S070, S089, S113, S121)

### 13. LTV levers: more customers or worth more
- **What it is:** Growth is either more customers or each customer worth more, and there is a fixed list for the second.
- **Parts:** Raise price, cut cost of goods, buy more times, upsell, cross-sell, downsell (S019, S123); add financing and payment terms (S089). Three strategic buckets: more customers, more lifetime gross profit, less risk (S042).
- **When to use:** The ratio is weak and the team wants to chase cheaper leads.
- **How to score:** Run every product through every lever and pick the one with the least operational drag.
- **Quote:** "You decrease the cost of goods you get them to buy more of them or you increase the price those are the ways that you increase LTV." (S013)
- **Sources:** 10 (S005, S013, S019, S025, S042, S043, S067, S084, S089, S123)

### 14. Churn math and the leaky bucket
- **What it is:** Retention sets the revenue ceiling. Fix the bucket before pouring in water.
- **Parts:** LTV = price / churn (S018, S040). Ceiling = monthly inflow / monthly churn (S025). Golden ratio: referral % / churn % above 1 means growth with no ceiling (S025). Benchmarks: 80% monthly retention average, 90%+ best, under 70% weak (S016). Churn cliffs: month 1 is 20%+, about 10% after day 90, about 2% after month 6 (S016, S107). Retention levers in order: onboarding, unlockables, exit interviews, attendance tracking, reach outs, events, handwritten cards (S018, S040).
- **When to use:** Before any big acquisition push in a recurring business.
- **How to score:** Compute the ceiling. If revenue is near it, retention options outscore acquisition options.
- **Quote:** "I'd rather fix the bucket before we pour a bunch of water. Pouring the water is almost a commoditized thing." (S113)
- **Sources:** 14 (S013, S014, S015, S016, S017, S018, S022, S025, S028, S040, S042, S044, S107, S113)

## Leads

### 15. Core Four *($100M Leads)*
- **What it is:** The only four ways one person can let others know about their stuff: a 2x2 of one-to-one or one-to-many against people who know you or strangers.
- **Parts:** Warm outreach, cold outreach, content, paid ads. Pick one, go all in, and spend the first 4 hours of every day on it while under $1M (S052) or about $3M (S070).
- **When to use:** Any question about getting leads.
- **How to score:** Is the proposal one of the four, done at volume? If not, it is not advertising. Prefer the channel that overlaps the founder's existing skills (S108).
- **Quote:** "if you're not spending your day doing one of those core four things, you are not advertising." (S070)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S005, S042, S052, S067, S070, S075, S112, S121)

### 16. Lead getters *($100M Leads)*
- **What it is:** People who run the Core Four for you: customers (referrals), employees, agencies, affiliates.
- **Parts:** Make the affiliate deal so good it hurts, for example let partners sell your lead magnet and keep 100% (S023, S030). Referral plays: ask at the peak of the result, trade discounts for names, cap the incentive near 1x CAC (S033). Go up a level of distribution (agencies, brokerages) (S078, S101).
- **When to use:** Once you can generate leads yourself and want leverage.
- **How to score:** Does the move add nodes that produce leads, or just leads? Nodes win. S112: 10 customers a month is $1.2M in 12 months; 10 affiliates a month is $7.8M.
- **Quote:** "You've got customers, employees, agencies, and affiliates, and lead getters using the core 4 complete the advertising cycle." (S112)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S023, S030, S033, S067, S078, S097, S101, S106, S112, S120)

### 17. Lead magnets *($100M Leads)*
- **What it is:** A complete solution to a narrow problem, given away free or cheap before the paid offer, that reveals the next problem your core offer solves.
- **Parts:** Three types: reveal a problem, a trial, or one step of many. Four formats: software or tools, information, services, physical. Make it genuinely good and easy to consume, with a clear next step (S116, S119). Make your free stuff better than their paid stuff (S070).
- **When to use:** Cold traffic, low opt-ins, or leads that cost too much.
- **How to score:** Effective CAC = magnet delivery cost / ascension rate, compared to lifetime gross profit. The magnet's "next problem" must be your core offer.
- **Quote:** "A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem. It's the free thing you give away before your paid thing so you can get more people to buy." (S112)
- **Sources:** 10 (S023, S030, S031, S070, S091, S106, S112, S116, S117, S119)

### 18. Rule of 100 *($100M Leads)*
- **What it is:** Set goals on inputs, not outcomes. 100 primary actions a day for 100 days.
- **Parts:** 100 warm or cold reach-outs, or 100 minutes making content, or $100 a day in ads (S002, S053). Judge the day by the inputs. A channel that "didn't work" at 300 flyers was never tested; his mentor tested 5,000 (S044).
- **When to use:** Starting out, or when goals keep getting missed and the team wants to switch channels.
- **How to score:** Was the current channel run at 100 a day for 100 days? If not, "it doesn't work" is unproven and switching scores low.
- **Quote:** "If you do 100 primary actions over 100 days you'll usually get the first result that you ultimately want." (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 by name (S002, S005, S052, S053, S054, S066); the volume rule behind it in 13 more

### 19. More, Better, New *($100M Leads)*
- **What it is:** The order for scaling any channel or function.
- **Parts:** More first, because it has the highest risk-adjusted return. Better when improving beats one more unit of more. New last. Any change costs about a 20% dip, so the upside must beat 20% (S075). 70/20/10 split on creative and effort: 70% the proven thing, 20% adjacent, 10% wild (S031, S052).
- **When to use:** Any debate about launching a new product, channel, or offer.
- **How to score:** A "new" proposal must show the current thing is saturated and an expected upside above 20%. Otherwise it scores below doing more.
- **Quote:** "the cost of change of doing something new is guaranteed, but the return on something new is not." (S070)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S042, S049, S052, S053, S070, S075)

### 20. Give, give, give, ask (content ratio)
- **What it is:** Most impressions should give; only a minority should ask. Judge content by revenue, not views.
- **Parts:** 70%+ give, 30% or less ask (S052). Hook, retain, reward on every piece (S112). Proof, promise, plan in the first seconds (S041, S074). Make content for the highest-value avatar; his view-chasing experiments raised views and cut revenue (S041, S049, S091).
- **When to use:** Content strategy, or brand versus direct-response budget fights.
- **How to score:** Share of ask impressions above about 30% means expect demand to dry up. Revenue per piece (UTMs) over views.
- **Quote:** "Toss out the views, focus on your customer, make stuff for them because likes ain't cash, views ain't cash, cash is cash." (S041)
- **Sources:** 9 (S022, S041, S042, S052, S065, S091, S096, S104, S112)

## Sales

### 21. CLOSER
- **What it is:** A question-led sales call that works B2C and B2B from about $500 to $100K tickets.
- **Parts:** Clarify why they're here. Label the problem. Overview past pain (what have you tried, how long, how did it work). Sell the vacation, not the plane flight, in under 3 minutes. Explain away concerns. Reinforce the decision, which runs on into onboarding (S017, S074). Only questions, restatements, and short stories come out of the rep's mouth (S069, S124).
- **When to use:** Building or auditing any sales script.
- **How to score:** Question-led? Pitch under 3 minutes? Does the R extend into onboarding? Feature-heavy pitches score low.
- **Quote:** "Clarify why they're here when they come in. Label them with the problem to show that you listen." (S026)
- **Sources:** 10 (S005, S010, S017, S026, S045, S067, S069, S074, S087, S124)

### 22. Obstacles before objections
- **What it is:** Obstacles come up before price and objections after it. Surface and handle them early.
- **Parts:** Three buckets, peeled like an onion: circumstances (time, money, fit), other people (spouse, partner, decision maker), myself (self-doubt). Each objection is really about value, priority, or avoidance: time is prioritization, money is value, the partner is support not permission, stalls are decision avoidance (S074).
- **When to use:** Close rate problems, or designing pre-call nurture.
- **How to score:** Tag lost deals by bucket. If most are circumstance objections raised after price, the fix is earlier qualification, not harder closing.
- **Quote:** "You've got obstacles and you've got objections an obstacle occurs before you've mentioned the price" (S009)
- **Sources:** 10 (S009, S045, S068, S069, S072, S074, S086, S087, S111, S124)

### 23. Sales throughput outside the call (Six C's and the multipliers)
- **What it is:** Most sales results are set before and after the call, not in the script.
- **Parts:** Availability and speed: respond to leads in under a minute, sell 7 days a week (+29% selling days), same or next day appointments, best leads to the best closers (S074). Show rate around 70% is the benchmark (S069). Six C's of a sales team: closer sequence, consistent daily training and conviction, call recordings, communication and feedback, cut the fat fast, competition and career path (S069, S087). Conviction corrects tone (S087). Comp: total sales cost under about 10% of revenue, paid on cash collected (S069, S074).
- **When to use:** Scaling past founder-led sales, or before buying sales training.
- **How to score:** Fix out-of-call levers and show rate first. A 20% lift in show rate is worth the same as a 20% lift in close rate.
- **Quote:** "these are all the things that happen outside of the sale that affect your sales more than anything that happens inside the sale" (S074)
- **Sources:** 9 (S009, S012, S069, S074, S087, S092, S113, S124, S001)

## Focus and constraints

### 24. Constraint first, and one product, one avatar, one channel
- **What it is:** A business grows until one constraint stops it. Find it, relieve it, repeat, and add complexity one variable at a time.
- **Parts:** Four constraints: leads, sales, delivery, profit (S113). Supply or demand first: if lead flow doubled, could we handle it? (S028, S106). About 70% of $1M to $50M businesses are demand constrained (S044). Fix order from the back: offer, sales process, avatar, traffic (S098, S101). The 111 rule: one product, one avatar, one channel until $1M (S059). $1M to $10M, raise lifetime gross profit; $10M to $50M, professionalize; $50M+, talent and new P&Ls (S008).
- **When to use:** Any prioritization or next-move decision, and any proposal to add a product, avatar, or channel.
- **How to score:** An option that does not hit the named constraint scores near zero. Under $1M, every added variable is a strike against it.
- **Quote:** "I have a a a 111 rule, which is you want to sell one product or service to one avatar on one channel until you make $1 million." (S059)
- **Sources:** 20 (S002, S003, S008, S022, S024, S028, S044, S049, S052, S058, S059, S070, S082, S084, S090, S094, S098, S101, S106, S108, S113)

## Numbers he reaches for

- LTGP:CAC floor 3:1; 5:1 great; over 12:1 when people staff every step (S014, S070, S089).
- 30-day cash > 2 x (CAC + COGS) (S037, S060).
- Price at 5 to 10x the true delivery cost for services (S094).
- Close rate above 80% means raise prices, possibly 3 to 4x; under 30% means fix selling (S021).
- Each new tier 5 to 10x the one below, about 20% take it (S021).
- Annual plan at about 16 to 17% off ("buy 10, get 2"), 20 to 30% take it (S016).
- Discounts need to be 50% or more to change behavior (S023).
- Monthly retention under 70% means retention is the priority (S016); churn target under 3% (S113).
- Show rate benchmark about 70% for appointment businesses (S069).
- Lead response under 1 minute; speed to lead can triple sales (S074, S092).
- Changing a working system costs about a 20% dip; only change for more than 20% upside (S075).
- Rule of 100: 100 actions a day for 100 days (S002).
- First 4 hours of every day on the Core Four under $1M to $3M (S052, S070).
- A players cost 25 to 50% more and are worth three to five B players (S012).
