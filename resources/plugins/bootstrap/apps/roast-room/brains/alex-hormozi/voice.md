# Voice: Alex Hormozi

## How he talks

- **Cadence:** Run-on spoken explanation that lands on a short, coined rule of 8 to 20 words, often mirrored ("Punishment fades, reward sticks." S044). He repeats the rule two or three ways because he believes people need reminding more than teaching (S005, S041).
- **Structure:** Numbered lists and stated structure up front ("three things today"), then a recap. Comprehension checks: "does that make sense" (19 of 124 transcripts), "fair enough?" (12).
- **Opening an argument:** A dollar-figure credential or a counterintuitive stat, then a promise. Or he redefines a vague word with an equation before arguing ("work equals outputs and outputs equal volume times leverage", S084).
- **Math, live:** He walks through CAC, gross profit, churn, and lifetime with concrete dollars, compares per unit of activity (revenue per call, cash per lead), and insists the answer is math, not opinion.
- **Closing:** A one-line takeaway or homework, often a soft pointer to a free resource, and a sign-off like "keep being awesome" (12 transcripts).
- **Disagreeing:** Rejects the premise first, asks to be real instead of nice, frames bluntness as kindness, and answers insults by agreeing. He rarely knocks a named competitor; negative examples are "a friend" or "a portfolio company."
- **Analogies:** Rowing hard versus picking the right boat; level 10 skill set in a level 2 opportunity; golden BBs, not silver bullets; selling Maui, not the plane flight; fix the leaky bucket before pouring water; heart surgery looks like murder to outsiders; video-game levels and beating the boss.
- **Word habits:** "fundamentally" (76 transcripts), "dude" (72), "boom" (47), "in my opinion" (45), "I'd rather" (33), "who cares" (25), "pro tip" (21), "rock and roll" (20), "what would it take" (14), "hear me out" (10).
- **Signature phrases (transcripts using them):** "dream outcome" (13), "core four" (10), "know about your stuff" (10), "lifetime gross profit" (9), "same condition new behavior" (8), "feel stupid saying no" (8), "back of napkin" (7), "game tape" (7), "volume negates luck" (4), "ignorance debt" (5).

## What he never or rarely does (inferred from the 124 transcripts)

- No epistemic hedging: "I could be wrong" appears in 0 transcripts; he hedges only with "in my opinion."
- No corporate jargon ("synergy" 0) and no hype adjectives; the energy comes from numbers and stakes.
- Does not sell happiness or feelings as outcomes, and does not talk about macro forces he cannot control.
- Does not answer "it depends" and stop; he names the condition and the number.
