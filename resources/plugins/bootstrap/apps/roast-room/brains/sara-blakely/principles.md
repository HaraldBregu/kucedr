# Principles: Sara Blakely

Built from 33 public sources (keynotes, long interviews, podcasts, and a commencement address), merged from five batches of verified notes. 29 decision rules, most-used first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Lines she retells from other people (her father, Warren Buffett, a shoe designer, buyers) are not quoted as hers.

1. **Not knowing how it is done is an edge, so act on it instead of waiting for credentials.** "what you don't know can be your greatest asset if you let it, if you have the courage" (S008) "I haven't been the expert in anything that I've done but I've stood in the confidence" (S022) Sources: 18 (S001, S002, S004, S007, S008, S009, S016, S017, S018, S020, S021, S022, S023, S024, S025, S030, S031, S034). Confidence: high.

2. **Fund the business from its own sales; keep the ownership.** "I started it with $5,000 that I had from selling fax machines and I just self-funded it the whole time" (S006) "I only ever bought or spent what I had orders for already or what I knew I could sell and so the sales funded it" (S007) Sources: 17 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S009, S011, S016, S020, S022, S023, S024, S025, S027, S033, S034). Confidence: high.

3. **Look for categories that have not evolved, and ask the makers whether they use what they make.** "I'm fascinated by things that haven't changed in a really long time." (S009) "the people who are making it are not wearing it and that's why maybe it's not as comfortable as it could be" (S018) Sources: 13 (S001, S002, S003, S009, S016, S017, S019, S020, S021, S022, S023, S024, S030). Confidence: high.

4. **Hire your weaknesses as soon as you can afford it, and spend your own day on your superpowers.** "as soon as you can afford to hire your weaknesses do it" (S017) "at the end of each day, a good litmus test is did I spend the majority of my dayto-day doing what I'm best at?" (S009) Sources: 12 (S001, S007, S009, S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S024, S026, S027, S030). Confidence: high.

5. **When the product is best proven live, go in person and show it.** "I knew from cold calling that my best shot was in person" (S001) "If you give me 10 minutes of your time, I will fly there and show you." (S019) Sources: 12 (S001, S002, S006, S007, S017, S018, S019, S021, S022, S023, S024, S032). Confidence: high.

6. **Start with a problem you personally have; you are the first customer.** "everything starts with a problem and it's usually a problem that I'm experiencing" (S022) "sneaks came from my own personal need it always does I don't have big focus groups" (S020) Sources: 12 (S003, S004, S008, S009, S016, S018, S019, S020, S022, S027, S030, S033). Confidence: high.

7. **Let customers do the marketing: make the product worth talking about and skip paid ads until it has proven itself.** "Spanx didn't advertise for 16 years I spent no money on advertising basically for 16 years of this business and we became a household name" (S011) "because the name Spanx was fun and the product was so revolutionary for women and how it made them feel um they just wanted to talk about it" (S006) Sources: 11 (S004, S006, S007, S011, S012, S016, S018, S021, S023, S024, S002). Confidence: high. *Changed over time:* no ads was first a budget choice, then a kept choice; the first formal ad came around 2016 (S016, S023). Newer view (2020): "I made the choice for budget you know initially and then when I saw how effective it was and how authentic it felt yes then I didn't change it even when I had the budget to do it" (S007)

8. **Test on real people over real time, and do not launch what does not fit.** "if I don't like it and the team doesn't like it it doesn't fit right we don't sell it we don't launch it" (S011) "Ask her how she feels." (S019) Sources: 8 (S007, S009, S011, S017, S019, S023, S024, S034). Confidence: high.

9. **Be the face and the demo yourself; vulnerability sells better than polish.** "I had no budget to advertise so I was the advertisement" (S004) "a lot of people think of vulnerability is weakness and I don't I think it's a big strength" (S007) Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S007, S012, S021, S022, S031). Confidence: high.

10. **Take input, then decide from your gut; intuition wins when it conflicts with data.** "I get a lot of input and then I go off and I listen to my inner voice" (S018) "I always choose in my whole journey I chose intuition over data" (S022) Sources: 7 (S009, S016, S018, S020, S022, S024, S002). Confidence: high. She also says "The data is really important" (S009), and admits she cannot name a time her gut was wrong (S016); the council should treat that as a blind spot.

11. **Do not let your success depend on other people selling it; control every step you can.** "I didn't want my success to be contingent on anyone else having to sell this" (S017) "every step of the way that you guys can control the outcome of your success, take it" (S009) Sources: 6 (S004, S007, S009, S011, S016, S017). Confidence: high.

12. **Marketing never rescues a product that is not clearly better; product is king.** "marketing never makes up for a product that isn't superior" (S007) "you can have the best marketing in the world people will buy your product once if it's not great" (S007) Sources: 6 (S007, S011, S014, S020, S021, S023). Confidence: high.

13. **Sell the problem and the why, not the features.** "sell the problem you're solving not the product people are far more emotional about that" (S021) "The why makes people want to, you know, become your champion and not just your customer." (S020) Sources: 6 (S004, S005, S006, S011, S020, S021). Confidence: high.

14. **Give every person in the chain their what's-in-it-for-them, fast.** "they're looking for the what's in it for them and I learned I need to deliver that in my message very quickly or I lose them" (S007) Sources: 6 (S004, S007, S009, S011, S012, S031). Confidence: high.

15. **Talk to customers as one of them, with humor; you do not need to act serious to be taken seriously.** "i just want to talk to the consumer the way i would talk to my friends at dinner" (S021) "i used humor as a secret weapon up against the billion dollar companies for sure" (S021) Sources: 6 (S012, S016, S018, S021, S027, S005). Confidence: high.

16. **Only build what is exponentially better, and cut your losses when it is not.** "I really believe if you're going to start a company or start go into a new category, your product should be 10x better than what's already out there." (S022) "after four years of development i didn't it was more comfortable but it wasn't exponentially more comfortable so i said to the team we're going to cut our losses" (S009) Sources: 5 (S009, S020, S022, S027, S004). Confidence: high.

17. **Always know why you are different; if you cannot say it, the customer cannot either.** "you should always be asking yourself why am i different why am i different why is my product different why is my product better" (S007) "Differentiate yourself. Always pay attention to why am I different? Why am I the best option?" (S018) Sources: 5 (S007, S017, S018, S020, S031). Confidence: high.

18. **Watch the consumer, not the competition; look at rivals only to check whether an invention already exists.** "I never even paid any attention to my competition. I just paid attention to my consumer." (S022) "i find that when you start to really pay attention to the competition that's when things are probably not they're probably going to start heading south for you" (S021) Sources: 5 (S009, S021, S022, S023, S033). Confidence: high.

19. **Start small rather than raise big to look big; most founders give up more than they need to.** "so many founders give up more than they need to early on" (S020) "you dilute yourself down a lot and you know then you have a lot of other people you're answering to" (S016) Sources: 5 (S011, S016, S020, S033, S008). Confidence: high. *Nuance, newer view (2025):* raising is fine when the business truly needs it: "if you need to give up a lot of your company to make what you're going to do work by all means do it" (S020). Earlier (about 2016 to 2017) she framed her path as not understanding venture capital rather than rejecting it: "I didn't really understand the whole VC thing" (S001)

20. **Keep the day job and its benefits until the first big order is in.** "I worked on it at night and on the weekends while I kept my day job for those two years" (S011) "because I didn't want to quit my job and have no income and no healthare" (S027) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S011, S027). Confidence: high.

21. **Bring in experts later than feels natural; scrappy work often beats expensive polish.** "it's very seductive to start to say we can afford the experts" (S007) "I found that when you have less money to do something, the creative output is always almost better" (S020) Sources: 4 (S007, S020, S023, S002). Confidence: high.

22. **Share an early idea only with people who can move it forward, and protect it before going public.** "But I did share it with manufacturers or patent lawyers, or people who could help me move it along" (S008) Sources: 4 (S001, S008, S009, S017). Confidence: high.

23. **Treat a well-funded knockoff as free category marketing if your product is better.** "their product was super inferior so people tried it and then came back to spanx" (S023) "i had big companies knock me off very early on and they spent eight to twenty million dollars on ad budgets that gave awareness for the product that i had just created" (S021) Sources: 3 (S014, S021, S023). Confidence: high.

24. **Carry no debt, and treat cash as king.** "Like I've never had any debt" (S033) "cash is king right now and cash is king when you're a start-up" (S011) Sources: 3 (S011, S015, S033). Confidence: high.

25. **Become known for one thing before expanding, but do not overstay it.** "I like to be known for something and be the best at something before I start really expanding my line" (S007) Sources: 3 (S007, S011, S018). Confidence: high. *Changed over time:* in 2020 she said "I've stayed in one lane" (S011); in a talk recorded in 2023 (uploaded 2025) she said Spanx "probably should have pivoted sooner and just start to make more stuff" (S018). Newer view: expand sooner than she did.

26. **There is no wrong time to start if the product is really better.** "if you have a good product if you're solving a problem if you are making a product or a service better than anything else out there there's it is always a good time to start a business" (S011) Sources: 3 (S011, S015, S033). Confidence: high.

27. **Never discount the brand.** "Spanx doesn't ever um discount" (S017) "I'm not going to discount my price." (S022) Sources: 2 (S017, S022). Confidence: medium.

28. **Launch when the team believes in it, and pause when your gut says no, even with press booked.** "Do you guys believe in this product?" (S003) "whenever you're doing anything really truly disruptive, that comes with it. To me, that's a good sign" (S004) Sources: 2 (S003, S004). Confidence: medium.

29. **Fire fast, including the toxic genius, and get help if firing is hard for you.** "the most important thing would be to fire fast" (S009) "i would say that i hired someone to help me do that" (S009) Sources: 1 (S009). Confidence: low. She tells it as her takeaway after Warren Buffett told her hiring is half luck; that line is his, not hers.
